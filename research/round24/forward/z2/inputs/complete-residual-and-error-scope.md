# Complete residuals and useful approximation questions

An explicitly assembled physical Ritz matrix becomes a full-model certificate only after its entire operator residual is accounted for. Keep every product channel and shared-link representation branch. One nonzero omitted matrix element proves leakage; it does not bound the omitted norm from above.

Before converting a residual into an energy or projection error, establish the actual full-space spectral separation and operator domain. A finite-matrix gap is not a substitute. If the candidate vector has mean energy mu, the actual excited spectrum is at least b>mu, and its complete residual norm is rho, the spectral measure gives mu-epsilon <=rho^2/(b-mu) and ground projection distance <=rho/(b-mu), provided the isolated simple ground and finite spectral second moment have been established. Derive the hypotheses in the named model. A residual-enriched variational space needs its own complete residual; it does not close the dynamics by construction.

Classify the approximation question before trying to repair it: absolute or relative error, retained or all inputs, zero or positive starting time, finite or unlimited duration. Finite rank has an exact full-space identity error at time zero; heat smoothing may give a useful delayed bound, whereas real-time unitarity does not supply that smoothing. A time/input restriction changes the approximation question, not the action.

Center each heat operator by its own actual ground energy. For a numerical spectral representation, preserve an exact unit ground coefficient and round positive excitation gaps separately. A small independent error in the ground exponent can grow or erase the long-time ground limit. Keep physical truncation, representation arithmetic and scalar time-evaluation errors distinct. An absolute bound gives a useful relative bound only with a justified denominator lower bound for the stated input class.

In the reviewed Round24 T graph example, the actual 21-state Ritz residual is proportional to `(sum W_p)^2-5`. Its squared norm factor is 195/4 from 20 spin-one and 190 face-pair channels. Spin-one terms alone retain 1/39 of that norm. These numbers apply to that graph and normalization only. The exact electric first-moment refinement arose in forward work and passed separate review; do not relabel it an independently duplicated discovery.

Require discriminating missing-channel, wrong-normalization, invalid-separation, zero-coupling and wrong-centering controls as relevant. Keep complete source decomposition distinct from completeness of the entire omitted Hilbert space. A finite graph's ground gap cannot be transferred to another model, to excited Bohr frequencies, or to a continuum claim. Scientific priority remains unverified without a primary-source comparison.
