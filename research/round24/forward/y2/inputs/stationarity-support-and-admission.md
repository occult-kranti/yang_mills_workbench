# Stationarity, support and admission

Use these lessons for operator comparisons or evidence admission. Their examples come from reviewed Round22 N1/N2 and a separately recorded admission repair in the Yang–Mills workbench; they do not transfer a theorem between its models.

## Stationary operator comparison

Before retaining a scalar ground-energy error in a stationary correlation estimate, test whether the correlation can be rewritten using Heisenberg conjugation. Prove the rewrite with the actual state, complex adjoint and both centering means. A scalar cancels from conjugation; it does not disappear from an arbitrary single-unitary matrix element.

Do not differentiate an expression containing H B unless B preserves the required unbounded operator domain. For bounded perturbations, a useful route is to differentiate a relative unitary product first on the common generator domain, establish a bounded strongly continuous derivative on all vectors, integrate vectorwise and only then insert the bounded observable. State which integral exists; a norm estimate after a strong integral does not make it an operator-norm Bochner integral.

## Growing supports and endpoint claims

Define the complete reference-factor cover before counting local interactions. For tail-owned blocks, links leaving the drawn vertex box still belong to the block. Count every incident interaction, including those that cross the cover boundary. Derive an all-size incidence argument before using finite enumeration as a check.

Apply a local estimate separately at each regulator value when the observable support grows. Identify which part of the estimate is uniform over bounded operators and which depends on the growing cover. Compare the same moving observables on both sides.

When the upper certificate is a sum of nonnegative terms, its vanishing condition may be necessary and sufficient for that certificate alone. A nonvanishing certificate supplies no lower bound on actual error. State floors separately at zero growth exponent: a fixed floor need not be asymptotic to its real-valued design parameter. Record whether origin-boundary geometry survives translation; downward closure can fail for a translated region.

## Coherent evidence mutations

Test admission after changing both an artifact and its recorded hashes. A stale-hash mutation tests only byte binding. In the demonstrated failure, a validator admitted removal of a contract-required instruction snapshot after its manifest and gate were consistently rebound; it also admitted a consistently rebound failed control file.

Require the contract's entire declared instruction set in each producer closure and the gate inventory. Validate required control identities and exact Boolean success values as well as output hashes. Execute rejection tests with ordinary and optimized Python; disabled assertions must not admit failure. Keep the original gate immutable and record validator repairs separately.

Bind the actual replay and validation source in final release evidence. Preserve the instruction snapshots used by each admitted loop when current skills change. Temporary replay directories can be deleted after comparisons; retain compact commands, statuses and hashes rather than duplicating every output tree.

Hashes, independent agreement and passing fixtures remain evidence controls. Mathematical claims still require their scoped arguments and skeptical review.
