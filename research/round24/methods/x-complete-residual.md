# Complete residual and approximation question

An explicitly assembled physical Ritz matrix is a useful computation only when the full operator residual is also accounted for. Keep all product channels and shared-link representation branches. A nonzero omitted matrix element proves leakage; it is not an upper bound for the complete omitted norm. Use an actual spectral separation with the correct operator domain before deriving energy or projection errors.

X2's finite graph has `||((sum W_p)^2-5)||^2=195/4`; keeping spin-one faces alone loses 38/39 of that squared norm. This is a project-specific example of the general obligation, not a constant for a different graph or model. Its refined first energy moment was derived in forward work and then reviewed; do not claim both directions independently found it.

Specify whether an approximation is absolute or relative, which input class is admitted, and whether the window starts at zero, at positive time, or extends forever. Center each heat operator by its own actual ground energy. A rational spectral surrogate should preserve an exact unit ground coefficient; rounding that exponent independently can invalidate all-time accuracy. Keep representation error, scalar evaluation error and physical truncation error separate.

An approximation restriction or regulator is not a new action. Useful exceptions require a proved input, spectral or time condition. Do not transfer finite-graph ground-gap constants to excited Bohr frequencies, another spatial family, or the homogeneous S model. Preserve every earlier gate's original instruction bytes.
