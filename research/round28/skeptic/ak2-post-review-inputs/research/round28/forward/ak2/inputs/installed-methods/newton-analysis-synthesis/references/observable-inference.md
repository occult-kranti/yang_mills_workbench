# Observable inference and finite readout

Use when turning a proved local correlation into a measurement claim. These lessons were independently checked in Yang–Mills Workbench Round24 V1; they are standard operator methods applied to its declared observable, not external peer review or established scientific novelty.

Start backward from the exact target: a complex unordered connected correlation is not determined by the real product of two sequential projective outcomes. Name the measurement instrument and state disturbance. A finite-spectrum observable can have an infinite-rank zero eigenspace, so finitely many outcomes do not truncate the physical dynamics.

Then synthesize forward. If ||W||≤1 and ||A−W||≤δ, a single connected correlator changes by at most 4δ+2δ² for ||A||≤1+δ; comparing two models costs twice that. Unitary automorphisms preserve norm, making the estimate uniform in time. For contractions the two-model cost improves to 8δ. State-preparation, timing, gate and sampling errors require separate estimates.

State the operator space. A finite-rank unit-norm operator W on a nonatomic L² space has distance at least 1/2 from every bounded multiplication operator: an orthonormal sequence supported where |f| is large gives ||W−M_f||≥||f||∞, and the triangle inequality gives ≥1−||f||∞. This blocks that norm-approximation route, not a different multiplication observable or a weaker state-dependent topology. A full-local-space bound does not silently establish the same bound after restriction to a physical quotient.

Use a computed wrong-model control for the noncommuting measurement/sign issue. A stipulated imaginary expectation can illustrate a proof but is not a circuit simulation. Classify an abstract coherent-control protocol separately from finite circuit construction, feasible cost and laboratory validation.
