# Finite observations and operator limits

## Identify a family before fitting it

Write each initial correlation slope as a linear functional of mobility. For a declared finite basis, compute the actual coefficient matrix and its rank on the stated parameter domain; include parity-blind choices as controls. Separate a positivity or gap region from the domain where an inverse was proved. Give conditioning bounds for coefficient reconstruction and inspect division by a clock coefficient approaching zero.

A full-rank fit inside one finite family does not identify unrestricted positive mobility. For N initial slopes, the N functionals on degree-at-most-N polynomials have a nonzero nullspace. Check full marginal support so a nonzero polynomial is a nonzero function, normalize by its coefficient l1 norm, and use an explicit baseline positivity margin before adding it. This construction concerns a finite list of initial slopes. Test higher derivatives or complete time curves before extending the ambiguity claim to them.

Treat fitted or introduced mobility coefficients as model deformations until physical matching is independently established. Classify an observable-design coefficient separately from a dynamical coefficient. Check classical polynomial formulas before describing an explicit null direction as an invented family. In the worked SU(2) example, x^3-3x/8 and x^5-5x^3/6+x/8 are scaled Gegenbauer polynomials, and a fifth-degree first-slope null direction changes the same observable's second time derivative.

## Preserve the full operator support and topology

For an operator on dressed reference factors, close its displayed support to every link of those factors before counting interacting faces. The displayed Wilson edges may suffice for that multiplication operator but not for an arbitrary operator on its complete factor cover. Check the boundary incidence rule, count each face once and show the commutator series reduces to the finite intersecting set.

To prove strong convergence from a vacuum residual, use a dense bounded-local-operator orbit and a uniform operator bound. A rank-one operator on complete finite factors proves that density without dividing by a vacuum wavefunction or assuming an arbitrary bounded operator preserves an unbounded Hamiltonian domain.

Distinguish convergence on fixed vectors from a supremum over moving vectors. A perturbation with nonzero constant operator norm can tend strongly to zero. That norm alone determines neither the norm-resolvent limit nor the operator-norm limit of propagators. State the topology and use a discriminating example for the inference actually under review.

For resolvent identities place the strongly convergent perturbation on a fixed reference-resolvent vector. For compact-time strong dynamics justify uniformity on the compact reference orbit, or give a uniform local Duhamel bound. Include the ground-energy subtraction explicitly. A fixed-time theorem does not automatically extend to growing observation windows.


For connected real-time correlations, compare the raw matrix element and both mean factors, including conjugation for complex observables. Subtract the actual ground energy so the unitary fixes the vacuum. Re-establish the gauge-invariant cyclic/reducing sector for each coefficient profile instead of carrying over a numerical gap from another profile. Finite interaction truncations and bounded-tail Duhamel can prove quasilocality without point-norm continuity of arbitrary bounded observables. For growing windows, keep the explicit time factor in the residual estimate; a nonvanishing sufficient upper bound is not a proof of nonconvergence.

## Transfer connected correlations with the correct vacuum and time scale

For complex observables, center both factors with conjugation on the first mean. Test invariance under adding independent scalar constants to either observable. A scalar ground-energy shift cancels in Heisenberg conjugation but must be retained in a unitary matrix element with stationary-vacuum subtraction; constant observables provide a rejecting control.

Separate state-distance costs, both mean-product costs and the dynamical Duhamel cost. Prove that reference factor evolution preserves the complete finite support for every time before making the local estimate uniform. Do not assume arbitrary bounded operators preserve an unbounded generator domain. Re-establish the physical invariant sector and its own gap for a changed coupling profile rather than transferring a numerical gap from another model.

For a growing window, substitute its physical scale into every error term and inspect the endpoint limit. A nonvanishing upper bound at an endpoint establishes failure of that certificate, not nonconvergence of the actual correlations. Give any nonzero fluctuation witness in the actual profile ground state, with a concrete sufficient projector-distance condition. Keep a growing-window exponent as an observation protocol, not an action deformation or a fitted physical clock.
