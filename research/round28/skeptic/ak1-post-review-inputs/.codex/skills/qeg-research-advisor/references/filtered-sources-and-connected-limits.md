# Local sources, filtered commutators and connected limits

Apply these lessons when continuing local operator inversion or filtered dynamics. They were admitted from Round23 S1/S2 in the Yang–Mills workbench; their numerical constants belong only to its frozen initial model.

## Identify the actual source

A nonzero indexed term in a BCH remainder does not prove that the full remainder or its grouped coefficient is nonzero. Keep the index, order, support and any possible cancellation explicit. A creator with a prescribed vacuum image is not the same operator as a local source tensored with the exterior identity. Test the distinction on an actual nonvacuum exterior state. An upper bound on a crossing commutator is not a calculation of its nonzero value.

For a full commutator inverse, a ground gap is insufficient: equal-energy source blocks must vanish and the divided-difference map needs a boundedness/locality argument. A finite resonant matrix refutes a general inference, but does not prove a resonance obstruction in the actual physical model. Keep actual solvability and actual obstruction separately unresolved when neither is established.

## Retain the filter residual

A compact triangular strong integral can yield a bounded skew map with an exact time-average residual. Derive the distributional jump and endpoints, fix the commutator sign, and evaluate the zero-frequency value. For the triangular example the residual multiplier is sinc(Theta omega): shrinking Theta leaves the low-frequency residual close to the source. It does not improve the inverse. A scalar multiplier bound alone does not bound its induced map on all bounded operators.

Classify Theta as a proof duration/energy resolution with units, distinct from an action parameter or calibrated clock. Do not remove a filter without estimating its residual in the topology required by the next stage.

## Prove domains and support sums separately

If a bounded source does not preserve the generator domain, use scalar pairings on domain vectors and integration by parts. A bounded weak commutator plus self-adjointness can establish graph preservation through the adjoint-domain characterization. Prove exponential preservation in the graph norm; do not assume it from Hilbert norm boundedness alone.

A connected union-size bound is not a weighted interaction estimate. Include ordered words, repeated anchors, stars entering the accumulated support, root translations, commutator factors and time simplices. Different complete positive counts may yield different valid constants. Attribute refinements to their actual derivation. Do not integrate a positive series outside its convergence radius just because the exact operator integral exists.

For an infinite initial operator, identify the finite-volume limit: use an actual common-form tail or a proved resolvent criterion, then justify the required evolution topology. Stabilization of finite-depth local words plus a uniform summable tail can prove norm convergence for one local observable, while strong resolvent convergence identifies that limit. Neither gives global norm convergence of unitaries, equality of operator domains, or a bounded sum of every translated generator.

These initial-operator results require new hypotheses for later evolving diagonals. Preserve immutable instruction snapshots in each gate when updating the live skill or agent rules.
