My preference is the first two pairs. The homogeneous observable question works directly in the already admitted nonsummable model and makes a representation obligation concrete. The fixed-design identification question addresses a demonstrated limitation of the successful AI4 family rather than adding Taylor terms to an already adequate retained calculation. The numerical heat evaluator is a useful alternative if the advisor chooses a computational deliverable, but it provides less direct progress on the outstanding homogeneous representation and calibration obligations. None is promised to succeed.

For the homogeneous pair, freeze the exact I1 state/boundary prescription and algebra before production. Distinguish local gauge invariance of the state, implementation of the gauge action in its GNS space, the invariant-vector projection, the physical observable cyclic closure, reduction of the actual time generator, and any nonzero observable assertion. The gap restriction is conditional on the admitted I1 smallness range with source constants still symbolic. Equality of unrelated thermodynamic boundary limits remains outside the target. The second loop should be chosen from the first review, not presumed to establish a Wilson fluctuation automatically.

For the identification pair, the smallest useful first contract is one compact externally known band with independently specified alpha and carrier phase. Freeze the candidate relations, actual timestamps, data vector, complete collars, preparation/readout/timing/phase errors and promised separation statement before arithmetic. The realized scaled duration varies across that band; AI4's coefficients cannot be copied as though it were constant. Only after reviewing the first result should the second contract compare independent nuisance values across candidates, perhaps returning a certified set or an unresolved option. A retained collision or overlapping conservative physical enclosures is not by itself an impossibility theorem. Exact point recovery with nonzero uncertainties should not be promised.

I do not recommend using the remaining two-loop pair merely to rename unknown stability constants. A genuinely new explicit homogeneous interval would need all source, inverse, diagonal, support, domain and convergence constants; an all-stage AG construction would need the actual changed reference and a summable weight allocation. Those are legitimate alternatives only with a bounded contract and an honest insufficient outcome, not a predetermined positive decimal.

The two producers give about 8.5197e-5; the independent skeptic uses a slightly tighter valid source cap and gives about 8.4637e-5. These are proof-budget constants, not empirical accuracies or a metric for Yang–Mills completion. Six reviewed investigations out of ten measures the authorized work schedule; it does not mean 60% of the Yang–Mills problem has been solved.
