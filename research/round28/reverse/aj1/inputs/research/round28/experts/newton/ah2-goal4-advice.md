## Comparison with the other two candidate directions

**Certified numerical heat evaluation** is the most immediate deliverable
using the completed AH matrices. A bounded goal would use the actual 561-state
metric at a fixed declared coupling, preserve the AH2 physical budget and
add certified early polynomial, center, arithmetic/export and late ground-
projector errors over the same true denominator. AF2 already did this for the
older 293-state graph, so the new contribution would be a verified extension
and executable states, not an invented numerical principle. A finite-time
branch plus a late projector avoids exponentiating an uncertain center forever.
The contract must distinguish a fixed-coupling evaluator from a uniform
continuous-coupling claim. This route is useful and lower in conceptual risk,
but it does not address the homogeneous representation or continuum gap.

**Unknown-clock readout** addresses a different genuine quantifier gap.
Tesla's candidate A asks for one design chosen from an independently known
prior band, valid throughout that band. AI4's separate setting for every
candidate u does not supply this. A bounded first question would freeze the
prior band, known energy/phase information, common physical timestamps,
collar/readout settings and complete errors before evaluating a new bound.
The realized scaled duration varies with u and must be controlled anew.

Even success there remains a same-u paired statement unless a decision rule
also compares H1(u) with H2(v) for independently varying nuisance parameters.
Tesla's candidate B is the relevant later cross-parameter question, perhaps
with a declared unresolved region or resolution. Candidate C adds independent
clock/carrier calibration and alias control and is appreciably broader.
No phase cancellation or positive fixed instrument floor over the whole
shrinking-gap endpoint follows from AI4. This direction is valuable for
identifiability and would be a reasonable alternative if that is the advisor's
priority, but it remains in the canonical model rather than solving the
homogeneous or continuum gap.
