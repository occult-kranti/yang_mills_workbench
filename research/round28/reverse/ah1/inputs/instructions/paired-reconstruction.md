# Reconstruction and interval lessons

## Spectral and graph reconstruction

Count physical channels as the sum over admissible edge assignments of the product of vertex intertwiner multiplicities. One assignment need not represent one state. Match edges by endpoint coordinates, faces by cyclic ordered signed words and states by physical supports before comparing implementation indices.

Compare ordered matrix entries and their adjoints before reducing a Hermitian matrix to a triangle or comparing quadratic forms. Include an antisymmetric wrong-matrix control: opposite errors in transposed entries must not cancel in admission. For an enlarged projector P_new=P_old+R, account for the removed complement contribution P_old V R V P_old rather than reusing the old cross Gram unchanged.

For bounded self-adjoint V, state D(H_ref+V)=D(H_ref). Distinguish this operator fact from a coefficient-norm computation. For a finite-volume limit, specify one Hilbert-space embedding, the convergence topology and any reference-state energy subtraction. Strong resolvent convergence alone does not preserve an isolated gap or a nonzero limiting ground state; supply the missing spectral/projection argument.

A scalar boundary-energy subtraction preserves states, gaps and Heisenberg conjugation, but changes absolute energies and fixed-z resolvents. For a local-state limit, complete the reference-factor supports of the observable as well as the retained interactions. Moving boundary factors can converge on every fixed local observable while failing global vector-norm convergence.

When the perturbation norm does not vanish, test the reference-vector residual or vacuum variance against a uniform excited threshold. This can control ground projections in a topology that the global operator-norm estimate does not settle. Derive covariance cancellations from actual free integration variables; pairwise orthogonality does not imply independence of higher joint moments. A bounded-budget deformation that returns to a reference state can obstruct that route to nonzero homogeneous interactions without ruling out local stability theorems or a physical mass gap.

## Shared-variable integration

When active faces share a link, retain that one random variable in the joint measure. Replacing it with independently resampled copies is a different model. Use a mixed moment that detects the difference. Derive freeze-link regressions algebraically: a common exponential factor in numerator and partition may cancel exactly; interval overlap does not prove this identity.

Call exterior-fixed links a conditional model unless gauge-fixing equivalence to the original graph is proved. A fixed static density can admit many reversible generators with different rates; a proposed generator and its coefficient require their own contract. Differentiate with respect to the actual shared variables before closing the observable algebra; a generated trace term may be a derived observable rather than an added interaction.

Distinguish raw moments from Taylor coefficients: the nth coefficient includes 1/n!. Compare exact exponent-keyed moment tables rather than relying on row order or decimal closeness. Verify explanatory coefficient metadata as well as executable arithmetic.

## From a point to an interval

For F(k)=N(k)/Z(k), state separately the certified interval, endpoint and sign-asymmetry range. An accurate enclosure at one k is not a continuous bound. Derive a uniform remainder from the declared |action| and |observable| bounds. If N(k)>=c k^2 with c>=0, then use Z(k)>0 and a proved upper bound Z(k)<=E to conclude F(k)>=c k^2/E; a lower denominator bound points the wrong way. Establish k=0 from exact coefficients before dividing by k^2 or k^3.

Use D(k)=N(k)Z(-k)-N(-k)Z(k) and positive denominators for sign comparisons. Keep retained polynomial terms and the full product remainder separate. A negative lower-certificate margin only shows that certificate is insufficient; it does not prove the observable or gap is negative. Boundary probes should preserve this distinction.

Recompute a producer's claimed exponential envelope, retained losses and remainder at its declared interval. A positive recorded margin or an enlarged interval label is not the corresponding inequality. Execute coherent false-certificate controls that preserve file structure and point values while changing those premises.

## Time evolution

Unbounded local Casimirs can have strongly continuous Hilbert-space implementations without point-norm continuity on every bounded local operator. State the algebra and topology of a time-evolution claim; use a high-energy shift observable to test an asserted universal norm-continuity premise. A fixed-time local approximation estimate and a norm-continuous C*-dynamics theorem are different conclusions.

## Gates and feedback

Require every declared source and output before verifying its hash. Run controls that alter theorem content, not only presence flags. A comparison that computes its expected value from the same producer implementation is not independent reconstruction. Retain the failed premise and its repair without copying large unchanged outputs into every historical directory; record deltas and reusable input bindings when sufficient.

Freeze the accepted scientific inventory before publishing and keep presentation metadata outside it where possible. A later scientific edit needs renewed review; a later design change should not silently rewrite an accepted derivation. Count paired research loops, repeated executions and advisor decisions separately. If two later goals must be chosen after three completed pairs, keep them unselected until that decision point.
