# Contract for the Yang–Mills connection

The closest related Millennium target is existence and mass gap for nontrivial pure quantum Yang–Mills on four-dimensional space for every compact simple gauge group. Check Clay's maintained status when reporting it. The finite U(1) system's positive coefficient is not a Hamiltonian gap, reflection positivity or a continuum construction. An added scalar changes the theory; inserting a mass does not solve pure Yang–Mills.

For a finite nonzero positive measure, C(t)=integral exp(-Et)dmu(E), the exact effective mass [log C(t)-log C(t+delta)]/delta is at least inf supp mu and decreases at fixed positive delta. This is an upper estimate of the channel threshold. Noise and fitted finite-time windows do not inherit an exact bound automatically. Thermal wraparound requires a different correlator model. Use smeared operators giving finite-norm states and specify vacuum subtraction.

One sufficient gap strategy assumes the physical Hilbert space and nonnegative self-adjoint Hamiltonian already exist. Uniform regulator/volume bounds C_O(t)<=A_O exp(-m t), with a common physical m>0 and fixed finite per-operator prefactors/onset times, pass through pointwise continuum limits. Positivity excludes spectral weight below m; density of the vacuum-orthogonal operator states extends this exclusion to the whole physical sector. None of these Yang–Mills premises has been supplied by the current project. The conditional spectral argument is standard; do not claim literature novelty.

Keep a (spacing), L (physical volume), coupling matching and physical temporal extent distinct. Every finite-regulator gap being positive does not imply a common positive lower bound. A periodic massless nonzero-mode frequency explicitly excludes a zero mode and is not the full finite-volume mass gap. A strong-coupling lattice theorem needs a separate argument to reach a weak-coupling continuum path.
