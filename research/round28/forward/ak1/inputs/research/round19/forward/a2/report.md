## Representation and scale

Fix one positive physical reference energy \(E_\star>0\). The evidence records \(\alpha/E_\star\), \(\delta/E_\star\), \(\beta/E_\star\) and \(\tau\) separately. Static Euclidean \(\kappa\), tolerances, Fibonacci labels, lattice volume and graph coordinates are not Hamiltonian energy scales.

A1 accepted the local full-link estimate \(\delta=\alpha/8\) for every clipped component and, in particular, for the complete three-face `LMR` strip. A2 uses only complete infinite strips in a direct product representation. It does not prove that finite clipped restrictions converge to that representation.

The reference sector is the incomplete tensor product whose reference vector is

\[
\Omega_{\rm ref}=\bigotimes_C \Omega_C\otimes\bigotimes_e \Omega_e,
\]

where \(C\) ranges over complete strip anchors \((4i,2j,z)\), \(i,j,z\ge0\). Each \(\Omega_C\) is the complete-strip ground state from the accepted local theorem. Each free-link vector \(\Omega_e\) is the normalized Haar constant on a link not contained in a selected strip support.

The finite tensor core is the finite linear span of tensors in which all but finitely many factors equal their local ground vector, and the remaining factors lie in the corresponding local operator/form domains. This is the core used for the quadratic form. It is not the orbit of the product vector under arbitrary bounded operators.

## Reference Hamiltonian and gap

Define the nonnegative reference form

\[
H_{\rm ref}=\sum_C (H_C-E_C)+\sum_{e\in F}\alpha C_e.
\]

Each shifted complete-strip term is nonnegative with gap at least \(\alpha/8\). Each free link has Casimir gap \(3\alpha/4\), so it also dominates \(\delta=\alpha/8\). On the finite tensor core,

\[
q_{\rm ref}(\psi)\ge \delta\|(1-P_\Omega)\psi\|^2.
\]

The closed reference form is the closure of this monotone nonnegative sum on the finite tensor core, and \(H_{\rm ref}\) is the associated self-adjoint operator. The same inequality extends to the closed form domain. This gives a one-dimensional kernel generated by \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\) and spectral subspace above the vacuum bounded below by \(\delta\) in this representation. No compact-resolvent property is asserted or needed.

## Summable perturbation

A2 adds only omitted faces outside the complete infinite strips as a bounded summable perturbation

\[
V=-\sum_{f\in O}\nu_f x_f,
\qquad \nu_f=\alpha\tau w_f,
\qquad w_f=\frac{1}{24\,2^{x+y+z}}.
\]

The selected xy faces are those on even-y rows with x residues 0, 1 or 2 mod 4. All other faces have the actual dyadic coefficient \(\nu_f=\alpha\tau/(24\,2^{x+y+z})\). The omitted cases are exhaustive: non-xy faces, odd-y xy faces and x-separator xy faces. Each omitted face has an actual unused Haar factor in the reference product: a z link for non-xy faces, an odd-y link for odd-y xy faces, and a horizontal separator link for x residue 3 on even-y rows. The tail fixtures record finite coordinate cutoffs and the exact positive remaining tail to the infinite ledger. Therefore each term has zero reference mean, and

\[
\langle\Omega_{\rm ref},V\Omega_{\rm ref}\rangle=0.
\]

The exact positive orthant ledger is

\[
\sum_{\text{all faces}}w_f=1,
\qquad
\sum_{\text{selected}}w_f=\frac{28}{135},
\qquad
\sum_{\text{omitted}}w_f=\frac{107}{135}.
\]

Thus \(V\) is a bounded self-adjoint operator with

\[
\|V\|\le \beta_{\rm exact}=\alpha |\tau|\frac{107}{135}\le \beta_{\rm crude}=\alpha|\tau|.
\]

Signed coefficients do not reduce this absolute budget.

## Spectral isolation theorem

Let \(P_\Omega=|\Omega_{\rm ref}\rangle\langle\Omega_{\rm ref}|\) and \(Q=1-P_\Omega\). Since \(V\) is bounded self-adjoint, Kato-Rellich gives \(H=H_{\rm ref}+V\) self-adjoint on exactly \(D(H_{\rm ref})\), so \(D(H)=D(H_{\rm ref})\). Its closed quadratic-form domain is also unchanged: \(Q(H)=Q(H_{\rm ref})\), with form \(q_H=q_{\rm ref}+\langle\cdot,V\cdot\rangle\).

For every normalized \(\psi\perp\Omega_{\rm ref}\) in the form domain,

\[
q_H(\psi)\ge \delta-\beta.
\]

Also \(q_H(\Omega_{\rm ref})=0\) by the zero-mean ledger. If \(\beta<\delta\), the spectral projection below \(\delta-\beta\) is nonzero because \(q_H(\Omega_{\rm ref})=0\) is below that threshold. It has dimension at most one: any two-dimensional subspace contains a nonzero vector orthogonal to \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\), whose form value is at least \(\delta-\beta\). A nonzero finite-dimensional spectral subspace below an isolated threshold is an eigenprojection, so this proves a genuine isolated ground eigenvalue without compact resolvent. All other spectrum is at or above \(\delta-\beta\), and the spectral gap in this product representation is at least \(\delta-\beta\) because the ground energy is at most zero.

For the default \(|\tau|=1/64\), the crude common-scale result is

\[
\Delta\ge \alpha\left(\frac18-\frac1{64}\right)=\frac{7\alpha}{64},
\]

and hence \(7\alpha_{\min}/64\) when \(\alpha/E_\star\ge\alpha_{\min}/E_\star>0\). The exact omitted ledger gives the sharper internal value

\[
\Delta\ge \alpha\left(\frac18-\frac{107}{8640}\right)=\frac{973\alpha}{8640}.
\]

The reported theorem may use either the exact ledger or the conservative contract bound; the conservative bound is the one used for common-scale wording.

## Gauge-sector wording and nonvacuity

The complete-strip Hamiltonians, free-link Casimirs and plaquette trace perturbations are gauge invariant on every local finite support. Each complete-strip ground is unique, so it is gauge invariant; each free Haar constant is gauge invariant; hence \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\) is invariant under finite-support vertex gauge transformations.

The perturbed Hamiltonian also commutes with every finite-support local gauge group. The spectral projection below \(\delta-\beta\) is one-dimensional, so a finite-support gauge transformation maps its ground vector to a scalar multiple of itself. This scalar is a continuous one-dimensional character of a finite product of SU(2) vertex groups, and such characters are trivial. The unique perturbed ground is therefore gauge invariant.

The Gauss-invariant sector is nontrivial beyond the vacuum: for any omitted plaquette \(f\), the vector \(x_f\Omega_{\rm ref}\) is gauge invariant, lies in the finite tensor form domain, has zero reference mean by the unused-link Haar witness, and has nonzero norm by the corresponding Haar second moment. Any physical-sector gap wording is restricted to this product representation and this nonvacuous invariant sector. It is not a convergence theorem for finite clipped restrictions.
