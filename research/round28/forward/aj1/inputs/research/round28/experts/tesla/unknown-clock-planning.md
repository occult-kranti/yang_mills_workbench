# Tesla-method planning: unknown parameters and a fixed readout design

This is source-only planning after AI3/AI4 admission, while AH2 remains in
progress. It proposes bounded questions for later panel consideration. It
selects no goal or loop and supplies no new calculation, fixture, schedule,
derived bound or executable result. Current AH2 science was not read. All
accepted sources and previous expert notes remain unchanged.

## What is already available

The AI4 gate admits a uniform family of complete physical ratio certificates
for the canonical summable full-link SU(2) model and actual B4/B5 Wilson
multipliers. For each proposed pair, the two hypotheses share the original
physical time `2zu^-3 hbar/alpha`, with the admitted `z=10^-7` and collar
schedule. The admitted pairwise gap is at least `39u/40`. Both the clock and
resource schedule are indexed by the proposed u. This is useful information
about a family of candidate tests, but does not tell an instrument how to
choose its time before the exact unknown u has been learned.

There is a second, separate quantifier issue. Separation of H1(u) from H2(u)
at every u does not establish separation of H1(u) from H2(v) when u and v are
unrelated unknowns in a prior set. The measured ratio uses no true q, but a
decision threshold selected using the true unknown parameter would still be
circular. A common fixed clock alone would not resolve that threshold issue.

AI3 already established that better retained arithmetic need not overcome
state and spatial upper budgets. AI4 succeeded with a different duration and
a uniform full-error argument. Further Taylor terms or a replay at another
known u would not address either missing quantifier. Every added scalar
tolerance currently admitted vanishes with u; a fixed positive absolute
instrument floor is not covered throughout the endpoint family. Unknown
common phase also need not preserve the imaginary-only ratio.

## Candidate A: a clock selected from a coarse prior band

**Question.** Given a declared coarse band known independently of the
measurement, can one choose the physical time, complete collar and readout
settings using only that band, then certify the inherited H1(u)/H2(u) test
for every real u in it without retuning the settings?

This would change the design quantifier from a separate setting for each
exact candidate pair to one setting shared across an unknown-parameter band.
It is therefore new relative to AI4 even if the same probes and retained
moment lemma suffice. A bounded first target could concern one fixed prior
band; an all-band schedule would be an additional obligation, not an automatic
extension. No band, time or schedule is chosen here.

The contract would need to freeze the band's endpoints and their status as
external prior information; the candidate relations; what is known about
alpha, hbar and carrier phase; the common physical timestamps; the observable
normalization and complete source support; collar selection; and all allowed
preparation, readout, timing and phase errors. When the time is fixed from the
band, the realized scaled duration varies with the unknown parameters.
AI4's numerical constants cannot simply be reused as if that duration stayed
at its prescribed value. The complete physical transfer must be bounded over
the entire new domain.

A useful accepted result would certify a band-wide same-u separation at that
fixed design, or precisely identify why its sufficient bounds fail. It would
remove exact-u dependence from the clock setting. It would still be a paired
theorem unless it also supplied a decision rule independent of the nuisance
u and checked cross-parameter candidates. Calling it an unknown-u instrument
on the strength of the paired inequality alone would overstate the result.

## Candidate B: composite-family discrimination or set-valued localization

**Question.** At one predeclared physical measurement design, can the complete
B4/B5 data exclude wrong parameter regions when every nuisance parameter is
allowed to vary independently in each competing hypothesis?

The smaller version would compare two explicitly bounded candidate classes,
with a declared unresolved region or minimum parameter resolution if needed.
The richer version would return a certified set of compatible `(q,eta)` in
a declared compact family, initially with independently known alpha and
phase. Neither version should promise an exact point inverse from finite
data with nonzero uncertainties. The raw scalar data may contain information
discarded by the ratio; the admissible data vector must be frozen before
testing, rather than enlarged after seeing a failure.

This question is new relative to AI4 because it must compare different
parameter values across the two candidate sets. It is also different from
AI1's conditional retained slope-ratio algebra and its exact fibers for
declared surrogates: the data here would be finite-time observables of the
actual canonical model, with physical transfer errors. Round21 L1's exact
three-rate inverse belongs to a different conditional mobility model.
L2's finite-slope nullspace obstruction neither supplies nor rules out a
finite-time inverse for this canonical family.

The contract would need to freeze the parameter family and prior bounds,
which coordinates are unknown, the parameter-space distance or resolution,
the common measurement design, admissible data, error model, and a globally
valid rule for keeping or excluding each candidate. It must check all
cross-candidate combinations relevant to the promised conclusion; a local
Jacobian or successful recovery of a few synthetic points is insufficient.
If classes meet at a boundary, an exclusion zone or an explicitly unresolved
boundary may be necessary. Its existence and width must not be assumed.

A useful result could be a certified localization bound on the declared
domain, a valid class decision with an unresolved option, or a scoped failure
of the proposed sufficient enclosure. Overlap of conservative enclosures
would still not establish equality of physical outputs or an impossibility
theorem. An exact collision in the retained matrix would likewise need its
own relation to the full physical model before supporting that stronger
claim. A reserved datum can challenge the chosen family, but cannot identify
arbitrary model deformations.

## Candidate C: actual finite-q calibration under a declared clock and phase model

**Question.** Can a bounded finite-q physical data set determine or constrain
the energy scale and carrier nuisance parameters, under an independently
specified physical clock, without treating a fitted parameter as an exact
demodulation input?

AI1 already classified carrier-retaining surrogates and their conditional
information, including aliases from one phase time or uniform samples.
Repeating that algebra is not a new target. A new bounded question would
require actual finite-q B4/B5 scalar transfer, a declared finite prior for
the frequencies and nuisance parameters, and certified alias or ambiguity
control for the chosen observations. It need not attempt unrestricted
identification of alpha, eta and q simultaneously.

The contract would need to freeze an external time reference in physical
units, observations and timestamps, the frequency prior, whether phase is
known or estimated, allowed timestamp and phase errors, and exactly which
parameter interval the inference promises. Any estimated carrier must carry
its uncertainty through the ratio or later demodulation. A common unknown
phase cannot be assumed to cancel in an imaginary-only ratio. Introducing
another phase-insensitive observable would require a declared observable
and its complete physical error theorem.

This is the largest additional calibration burden of these candidates. A
useful bounded outcome would be a conditional interval for one scale with
proved ambiguity control, or a scoped obstruction for the specified data.
It would not be a demonstrated apparatus or an empirical calibration. The
finite-graph P1/P2 results also prevent borrowing a fitted conditional clock:
their different physical observable completions and full induced derivatives
matter, and neither maps the conditional mobility clock to the present
canonical interacting family.

## Common controls and decision criteria for later selection

Candidate A has the smallest change in scope. Candidate B directly addresses
the missing cross-parameter inference. Candidate C addresses independent
calibration and phase assumptions. These are alternatives for panel review,
not selections of goal 4 or goal 5, and their positive outcomes are unproved.

Any selected test must retain the actual Wilson source loading, connected
means, complete ten-link seed and factor collars, ordered spatial words,
both averaging columns and the growing M-squared cost. The sixteen-state
averaged reached component must retain its admitted status. A smaller
surrogate or a different conditional generator is not the same physical test.

The inherited signed retained-moment cancellation is available as an exact
finite-matrix premise. The physical combination error remains `R5+qR4`.
No value-to-derivative inference, cancellation of preparation or readout
errors, or uncharged denominator division is allowed. Preserve dependence
between shared numerator and denominator errors when useful; smaller interval
width is not endpoint containment. Charge rounding where it actually enters:
the admitted inner-square-root rounding has a tau multiplier, whereas a
final scalar uncertainty does not inherit that suppression.

A historical mechanism and measurement lens motivates asking who sets the
clock, what information is supplied beforehand, how the system is loaded,
and which output is actually observed. It supplies no additional force,
energy term, resonance law or historical-person endorsement. Successful
readout inference in this declared family would still leave homogeneous
matching and the continuum Yang–Mills construction and mass gap unresolved.
