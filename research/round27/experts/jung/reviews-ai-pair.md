## Decision: select AG1, subject to AI2 admission

The third loop should attempt the first **selected-source homogeneous nonlinear update** under Penrose's corrected contract. Preserve AI3 ratio sharpening as a planned follow-up. The rationale is proximity to an unresolved homogeneous stability premise, not a numerical prediction that AG1 will succeed.

AI1 established exactly which parameters its declared endpoint and physical-time surrogate maps identify. AI2 then tested the damaging alternative: identical endpoint surrogates need not mean identical actual finite-q correlations. Its complete scalar disks separate the two frozen hypotheses at one prescribed setting, `u=10^-18, k=5`, for both probes. This is the principal interpretation repair that motivated the identification pair. Further metrology improvement is useful, but it does not by itself advance the missing homogeneous interaction update.

## Why the canceled-ratio proposal is valuable but secondary

The exact moments in the AI2 reverse report give

\[
m_{3,4}=3q^{12}+5q^{14},\qquad
m_{3,5}=5q^{13}+3q^{15},
\]

and hence the proposed identity

\[
m_{3,5}-q m_{3,4}=2q^{13}(1-q^2).
\]

At the level of the exact retained analytic functions, their common first-order term cancels in

\[
\operatorname{Im}h_5-q\operatorname{Im}h_4
=-\frac{v^3}{3}q^{13}(1-q^2)+O(v^5).
\]

This direct algebraic consequence is a plausible route to a sharper ratio certificate. It does **not** validate a full uniform remainder or execute AI3. A new contract would have to preserve the cancellation in every retained higher-order term, certify the denominator, and propagate actual-model state, collar, averaging, arithmetic and calibration errors. Separate error bounds do not become correlated merely because their retained centers have a cancellation. If the certificate depends on unknown q, it must be uniform over a declared q interval before being used as an inverse.

In particular, eliminating the current uncanceled cubic floor need not improve the physical error terms. AI3 could become the next sensible experiment after a later metrology-focused authorization, but selecting it now would postpone the already identified homogeneous bottleneck.

A failed positive majorant should be reported as a limited outcome, not instability of the physical model. Those outcomes can still clarify the next roadmap decision.

## Percentage recommendation

There is still no defensible percentage of the Yang–Mills problem solved. The result count does not identify such a quantity: finite-model accuracy, canonical-model discrimination, homogeneous stability, physical matching, required continuum axioms, nontriviality and a positive physical mass gap are different obligations with dependencies, not equally weighted tasks.

For this specific assignment, the denominator of three requested loops is meaningful. At this note's cutoff, one loop is admitted and a second has agreed producer results awaiting final admission; the third is unselected. After the advisor admits all three, **3/3 requested loops completed** is a valid workflow statement. It must not be rewritten as 100% of Yang–Mills solved, nor converted into an arbitrary low percentage of the theorem.

The expert estimate should therefore be recorded as **not quantitatively estimable from current evidence**, followed by the named unresolved proof obligations. This preserves both the value of the calculations and the actual size of the remaining inference gap.
