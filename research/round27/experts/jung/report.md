## What current methods add

Petrica and Popescu's July 2026 revision supplies a useful local-inverse template: for a twice differentiable observation map, a positive lower singular-value bound at a parameter point and a uniform curvature bound certify a neighborhood of injectivity and a lower Lipschitz constant. Their distinction between identifying a discrete propagator and identifying its continuous generator is especially relevant to the workbench's finite-time readouts. This report inspected their theorem and proof; it has not imported its hypotheses into an infinite-dimensional gauge model. [J8]

Maclaren and Nicholson distinguish uniqueness from stable estimability. Exact identifiability can coexist with a badly conditioned inverse. This supports reporting both a nullspace and an inverse-error bound rather than just displaying a successful fit. [J9]

Zhu and Simeone's July 2026 revision studies data-dependent conformal selection using e-variables. Only the abstract was inspected. It is a candidate for future stochastic corpus work, not an admitted theorem for deterministic operator calculations or arbitrary browsing-generated claims. Its calibration and validity assumptions would have to be read and verified before use. [J10]

A source-critical check is essential even for recent mathematical preprints. Arai and Itano discuss identifying a velocity-gradient tensor from

\[
 f_A(s)=s\times(s\times As).
\]

Direct algebra gives

\[
 f_{A+cI}(s)-f_A(s)=c\,s\times(s\times s)=0.
\]

Their displayed scalar reconstruction discussion therefore cannot mean identifying an unrestricted isotropic shift from these observations. Incompressibility can fix the trace externally, but then that scalar is imposed, not observed. This is a narrow scope objection, not a complete assessment of the paper. The displayed manuscript date and version metadata also differ; the inventory retains both. Do not transfer its broad representation-theoretic claims into AI without resolving the parameter domain and nullspace. [J11]

## Candidate mathematical contract, not yet executed

Freeze a parameter domain \(\Theta\), the actual observables \(B_j\), preparation states, physical times, and a model-specific readout map

\[
 R:\Theta\longrightarrow\mathbb R^m.
\]

For any proposed target \(g(\theta)\), require the implication

\[
 R(\theta)=R(\theta')\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 g(\theta)=g(\theta').
\]

If it fails, exhibit the indistinguishable pair and report only the quotient parameters actually identified. If it holds locally, also seek a declared constant \(c>0\) such that

\[
 \|R(\theta)-R(\theta')\|\ge c\|\theta-\theta'\|
\]

on the stated identifiable domain. This excludes directions already quotiented out. If observed data are within \(\varepsilon\) of the true readout and a candidate parameter also fits within \(\varepsilon\), the triangle inequality gives parameter separation at most \(2\varepsilon/c\). This last step is an elementary consequence, not a new physical equation. Model approximation, numerical arithmetic, and measurement error need separate budgets before they are combined.

Required falsifiers: (1) a null observable; (2) an exactly invariant parameter family; (3) a held-out observable not chosen after the fit; (4) a near-singular design; and (5) two distinct generators with the same sampled propagator whenever the declared model class permits that alias. Parameter rank at one point gives neither global uniqueness nor physical model matching.

For any later empirical coincidence experiment, freeze the event dictionary, observation window, null distribution, exclusions, and trial count. With \(m\) valid predeclared tests and threshold \(u\), the union bound gives

\[
 \Pr_0(\min_i p_i\le u)\le\min(1,mu).
\]

The equality \(1-(1-u)^m\) additionally needs independent uniform null p-values. Neither formula legitimizes an unrecorded, adaptively redefined collection of anecdotes. No such empirical experiment is claimed executed here.
