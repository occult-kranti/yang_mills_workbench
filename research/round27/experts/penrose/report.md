## Historical sources and a useful modern inference

Penrose's 1971 spin-network paper [P02] starts from angular-momentum relations and explores emergent directional geometry. Its trivalent labels obey triangle and parity conditions, but the paper explicitly warns that satisfying local admissibility conditions does not prevent a complete network having zero norm. The useful inference for AH is precise: do not count candidate invariant diagrams as independent states. Compute their inner products, null relations, and complete channel support. This inference supplements the workbench's existing Gram gate; it supplies no new physical premise.

The handwritten *Theory of Quantized Directions* notes [P03] were inspected visually on their first two pages. They show angular-momentum coupling diagrams and projectors. I have not read or authenticated every page, and infer no hidden physical equation from them. The hosting index [P01] describes the stated provenance chain and Penrose's permission for the typed papers.

The primary *Twistor Algebra* abstract [P04] constructs a conformal/Poincaré geometric algebra involving null directions and location. That is motivation for asking whether two descriptions have a proved map. It is not a nonperturbative mass-gap theorem, and no twistor transform is supplied by the current workbench. A geometry-like evidence-network drawing is also not a spin network.

Penrose's gravitational reduction proposal [P05] associates a proposed superposition lifetime with a gravitational self-energy difference. It is explicitly a different physical proposal. Its abstract was read; the subscription full text was not. Substituting that proposed energy into a pure Yang–Mills Hamiltonian would change the model. Neither this proposal nor consciousness associations fill the missing continuum proof. I found no basis in these inspected primary sources for treating them as occult gauge dynamics.

## Proof obligations versus the draft

The official Clay statement [P06] requires a nontrivial quantum Yang–Mills theory on R^4 for every compact simple gauge group, with appropriate axioms and a positive finite physical mass scale. The current official page [P11] still marks the problem unsolved. The following table is this review's decomposition of the workbench's missing implications, not a Clay scoring system.

| Obligation | Present status |
|---|---|
| A proved map from the chosen model to the required local continuum theory | Missing; the model families are deliberately distinguished. |
| A declared cutoff/spacing/coupling trajectory with common physical units | Missing for the target theory; fixed-scale finite calculations exist. |
| Uniform estimates surviving representation, volume and cutoff limits | Selected fixed-graph tails are controlled; required combined limit estimates remain open. |
| A nontrivial limiting state/field theory with reconstruction, locality and positivity properties | Not constructed. |
| A positive physical spectral gap in that limiting theory | Not proved. |
| Extension from the current SU(2) work to every compact simple group in the official target | Not established. |

For a dimensionless Hamiltonian L(a), writing H(a)=E(a)L(a) only gives

\[
\Delta_{\rm phys}(a)=E(a)\,\Delta_L(a).
\]

This elementary unit conversion is not a novel theorem. A positive finite-cutoff value of Delta_L cannot establish a positive limiting physical gap without a justified E(a), compatible limiting construction and a uniform estimate. Numerical convergence inside one 293-state representation does not supply those hypotheses.
