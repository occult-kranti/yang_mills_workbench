## 1. Fix the parameters before taking the limit

Let `alpha>0` be an energy, `hbar>0` the fixed action reference, `eta in (0,1)`, `q in (0,1)`, and `epsilon=1-q`. Put

\[
 \beta=\alpha/\hbar,\qquad
 \rho=\eta\epsilon^3,\qquad
 \lambda=\frac{\beta\rho}{84}.
 \tag{AI1-F1}
\]

Here beta and lambda have units inverse time, rho is dimensionless, and `alpha/E_star` remains a separately meaningful dimensionless physical ratio. Neither eta nor q is a lattice spacing. AD2's observation schedule is

\[
 T_q(z)=\frac{z}{\eta}\frac{\hbar}{\alpha}\epsilon^{-3},
 \quad z=\beta\eta\epsilon^3 T_q,
 \quad \theta=z/84=\lambda T_q,
 \quad 0<z\le10^{-6}.
 \tag{AI1-F2}
\]

As inherited, alpha and eta are fixed while q tends to one along that schedule; the physical time diverges. q is a sequence index, not an independent parameter remaining in the limiting scalar. For elementary plaquette B0, six-cycle X, and coherent six-cycle sum S, respectively, write their endpoint functions in theta:

\[
 f_0(\theta)=1,
 \quad f_X(\theta)=\frac23+\frac14\cos2\theta+
                         \frac1{12}\cos(2\sqrt3\theta),
\]
\[
 f_S(\theta)=\frac13+\frac12\cos2\theta+
 \frac16\cos(2\sqrt3\theta)+
 i\left(\frac14\sin2\theta+\frac1{4\sqrt3}\sin(2\sqrt3\theta)\right).
 \tag{AI1-F3}
\]

The inherited theorem concerns `lim exp(i E_j T_q/hbar) C_j,q(T_q)=f_j(z/84)`, with `E_0=3 alpha` and `E_X=E_S=9 alpha/2`. It is not a theorem about derivatives of `C_j,q(t)` at t=0. In particular, the analytic extension of f outside the admitted z interval does not extend the physical theorem.

## 2. Two distinct observation conventions

**Fixed scaled coordinate z.** The complete endpoint curves `z -> f_j(z/84)` are identical for every admitted fixed alpha and eta. All these parameters have zero sensitivity in this readout map. Comparing two q sequences with the same endpoint supplies no q estimate. Producing the schedule T_q or the demodulation already requires parameter information; if that timing information is retained as data, it is additional data, not information extracted from the universal curve.

**Independently recorded physical t, using an asymptotic surrogate.** Define the algebraic map

\[
 D_j(t;\alpha,\eta,q)=f_j(\lambda t),
 \qquad 0<84\lambda t\le10^{-6}.
 \tag{AI1-F4}
\]

For fixed q this is a declared surrogate, not the exact finite-q readout. Its complete demodulated curves depend only on lambda. Any fiber with `alpha eta (1-q)^3` fixed gives identical curves. For example, in any fixed energy unit E_star,

\[
 (\alpha/E_*,\eta,q)=(1,1/4,1/2),\quad(2,1/8,1/2)
 \tag{AI1-F5}
\]

have identical lambda but distinct alpha. Even after alpha is independently fixed, the pairs `(eta,q)=(1/4,1/2)` and `(2/27,1/4)` have the same `eta(1-q)^3=1/32`. These are exact rational fibers strictly within the declared domain. Both constructions work arbitrarily close to q=1 by choosing a smaller epsilon and compensating eta within its open range.

At genuinely fixed physical t, with alpha and eta fixed, sending q to one forces `z=beta eta (1-q)^3 t` to zero. The inherited nontrivial fixed-z endpoint therefore cannot be interpreted as a nontrivial fixed-t limit. This report asserts neither uniform derivative convergence nor a fixed-time physical inverse theorem.

The converse also holds for this surrogate on the positive-rate domain: a single known positive t within the admitted range identifies lambda from the imaginary part of D_S. Indeed

\[
 \frac{d}{d\theta}\operatorname{Im} f_S(\theta)
 =\tfrac12\cos2\theta+\tfrac12\cos(2\sqrt3\theta)
 \ge 1-4\theta^2>0.
 \tag{AI1-F6}
\]

The inequality follows from `cos x>=1-x^2/2`. Likewise `f_X'=-sin(2theta)/2-sin(2sqrt(3)theta)/(2sqrt(3))<0` in this small positive interval. Thus X is informative at finite positive theta despite its zero linear coefficient. If signed rates were admitted, X alone would lose the sign because it is even; S's odd imaginary part resolves it locally. B0 is exactly constant after demodulation, so its nonzero variance gives no slow-rate information.

For log coordinates `(log alpha,log eta,log epsilon)`, the demodulated surrogate's rate differential is `[1,1,3]`, of rank one. Its kernel is two-dimensional, for example generated by `(1,-1,0)` and `(0,-3,1)`. More samples or more copies of these same curves do not remove that structural ambiguity. This is an analytic identifiability statement, not a numerical conditioning claim.

## 3. What retaining the carrier adds

Define corresponding undemodulated surrogates

\[
 U_0(t)=e^{-i3\beta t},\quad
 U_X(t)=e^{-i(9/2)\beta t}f_X(\lambda t),\quad
 U_S(t)=e^{-i(9/2)\beta t}f_S(\lambda t).
 \tag{AI1-F7}
\]

With calibrated physical time and phase, a complete continuous carrier curve U0 identifies beta. On the admitted interval f_X is real and strictly positive; hence the unwrapped phase of U_X identifies beta and its positive amplitude identifies lambda. These statements concern the declared surrogate curves. Equality on an open interval of these analytic functions implies equality of their analytic extensions, so one may equivalently inspect their power-series coefficients for the proof. This is not differentiation of the actual q-dependent endpoint convergence.

An independent check using S is its squared amplitude. Its inherited moments `m1=1,m2=4` give

\[
 |f_S(\lambda t)|^2=1-3\lambda^2t^2+O(t^4),
 \quad U_S'(0)=i(\lambda-9\beta/2)
 \tag{AI1-F8}
\]

as identities of analytic surrogates only. The positive lambda and then beta can be reconstructed. If q is independently known, eta follows uniquely as `84 lambda/[beta(1-q)^3]`. If q is unknown, beta and lambda leave the exact eta/q fiber above. The logarithmic Jacobian of `(beta,lambda)` has rows `[1,0,0]` and `[1,1,3]`; rank two, kernel `(0,-3,1)`.

At a single sampled time t0, the elementary carrier has aliases `beta -> beta+2 pi n/(3 t0)`. On a uniform grid of elementary-carrier samples this ambiguity persists. A prior frequency bound, nonaliased sampling design, or a continuous time curve is required. No exact alias-free inference from one phase sample is claimed. Using the wrong alpha for demodulation inserts a residual carrier and can mimic a changed slow slope; the calibration value must therefore be recorded independently.

## 4. Finite-q data do not equal endpoint data

The inherited finite-q center retains a weighted sixteen-state matrix:

\[
 Q(q)_{cd}=q^{r_f}\quad\text{on a face flip},\qquad r_f\in\{4,5\},
\]
\[
 p(q)=2+5q+5q^2+6q^3+3q^4,
 \quad s_q(z)=\frac{3z(1+q)^2(1+q^2)}{p(q)},
 \quad h_{j,q}(z)=\langle\psi_j,e^{i s_q(z)Q(q)/96}\psi_j\rangle.
 \tag{AI1-F9}
\]

For S, the physical X/Y face has anchor sum four. Consequently the **finite-q center**, as an analytic function of z, has linear imaginary coefficient

\[
 c_S(q)=\frac{q^4(1+q)^2(1+q^2)}{32p(q)},
 \qquad c_S(1)=1/84.
 \tag{AI1-F10}
\]

This coefficient is not constant in q. The executable independently reconstructs the cube graph, all sixteen six-cycles, the two prescribed physical paths, and each face flip before evaluating it. Thus preserving lambda while changing q preserves the surrogate but need not preserve even the finite-q center. The checker provides exact rational examples. At fixed q and z the center is independent of eta, but the actual connected stationary correlation also involves the eta-dependent state and the full unaveraged dynamics.

AD2 gives an **enclosure**, not equality:

\[
 |e^{iE_jT_q/\hbar}C_{j,q}(T_q)-h_{j,q}(z)|
 \le6J_j^2\widehat d_q+J_j^2 D_k(15z)+(1+J_j)b_k,
 \tag{AI1-F11}
\]

where `J_X=2`, `J_S=2sqrt(2)`, `tau_q=eta/[8b(q)]`, and `dhat_q` includes division by `(1-eta)/8`. Different centers do not prove distinguishability unless complete uncertainty disks separate. Equal centers do not prove identical actual models. Nor can an error bound be differentiated to infer a derivative error bound. These facts block promotion of the rational surrogate fibers to exact finite-q nonidentifiability. The present loop makes no such promotion.

## 5. Discriminating additional readout and remaining obligations

Use an independently calibrated undemodulated elementary Wilson carrier alongside the informative six-cycle readout. Under the surrogate and known q, this breaks alpha/eta compensation. If q is itself unknown, additionally measure or independently prescribe the profile: a calibrated ratio of neighboring magnetic coefficients with consecutive anchor exponents would be q. That coefficient ratio is a proposed new readout, not an observation already supplied by AD2.

For an actual experiment or model-identification theorem, prove finite-q carrier accuracy and signal separation, calibrate physical time/action/energy units, identify the prepared stationary state and the actual bounded multiplication operators, include preparation/readout uncertainty, and prove that the proposed coefficient measurement isolates the same canonical parameters. A homogeneous comparison then still requires an explicit state/domain/generator matching map with a bounded defect. Neither the limiting curve nor this surrogate identification supplies that map.
