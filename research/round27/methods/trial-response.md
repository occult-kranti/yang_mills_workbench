**What the curve identifies.** Let the recorded coordinate be \(u\), physical time be \(t=su\), and the declared response be \(y(u)=F(\kappa_{\rm phys}su)\). If the known response and sampling identify the composite parameter, the reported fit identifies only \(b=\kappa_{\rm phys}s=0.1\). Its exact clock-scale fiber is

\{(\kappa_{\rm phys},s):s>0,\ \kappa_{\rm phys}=0.1/s\}.

If time is independently calibrated, that particular ambiguity disappears. Other ambiguities depend on the actual \(F\), sample times and demodulation map; periodic sampling can add aliases. If \(F\) itself is unknown, \(\kappa\mapsto c\kappa\), \(F(x)\mapsto F(x/c)\) preserves the entire curve. We cannot claim the complete parameter fiber without those missing definitions. A demodulated envelope does not automatically identify its discarded carrier, the full generator, or an unobserved low-energy sector.

Any derivative of an approximate response must retain a separately justified derivative-error bound before it is attributed to the exact dynamics.
