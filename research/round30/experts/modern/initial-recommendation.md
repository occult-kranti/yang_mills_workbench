## Selected current research and future strategy

Spriggs, Greplova, Carrasquilla and Nys, arXiv:2509.12323v1, supply a concrete magnetic-basis variational direction for pure SU(2) in both 2+1 and 3+1 dimensions. Sections II.1–II.2 use the continuous group, explicit gauge symmetry and a Jastrow/equivariant ansatz; their Hamiltonian retains `lambda=4/g^4` after common energy rescaling. A future AS contract could compare a rigorously specified trial state with the complete uniform Hamiltonian and measure residuals. Numerical energy improvement is not a lower-gap theorem. The source's public code has been located but not installed or run here.

Rouxinol and collaborators, arXiv:2607.26132v1, distinguish independent electric/magnetic parameters from the physical coupling line in a 2+1-dimensional model with dynamical matter. That distinction reinforces the required model dictionary; its matter and dimension preclude automatic AQ transfer.

Barata, Frenklakh and Mukherjee, arXiv:2507.16890v2, use real-time correlators and regulated Fourier transforms for finite-temperature 1+1-dimensional U(1) spectroscopy. Their setup suggests an operational response question, but its finite-system, thermal, group and dimension assumptions differ from AQ. A peak in a broadened finite Fourier transform is not by itself an AQ spectral atom.

Nachtergaele–Sims, arXiv:1410.8174v1, Section 3 and Theorem 4.1, remain the actual unbounded-onsite dynamics input. The more general NSY paper was checked at metadata/abstract depth only in this pass. The topologies must stay separate: norm convergence with increasing volume for fixed bounded local observables does not supply operator-norm time differentiability on all local B(H).

The Clay problem, Section 4, requires a nontrivial four-dimensional continuum theory for every compact simple group with the required axioms and a positive physical gap. No fixed-spacing AQ conclusion completes those obligations.
