The proposed damaging centering control is simple enough to specify before production. Let

`K=H/alpha`, `K Omega=0`, `m=<Omega,W Omega>`, and `chi=(W-m)Omega`.

Here `alpha` is the fixed positive physical energy scale, `u=alpha t_E/hbar` is dimensionless Euclidean time, and `W`, `m`, and the correlation are dimensionless. `m_hat` is a numerical estimate, not a physical deformation. For a real Wilson observable set

`C(u)=<chi,exp(-u K)chi>`.

Two commonly confused estimators have different errors:

1. **Vector centering:** using `chi_hat=(W-m_hat)Omega` yields

   `C_vec(u)=C(u)+(m-m_hat)^2`.

   The unwanted component lies in the vacuum. This follows by decomposing `chi_hat=chi+(m-m_hat)Omega`; the cross terms vanish because `chi` is vacuum-orthogonal. Positive energy moments cannot see the extra zero-energy weight.

2. **Scalar subtraction:** using the raw correlation and subtracting `m_hat^2` yields

   `C_sub(u)=<W Omega,exp(-uK)W Omega>-m_hat^2=C(u)+m^2-m_hat^2`.

   If both means lie in `[-1,1]` and their difference is at most `eta`, the added constant has magnitude at most `2 eta`. If only the true mean is known to lie in that interval, use `2 eta+eta^2`. The offset can have either sign. A negative late-time estimate is therefore a useful countercontrol, not a datum to discard after inspection.

Over a finite dimensionless readout interval `[0,U]`, these constant offsets integrate to `U(m-m_hat)^2` and `U(m^2-m_hat^2)`, respectively. Any nonzero constant defeats an unprojected infinite-time integral. The reduced inverse form projects off the vacuum and is a distinct operation. These identities are proposed test specifications, not an admitted new theorem or novelty claim. Their spectral-projection content is established mathematics, and AT1 already notes that centering changes the zero atom while leaving positive moments unchanged.

If the approximate mean comes from another finite-volume state, the identities do not magically compare the two states. First keep the exact identities within each state, then pay the state and dynamics comparison errors. In particular, solver accuracy alone cannot certify the AQ interpretation.
