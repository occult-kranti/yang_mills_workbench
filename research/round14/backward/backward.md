\[

## 2. Independent character oracle

Let chi_r be the SU(2) irreducible character of highest weight r, dimension r+1. Thus chi_0=1, chi_1=2x and chi_1 chi_r=chi_(r+1)+chi_(r−1), omitting chi_(-1). Repeatedly applying this tensor-product recurrence gives

\[
 x^a=\sum_r A_{ar}\chi_r(U),\qquad
 A_{a,a-2j}=2^{-a}\left[\binom aj-\binom a{j-1}\right],
 \quad 0\le j\le\lfloor a/2\rfloor.
\]

All other A entries vanish. The binomial with j=−1 is zero. The multiplicities are ballot-number differences, obtainable directly by induction with the displayed recurrence.

For normalized Haar measure Schur orthogonality gives the character convolution

\[
 \int\chi_r(U)\chi_t(UV)dU
 =\delta_{rt}\frac{\chi_t(V)}{t+1}.
\]

For clarity about the inverse: substitute U→U^(-1) and use the real SU(2) characters chi_r(U^(-1))=chi_r(U), then apply matrix-coefficient orthogonality to chi_t(U^(-1)V). Integrating the remaining chi_s(V) gives

\[
 \boxed{\int x^a y^b z^c\,dU\,dV
 =\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{A_{ar}A_{br}A_{cr}}{r+1}.}
\]

This is a finite rational sum. In particular E[xyz]=1/16, not 1/8; the latter results from omitting the representation dimension. All three exponents must have the same parity for a nonzero answer. Permutation symmetry of this formula is an integration identity, not a claim that the three coordinates are independent.

## 3. Independent quaternion comparison

Write U=x I+i sqrt(1−x²) n·sigma and V=y I+i sqrt(1−y²) m·sigma. For independent Haar holonomies, x and y have density (2/pi)sqrt(1−x²), and t=n·m is uniform on [−1,1], independently of x,y. Then z=xy−sqrt(1−x²)sqrt(1−y²)t. Odd powers of t average to zero, while E[t^(2j)]=1/(2j+1). Therefore

\[
 E[x^a y^b z^c]=\sum_{\substack{0\le j\le c\\j\ \mathrm{even}}}\frac{\binom cj}{j+1}
 H(a+c-j,j/2)H(b+c-j,j/2),
\]

where H(p,l)=E[x^p(1−x²)^l] and

\[
 H(p,l)=\sum_{q=0}^l(-1)^q\binom lq m_{p+2q},\qquad
 m_{2q}=\frac{\binom{2q}q}{(q+1)4^q},\quad m_{2q+1}=0.
\]

The angle formula and character formula agree exactly on all 1,000 triples 0≤a,b,c≤9. This finite check is independent arithmetic verification of the implementation; the displayed derivations establish the general identity. The implementation has an explicit degree limit of 256, which is not a mathematical restriction of the identity. ## 4. Exact exceptional family, meeting the existing one-loop result

When k1=k2=0, change variables from (U,V) to (U,W=UV). Haar invariance makes U and W independent under dU dW, and the tilt depends only on w=Tr(W)/2. Both U and V retain their Haar marginals. Conditional on W, the isotropic four-component unit quaternion U has second moments delta_ab/4. Consequently

\[
 E[x]=E[y]=0,\quad E[x^2]=E[y^2]=\tfrac14,\quad
 E[xy\mid W]=\tfrac14 w,

Here u is exactly the tilted-Haar mean already bounded in round13. It is odd, u(0)=0 and u'(eta)=Var_eta(w)>0 for every finite eta because the measure is positive on an interval. ## 5. A local deformation theorem for arbitrary finite k1,k2

The exponential-family identities are derivatives with respect to coupling. In particular C=partial_(k1)partial_(k2) log Z and partial_eta C is the third joint cumulant of x,y,z. At eta=0 the two tilted holonomies are independent, while E[z|x,y]=xy. Substituting all five terms of that third cumulant yields

\[
 \boxed{\partial_\eta C(k_1,k_2,0)=v(k_1)v(k_2)>0},
\]

where v(k)=Var_k(x). Using only E[xyz] would omit mean-subtraction terms and is wrong away from the Haar origin.

Every variable x,y,z is bounded in absolute value by one. The fourth cumulant has partition weights whose absolute sum is 1+7+12+6=26, so |partial_eta² C|≤26 at every finite real triple. Taylor's theorem along real eta, with C(k1,k2,0)=0, gives the explicit bound

\[
 |C(k_1,k_2,\eta)-\eta v(k_1)v(k_2)|\le13\eta^2.
 \tag{B1}
\]

Hence **0<|eta|<v(k1)v(k2)/13 implies sign(C)=sign(eta)**. A certified positive lower bound for the baseline variance product suffices. This is a conservative local-in-deformation theorem; it does not assert the same sign at all eta and arbitrary nonzero k1,k2. As an elementary existence bound, the tilted density relative to Haar is at least exp(−2|k|), so minimizing E[(x−c)²] over c gives v(k)≥exp(−2|k|)/4. Rational variance certificates can be sharper and avoid a new exponential enclosure.

**Feedback improvement from the forward member, independently accepted by backward and advisor:** use centered variables dx=x−E[x], etc. The exact fourth-cumulant identity is

\[
 C''=E[dx\,dy\,dz^2]-\operatorname{Cov}(x,y)\operatorname{Var}(z)
       -2\operatorname{Cov}(x,z)\operatorname{Cov}(y,z).
\]

Here |dz|≤2, E|dx dy|≤sqrt(Var(x)Var(y))≤1, and every variance/covariance has magnitude at most one. The three terms are bounded by 4,1,2 respectively. Thus the stronger, still fully uniform estimate is

\[
 \boxed{|C''|\le7,\qquad
 |C-\eta v(k_1)v(k_2)|\le\tfrac72\eta^2.}
 \tag{B1-improved}
\]

The accepted sign range becomes 0<|eta|<2v(k1)v(k2)/7. The earlier constant 26 remains correct; this feedback tightens a sufficient bound without changing the action or introducing a fitted constant. The global sign outside that range remains a separate question.

## 6. Loop 2 rational exponential-remainder certificate specification

Let S=k1 x+k2 y+eta z, M=|k1|+|k2|+|eta| and N a nonnegative integer. M is an envelope, N an approximation order; neither is a new physical field. For N+2>M, the absolute exponential tail is bounded everywhere on the true support by the rational number

\[
 R_N=\frac{M^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}\frac1{1-M/(N+2)}.
 \tag{B2}
\]

The ratio of successive nonnegative tail terms is at most M/(N+2). Thus B2 bounds the sum after degree N; it is not an asymptotic error estimate. The M=0 branch has zero remainder. The equality N+2=M and larger M require increasing N or using a separately proved bound, never dividing by a nonpositive denominator.

For a monomial O=x^p y^q z^r bounded by one, compute exactly

\[
 T_N(O)=\sum_{a+b+c\le N}\frac{k_1^a k_2^b\eta^c}{a!b!c!}
 E_0[x^{a+p}y^{b+q}z^{c+r}].
\]

Then A_O=E_0[O exp(S)] lies in [T_N(O)−R_N,T_N(O)+R_N]. Signed couplings are kept with their signs in T_N and only absolutized in M. Z=A_1≥1 by Jensen, since E_0[S]=0. Intersect its enclosure with [1,infinity); reject an empty interval. No floating exp, eigenvalue or numerical quadrature is a sign premise.

## 7. Backward obligations for a physical mass gap

The Clay target concerns a nontrivial theory on R^4 for compact simple gauge groups, with specified quantum-field axioms and a positive gap in the physical Hamiltonian spectrum. An SU(2) result would address that particular group, not automatically every group. The original problem statement also requires the short-distance behavior to match the gauge theory. [Jaffe–Witten, Sections 3–4](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf).

One sufficient route is to construct a compatible family of gauge-invariant continuum Euclidean correlation distributions with the required regularity, Euclidean symmetry, reflection positivity, clustering and nontriviality, together with the correct action/renormalization and local-field identification. Reconstruction must then yield the physical Hilbert space, a unique vacuum Omega and positive self-adjoint time generator H. These properties are not consequences of an arbitrary positive finite measure.

After such a construction, a precise sufficient spectral criterion is: there exists one Delta>0 such that, for a dense family of vectors psi orthogonal to Omega, each has a finite C_psi with

\[
 0\le\langle\psi,e^{-tH}\psi\rangle\le C_\psi e^{-\Delta t}
 \quad\hbox{for every }t\ge0.
 \tag{B3}
\]

The spectral theorem proves the implication. If the spectral measure of one such psi had positive weight below Delta, some closed subinterval [0,Delta−epsilon] would carry positive weight; its Laplace contribution would eventually exceed B3. Hence the low-energy spectral projection kills a dense family and is zero. The common Delta and all-time statement matter; one covariance at one separation cannot provide them. Nontrivial physical excitation space must also survive the construction.

Round13's reviewed Yarotsky application already supplies a different genuine result: a gapped fixed-spacing static SU(2) Hamiltonian in an existentially small magnetic/electric regime, uniformly over the specified spatial volumes, with a compatible gapped vacuum representation. Its blocked onsite spaces may be infinite-dimensional and its electric terms unbounded. It does not evaluate the coupling threshold. [Yarotsky, theorem source](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0411042). The workbench dictionary lambda/alpha=4/g_H^4 shows that the usual g_H→0 continuum trajectory exits this small-ratio regime. Required Lorentz/continuum/renormalized-field premises are still absent.

An exact countermodel isolates the missing dynamics. On L²(mu), keep the same positive finite measure, multiplication observables and Omega=1. On any complete orthonormal family e_n in the orthogonal complement of constants, define H Omega=0 and H e_n=(1/n)e_n. This bounded self-adjoint positive generator has a unique vacuum and no positive gap. Every static moment from mu is unchanged. It is not a Yang–Mills generator, which is precisely why matching the generator is a necessary premise. A declaration of a new mass constant or a chosen time scale cannot remove this counterexample.
