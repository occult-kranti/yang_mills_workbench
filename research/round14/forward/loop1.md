# Loop 1 forward result: a declared interacting compact measure

The first loop connects the previous one-plaquette results to an exactly defined two-loop experiment. It derives an exact one-dimensional reduction, a positive susceptibility at the undeformed baseline, and a special coupled family that reduces to the existing certified one-loop mean. Numerical comparisons test the implementation. The remaining numerical-certification problem is passed to the second loop; it is not supplied by these floating comparisons.

## 1. Physical contract and the exception being tested

Let normalized Haar measure on each copy of SU(2) have total mass one. Set

\[
x=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}U,\qquad
y=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}V,\qquad
z=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}(UV).
\]

For finite real parameters define the compact Euclidean probability measure

\[
d\mu_{\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\eta}
=Z^{-1}\exp(\kappa_1x+\kappa_2y+\eta z)\,dU\,dV.
\tag{1}
\]

All traces are normalized as written; no lattice spacing, physical time, Hamiltonian energy or continuum scale is implicit in these parameters. The finite action is minus the exponent, up to an irrelevant additive constant. The bounded mixed-loop coupling \(\eta\) is an explicit deformation of that action. It is not generated by calling a shared-link Hamiltonian kinetic term a Euclidean interaction.

At \(\eta=0\), (1) is the factorized holonomy measure obtained from two ordinary adjacent open planar Wilson squares after tree gauge fixing. That factorization is exact for this baseline. The present nonfactorized experiment changes the finite action by adding the larger based-loop observable \(z\). Any use in a four-dimensional pure Yang–Mills construction would require a separate geometric and continuum return argument.

Write SU(2) matrices as unit quaternions \(U=(x,\mathbf a)\), \(V=(y,\mathbf b)\), with \(|\mathbf a|^2=1-x^2\). Then

\[
z=xy-\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b,
\qquad
(z-xy)^2\le(1-x^2)(1-y^2).
\tag{2}
\]

The trace identity gives \(2xy=z+\frac12\operatorname{Tr}(UV^\dagger)\). This is an algebraic reduction, not a statement about expectation factorization. Finite-N bootstrap literature uses these trace identities while retaining loop geometry and positivity constraints; it does not eliminate an infinite loop-length hierarchy merely by eliminating the number of traces. [Kazakov and Zheng, finite-N lattice bootstrap, introductory identities and hierarchy](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16925v4).

## 2. Exact integration of the second holonomy

For fixed \(U\), the \(V\)-dependent exponent is

\[
\kappa_2y+\eta z=(\kappa_2+\eta x)y-\eta\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b
=\mathbf c\cdot V,
\quad
\mathbf c=(\kappa_2+\eta x,-\eta\mathbf a).
\]

Rotational invariance of the Haar three-sphere gives

\[
h=|\mathbf c|=\sqrt{\kappa_2^2+\eta^2+2\kappa_2\eta x},
\qquad
Z_0(h)=\int e^{\mathbf c\cdot V}dV=\frac{2I_1(h)}h,
\quad Z_0(0)=1.
\tag{3}
\]

The Haar marginal is \(w(x)=(2/\pi)\sqrt{1-x^2}\). Consequently

\[
Z=\int_{-1}^{1}w(x)e^{\kappa_1x}Z_0(h(x))dx,
\quad
d\mu_x=Z^{-1}w(x)e^{\kappa_1x}Z_0(h(x))dx.
\tag{4}
\]

Equation (3) follows by substituting \(\nu=1\) in the Bessel integral representation. Its derivative relations give \(u(h)=(\log Z_0)'=I_2(h)/I_1(h)\). [NIST DLMF integral representation](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.32.E2), [Bessel recurrences and derivatives](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.29).

Define

\[
t(h)=\frac{u(h)}h=\frac{I_2(h)}{hI_1(h)},
\qquad
r(h)=\frac{1-4t(h)}{h^2}
=\frac{I_3(h)}{h^2I_1(h)}.
\tag{5}
\]

The second form of \(r\) uses \(I_1-I_3=4I_2/h\) and avoids subtracting two nearly equal terms. The exact removable limits are \(t(0)=1/4\), \(r(0)=1/24\). Rotational invariance and differentiating \(Z_0\) give the vector moments

\[
E[V_i\mid U]=t c_i,
\qquad E[V_iV_j\mid U]=t\delta_{ij}+r c_ic_j.
\tag{6}
\]

For a direct check, the trace of the second equation is \(4t+h^2r=1\), as required by \(|V|=1\). Let

\[
A=\kappa_2+\eta x,\qquad B=\kappa_2x+\eta.
\]

Applying (6) to the two linear functionals defining \(y\) and \(z\) yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
E[y\mid x]&=tA,&E[z\mid x]&=tB,\\
E[y^2\mid x]&=t+rA^2,&E[z^2\mid x]&=t+rB^2,\\
E[yz\mid x]&=tx+rAB.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

The full first and second moments of \((x,y,z)\) now require only the one-dimensional integral (4). At \(x=\pm1\), the constraint is \(z=xy\); (7) satisfies it without dividing by a vanishing relative-angle sine. If \(x=1,\kappa_2=-\eta\), or \(x=-1,\kappa_2=\eta\), then \(h=0\), the conditional \(V\) measure is Haar, and (7) remains regular.

## 3. The shared response variable and a rigorous local sign result

For any fixed bounded observable \(F\), differentiating the normalized compact family gives

\[
\partial_\eta E_\eta[F]=\operatorname{Cov}_\eta(F,z).
\tag{8}
\]

Differentiation under the integral is justified on every bounded parameter interval by compactness and a uniform exponential majorant. All covariance and higher cumulant derivatives here are coupling responses, not physical-time dynamics.

Let \(u_i=E_{\kappa_i}[x_i]\), \(v_i=\operatorname{Var}_{\kappa_i}(x_i)\) in the one-loop factors. At \(\eta=0\), the angular average of \(z\) conditional on \((x,y)\) is \(xy\). Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_\eta E[xy]\big|_0&=E[x^2]E[y^2]-u_1^2u_2^2,\\
\partial_\eta E[x]\big|_0&=v_1u_2,\qquad
\partial_\eta E[y]\big|_0=v_2u_1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Subtracting the two differentiated mean products gives the exact identity

\[
C(0)=0,\qquad C'(0)=v_1v_2>0,
\quad C(\eta)=\operatorname{Cov}_\eta(x,y).
\tag{9}
\]

The strict inequality holds at every finite real \(\kappa_1,\kappa_2\): each tilted Haar density remains positive on the open interval, so neither factor is a point mass. Dropping the mean derivatives in this calculation gives the wrong susceptibility.

The backward researcher initially bounded the uncentered fourth-cumulant expansion by 26, giving a valid but conservative Taylor error \(13\eta^2\). The forward review sharpened this bound without changing assumptions. Let \(\delta x=x-E[x]\), and similarly for \(y,z\). The centered fourth-cumulant identity is

\[
C''(\eta)=E[\delta x\delta y(\delta z)^2]
-\operatorname{Cov}(x,y)\operatorname{Var}(z)
-2\operatorname{Cov}(x,z)\operatorname{Cov}(y,z).
\tag{10}
\]

Since \(|x|,|y|,|z|\le1\), all variances and covariance magnitudes are at most one. Also \(|\delta z|\le2\), and Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(E|\delta x\delta y|\le\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(x)\operatorname{Var}(y)}\le1\). Therefore

\[
|C''(\eta)|\le4+1+2=7,
\qquad
|C(\eta)-\eta v_1v_2|\le\tfrac72\eta^2.
\tag{11}
\]

This holds along the entire real coupling line for fixed finite \(\kappa_1,\kappa_2\), though the bound becomes uninformative away from zero. It gives a rigorous local sign exception:

\[
0<|\eta|<\frac{2v_1v_2}{7}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{sign}C(\eta)=\operatorname{sign}\eta.
\tag{12}
\]

To certify a particular numerical parameter, replace \(v_i\) by available exact rational positive lower bounds before evaluating the threshold. This loop establishes the conventional inequality and floating diagnostics; it does not yet publish the required joint rational certificate. The advisor and backward member independently checked (10)–(12).

## 4. An exact coupled family with unchanged Haar marginals

When \(\kappa_1=\kappa_2=0\), set \(W=UV\). The map preserves product Haar measure, so

\[
d\mu_{0,0,\eta}=dU\,Z_0(\eta)^{-1}e^{\eta\operatorname{Tr}W/2}dW.
\]

Here \(U\) is independent Haar and \(W\) is the tilted one-loop variable. Although \(V=U^{-1}W\), its marginal is also Haar. Conditional Haar second moments give

\[
E[x]=E[y]=0,\quad E[x^2]=E[y^2]=\tfrac14,
\quad E[xy\mid W]=\tfrac14\,\operatorname{Tr}W/2.
\]

Consequently the complete first/second-moment family is

\[
\begin{aligned}
E[z]&=u(\eta),&E[z^2]&=m_2(\eta),\\
\operatorname{Cov}(x,y)&=u(\eta)/4,&E[xz]&=E[yz]=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

The derivative at zero is \(1/16\), consistent with (9). For positive \(\eta\), the covariance is positive; for negative \(\eta\), it changes sign by one-loop parity. For every finite \(\eta\), its magnitude is strictly less than \(1/4\); the extremal value is only approached as \(|\eta|\to\infty\). Identical Haar marginals therefore do not imply independence. Any previously verified exact enclosure \(u(\eta)\in[L,U]\) transfers immediately to \(C(\eta)\in[L/4,U/4]\), provided the parameter and source certificate are bound correctly.

## 5. Numerical implementation and independent comparison

`forward_model.py` implements the reduced integral using Gauss–Jacobi quadrature with its exact Haar weight parameters \(\alpha=\beta=1/2\). Scaled Bessels remove exponential overflow. For small \(h\), positive power series evaluate \(t,r\), and `log1p` evaluates the logarithmic normalization. The implementation uses the norm form

\[
h=\operatorname{hypot}(A,\eta\sqrt{(1-x)(1+x)})
\]

and endpoint-centered sums for \(A,B\). This avoids the artificial negative radicand produced by subtracting large nearly equal terms in the expanded square. [SciPy scaled-Bessel API](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.special.ive.html), [Gauss–Jacobi API](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.special.roots_jacobi.html).

The separate direct integrator never calls the Bessel or conditional-moment functions. It computes the original integral in three coordinates:

\[
x=\cos\theta,\quad y=\cos\phi,\quad
z=\cos\theta\cos\phi-\sin\theta\sin\phi\,c,
\]

with \(\theta,\phi\in[0,\pi]\), sine-squared Haar weights, and \(c\) uniform on \([-1,1]\). All three axes use Legendre quadrature. Independent complex 2×2 matrix fixtures check the trace normalization and the relative-orientation term before the integrals are compared.

The public two-loop numerical API accepts real finite \(|\kappa_1|,|\kappa_2|,|\eta|\le100\), including signed and zero values; it rejects Boolean substitutions and nonfinite or out-of-domain input. The reduced node count is an integer from 4 to 512; the direct count is from 4 to 128 per axis. The derived conditional field can reach 200, so its radial helper accepts that range. These are explicit implementation limits, not limits of the exact formulas. A requested node count does not itself guarantee a desired error.

The delivered run has **196 producer gates**, repeated successfully under optimized Python without recounting the repeat as new checks. Across 17 selected fixtures, the 128-node reduced result and 96-node-per-axis direct result differ in the nine first/second moments by at most **1.7763568394002505×10⁻¹⁵**. Their agreement is a numerical diagnostic, not a rigorous error interval or independent theorem certification.

The checks cover the factorized baseline, signed couplings, center transformations, loop exchange, removable zero fields, near-central cancellation, the exact special family, covariance-matrix positivity as a diagnostic, invalid inputs, and centered-difference refinement of (9). Source bytes are bound to results and checked again after execution.

The saved quadrature refinement includes deliberately inadequate grids. At \((100,100,100)\), an 8-node reduced calculation misses the 128-node reference by about **0.09716** in the moment collection. At \((100,100,-100)\), 12 nodes per direct axis miss by about **0.09003**. These fail the declared \(5\times10^{-12}\) comparison target and are retained. Refinement brings these fixtures to the floating comparison floor; the coarse results are not erased.

At \((\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\eta)=(1,2,1)\), the computed covariance is **0.0309657346637432**, distinguishable from the false-factorization value zero. In the exact \((0,0,5)\) family it is approximately **0.179835145341079** even though both marginals remain Haar. By contrast, every tested \(\eta=0\) baseline factorizes correctly. An orientation swap of the second holonomy preserves Haar and the corresponding class-function distribution; it must not be mislabelled as an error simply because its coordinate dot-product sign changes.

One real implementation defect was found: a reference helper originally rejected the derived conditional argument 199 even though the declared inputs \(x=-1,\kappa_2=100,\eta=-99\) are supported. The helper now accepts fields up to 200; the special-family input remains limited to 100. The original source and the exact failing case are retained in `history/`. No historical passing result is used as evidence for the corrected bytes.

## 6. Loop-1 feedback and the second-loop acceptance question

Forward and backward formulations meet at an exact, normalized compact measure and a bounded observable. They do not meet at a Hamiltonian vacuum or at a continuum reconstruction theorem. The most useful second-loop target is to remove floating integration from selected covariance claims.

The backward member independently derived a rational Haar-moment oracle using SU(2) character coefficients. For rational couplings let \(S=\kappa_1x+\kappa_2y+\eta z\) and \(M=|\kappa_1|+|\kappa_2|+|\eta|\). For integer \(N\ge0\), \(N+2>M\), the exponential remainder obeys

\[
\left|e^S-\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{S^n}{n!}\right|
\le R_N:=\frac{M^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}\frac1{1-M/(N+2)}.
\tag{14}
\]

Every successive absolute term in the tail has ratio at most \(M/(N+2)\), proving the geometric bound. This handles signed \(S\) without a floating exponential oracle. Product Haar has \(E_0[S]=0\), so Jensen gives the independent lower bound \(Z\ge1\). For \(|F|\le1\), the exact rational polynomial numerator has error at most \(R_N\).

The covariance should be bounded as

\[
C=\frac{ZA_{xy}-A_xA_y}{Z^2},\qquad
A_F=E_0[Fe^S],
\tag{15}
\]

retaining numerator cancellation and a positive denominator enclosure before dividing. The concrete second-loop gate is an exact rational certificate with a replayable tail bound and parameter/source binding, challenged against the independent numerical formulation here and deliberate coefficient/sign mutations. The existing one-loop certificates also offer a simpler exact test through (13). The advisor selected the point \((1,1,1/4)\), already included in the numerical comparison, because the generic local Taylor sign bound is inconclusive there. That failure of a sufficient bound does not show a vanishing covariance. At \((1,1,\pm1/1024)\), the existing positive one-loop variance bounds can instead make the local sign test explicit. Publishing these exact enclosures is the next loop's work, not an accomplished numerical enclosure in this handoff.

The unresolved Yang–Mills obligations remain distinct: embedding into the intended interacting lattice action, a controlled deformation removal or exact equivalence, growing volume, continuum scale matching, reconstruction axioms and a nonzero physical spectral threshold. No finite compact partition function or covariance certificate supplies them by itself.
