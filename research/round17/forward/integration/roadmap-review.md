# Author review of the proposed next roadmap

This is a bounded planning/copy review after C2, not an additional scientific loop or independent acceptance of my own results. I read the three proposed goals in Round17's next-roadmap.md. Their scope is compatible with the accepted finite results, and no completed claim is promoted to a dense uniform or continuum theorem.

Two implementation premises should be explicit before the next contracts are frozen:

1. For Goal 2, choose the declared finite projector from a complete set of relevant free electric eigenspaces, or explicitly account for a nonzero electric cross block. If P reduces H0, then PH0Q=0 and the bounded interaction supplies the cross-block norm. For an arbitrary optimized trial space, this cancellation is not automatic. To claim a complement electric threshold, every physical free state below that threshold must be covered; sampling some low states or counting only elementary face states without a complete low-energy argument is insufficient. The existing min-max/Schur-complement and eigenvalue-isolation obligations are appropriate.

2. For Goal 3, a joint Gram record must be realizable: positive semidefinite, rank at most four, each a_i of unit length, and compatible with b=sum kappa_i a_i. The common-Gram isometry statement is mathematically sound for the stated scalar-product observable class, including deficient rank and b=0. An O(4) change of central integration coordinates is not automatically a gauge transformation or a permissible change of every surrounding link. The roadmap already retains this surrounding-measure limitation; these explicit algebraic constraints should accompany a future Gram-based cache.

Goal 1 correctly retains the original dense threshold and identifies the sparse exception as an input, not completion. The pair-plus-bridge calculation can remain a finite local estimate while its use in an applicable rotor stability theorem stays an independent obligation. The volume and energy-scale tests are necessary.

C2's feedback strengthens the case for Goal 3: b and the common partition alone provably omit observable-direction information, while the Gram proposal targets exactly that missing information. No next-goal proof or numerical experiment was executed during this review.
