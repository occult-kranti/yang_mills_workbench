## 1. What sub-round 5 established, in lens terms

**Feynman: the finite graph's exact coefficients.** AZ2 fixes a genuinely different finite model — the Round11 two-plaquette graph, `H_FG = K - tau_FG(W_1+W_2)`, both faces coupled equally — and computes its Rayleigh–Schrödinger series to fifth order as exact rationals, never fitted, with zero truncation error at either cutoff (`D` in {6, 8}, dimensions 84 and 165, because `psi_1=(W_1+W_2)/3` and the low-order coefficients live entirely inside `P_6`). The first-order coefficient is `1/6`; the second-order coefficient of `<W_1>` is **exactly zero**, by a shared-link centre flip (`S K S = K`, `S G S = G`, `S \cdot(x{+}y)\cdot S = -(x{+}y)` on the Round11 pencils) that is the finite-graph analogue — same mechanism, same "every first-order-in-the-square-of-the-coupling term vanishes by a central sign flip" shape — of the Z^3 parity theorem AW1 proved for `omega(W^2)`, `C(s)` and `c(theta)`. The two are not the same theorem: AW1's flip is `U_E`, a central `-1` on a link set meeting every plaquette oddly on an infinite lattice; AZ2's is a single-link centre flip on one shared link of a seven-link graph. Both are instances of the same principle (an odd number of `j=1/2` factors under an SU(2) centre grading gives a vanishing first-order term), read independently on two unrelated finite structures, which is exactly why the coincidence is worth recording and exactly why it proves nothing about the other model. The third order is where the graph's own geometry, not the shared normalization, takes over: `a_3=-187/33696` for the two-face model against `-5/864` for the one-face model (second square uncoupled), a certified difference of `tau_FG^3/4212 + O(tau_FG^5)` at every grid point, sign `sign(tau_FG)`. So the model identification — which finite graph was actually computed — is invisible at first and second order (both models agree on `1/6` and `0`) and becomes exact and checkable only at third order. This is not a flaw in the contract's discipline; it is the honest content of "a different finite model": two models can share every low-order feature of a shared normalization convention and still be different systems, distinguishable only once the expansion reaches the order where each model's own interaction structure first acts. The one-face model is not admitted — it is the skeptic's disclosed alternative reading, kept on record together with the certified shift between the two — and both models still carry the same unconditional label as AV1, AW1, AW2, AX1, AX2, AY1 and AY2 before them: **nothing here transfers to the AQ construction, its subsequential limits, `K_2`, the Z^3 shift, or roadmap goal 2.** The finite graph is consistent with the matched Z^3 coefficient (`1/6 <-> 1/144` under `tau_FG=tau/24`, an algebraic identity of two first-order formulas sharing the energy denominator 3 and `E[W^2]=1/4`, as the skeptic's contract review derived independently of any producer) and nothing more.

**Penrose: the continuum statement's lattice-unit floor and the exact reason the strong-coupling family cannot reach a finite-mass limit.** AZ1 states, and the skeptic's independent pre-comparison replay confirms bit for bit, the one uniform estimate the fixed-spacing family supplies: the volume-uniform gap `alpha/16=g^2/(32a)` at fixed `a`, for `g^4>=9.6x10^9` (AX1's route-B contraction), giving a directed lattice-units floor `a*Delta>=1250 sqrt6 ~ 3061.86` throughout the admitted regime — a floor on a dimensionless product of the lattice spacing and the gap, not a floor on any physical energy, since no scale is set. The exact reason the family cannot reach a finite-mass limit along any trajectory `(a_n,g_n)` with `g_n->0` is not that the gap "gets small" or "goes to zero" in some loose sense; it is that **the admitted regime itself is a closed, finite-index-bounded set along any such trajectory** — the AM2 contraction requires `g^4>=9.6x10^9` and the weaker AL1 bridge condition requires `g^4>=32`, and both fail from a finite index on for every sequence with `g_n->0` (the toy trajectory `g_n=g_0/n` crosses the cap at `n*=2` and the bridge at `n*=132` for `g_0^4=9.6x10^9`; at `n*=4` and `n*=421` for the illustrative `g_0=1000`). Inside the admitted regime, a finite physical gap along `a_n->0` would need `a_n*Delta_n->0`, which is impossible there because `a*Delta>=1250 sqrt6` is a *lower* bound that holds throughout the regime and does not shrink with `a`. So this is failure of a sufficient certificate to extend past a finite index, not a statement that the gap closes, vanishes, or that the continuum limit fails to exist by any argument given here — and AZ1's gate says exactly that, with `continuum_claim`, `uniform_in_a_claimed` and `weak_coupling_claim` all `false`.
