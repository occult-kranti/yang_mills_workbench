## 3. Link-flip antisymmetry: sound, under stated conditions
**Combinatorics.** In each orientation, exactly one link of one parallel pair lies in E, and the other parallel pair is both in or both out, so |f∩E| is 1 or 3. My scratch run found 0 even plaquettes among 20,577 (10,830 with 1 link in E, 9,747 with 3), and deleting one link from E makes 4 plaquettes even. E is not a Z_2 coboundary (its coboundary is 1 on every plaquette), so U_E is not a gauge transformation and genuinely flips gauge-invariant observables.

**Operators.** -1 is central, so U_E commutes with every E_l^a, Casimir, endpoint gauge action (hence the Gauss constraints) and on-site cutoff projection (U_E is diagonal, (-1)^{2j}), and it fixes Omega_0 and P_R. The exact identity is U_E H_{N,n}(tau,kappa) U_E* = H_{N,n}(-tau,-kappa). This gives antisymmetry in tau only when kappa=0, or when all faces scale with tau (route B uniform). It holds for AQ1's open boxes, where links are factor-owned and none is frozen. It fails, or needs a cohomologous E', for odd periodic sides, frozen or gauge-fixed links, and any j=1 or W_f^2 term.

**AQ limit.** A unique ground state gives omega_{N,-tau}=omega_{N,tau}∘alpha_E for every N and n. AQ1's diagonal subsequence depends on tau, so the limit statement is set-level, S(-tau)=S(tau)∘alpha_E, and holds pointwise only along a common subsequence; the chosen -tau state must be defined that way. The Nachtergaele–Sims dynamics is intertwined, so C(s) and omega(W^2) are even in tau for paired states.

**Overclaim.** Oddness removes the tau^2 coefficient in finite volume, but it does not turn an O(tau^2) bound into O(tau^3): r(tau)=K tau|tau| is odd. The grading kills <c^(2)_R, W Omega_R> exactly, but the third-order, straddling, pair and density terms remain absolute bounds. So "effective K_2~5" is not a consequence and AW2 must not use it; the honest K_2 of 1.1-1.9e4 already gives a margin of 36-63.

**AW1 must prove and control:** (a) the lemma, with an enumeration over the whole box family including selected classes and W, and the boundary convention stated; (b) the U_E parity of every term of H, including the cutoff compressions; (c) ground covariance at every (N,n); (d) common-subsequence passage; (e) GNS and dynamics covariance for C(s); (f) controls: kappa≠0 must break the identity, a j=1 term must not flip, odd periodic boxes, E minus one link and mixed subsequences must be rejected, plus an exact finite fixture, labelled as such, computing both signs separately; (g) no priority claim (this is a known kind of center-flip argument), with the -tau AW2 enclosure counted as an image of the +tau one.
