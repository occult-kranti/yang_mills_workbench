## 2. Assessment of the advisor's candidate directions

### 2.1 Boundary-influence decay (roadmap goals 1 and 3 together)

The brief proposes that the AM2 contraction, read as controlling how a boundary change propagates through chains of overlapping supports, could give a Cauchy-in-N estimate for the R-marginal. Reading AM2 §2–3 and AV1's admitted method side by side, this looks concretely derivable, but not by the route the brief's own phrasing suggests, and with one genuine open step.

**What AM2 actually supplies.** The multilinear estimate (HNM-AM2.5), `||L_k(c_1,...,c_k)||_a <= J L_k^num prod||c_j||_a` with `L_k^num=16*8^k(1+5k/4)`, and its all-order sum `G(t)=16e^{8t}(1+10t)` are a **global** bound on the total anchored norm of the fixed-point coefficient collection — they show the Picard map sends the ball of radius R=1/64 into itself (`J_0 G(R) < 148/25000000 < R`) and is a contraction there (`2 J_0 G'(R) < 77/390625 < 1`). Neither inequality, as stated, tracks how far a given creation support reaches from a fixed reference region; both are per-site maxima over *all* creations of *any* size, summed without regard to distance. So the brief's phrase "each chain step costing a factor of order J G(R) < 1/64" needs a correction before any contract freezes: `J_0 G(R) ≈ 5.92e-6` is the quantity the *self-map* inequality bounds, and it is already far smaller than R=1/64 (the ball radius itself, not a per-step ratio); the quantity that actually behaves like a per-application shrinkage factor in the Banach argument is the Lipschitz constant `J G'(R)`, bounded by half of `2 J_0 G'(R) < 77/390625 ≈ 1.97e-4`. Neither of these is yet a *distance*-indexed decay factor — both are size/order-indexed. Getting a genuine boundary-shell decay requires a new estimate, not a relabelling of an existing one.

**What is derivable, concretely.** AM2's own proof already uses, informally, that a creation with support `I` must meet the interaction support `X` it is nested under (`|X|<=4`, support diameter 2), and that `|I_j| <= |M|+p`. This licenses a genuine new lemma, not yet stated or proved anywhere in the admitted record: *any connected family of creation supports whose union meets both the fixed cover R and a coarse site at l1-distance `d` from R must have total creation order `k >= ceil(d/2)`* (each anchor star reaches at most 2 lattice units per application). Combined with the *already admitted* tail of `G(t)=sum L_k^num t^k/k!` — a legitimate, already-convergent series at `t=J_0<<R` — restricting the sum to `k>=ceil(d/2)` gives a tail `T(k)` that decays **faster than geometrically** in `d` (the `1/k!` in `L_k^num/k!` beats the `8^k` growth once `k` is bounded below by something growing with `d`). Re-applying AV1's own density-form inequality (`2eps(1+eps)/(1+eps^2)`) with this tail in place of the single "R-vacuum" split used once in AV1 gives exactly the shape of estimate the brief wants: `||rho_R^{(N+1)}-rho_R^{(N)}||_1` bounded by a quantity that shrinks with N, not merely stays below the AY1/AY2 constant 2D. **The per-step factor is therefore not J G(R); it is the k!-beating tail of the already-admitted G(t) series, indexed by the graph-distance-to-order lemma above, and the chain count (order needed to reach the current boundary) grows *linearly* in N — so the sum over all future shells converges very comfortably (faster than any single geometric series), with room to spare.**

**What could make it fail.** The one genuinely new, unproved step is the min-order-vs-distance lemma itself. AM2's connectivity argument (that fixed-point coefficients are supported on star-connected sets) is stated informally in AM2 §2's derivation of (HNM-AM2.5) and was never carried, in the admitted record, into a *quantitative distance* statement — the historical lens's own Round32 loop-1 memo flagged an analogous step (star-connectivity of the fixed point, "A1") as unproved and it was never in fact needed for AV1, because AV1 used the simpler one-shell split instead of the full polymer/distance machinery. If the star-graph adjacency used here (12 neighbours per coarse site, from a single anchor's `S={0,e_x,e_y,e_z}`) admits some shortcut — two different low-order creation supports jointly reaching a site farther away than the naive per-creation reach of 2 would suggest — the lemma fails as stated, and the chain argument does not close; the fallback is the existing, already-admitted N-independent 2D bound (AY1/AY2), which is a legitimate limited outcome, not a defect. A second, separate risk applies only to the "uniqueness" half of goal 1: comparing two *different exterior conditions at the same N* (needed for a genuine Dobrushin/HTW-type uniqueness statement) is not the same construction as comparing *two different N's* — clamping the exterior changes which Hamiltonian is diagonalized, not merely how many of its terms are summed — so even a fully successful shell-decay lemma for goal 3 (whole-sequence convergence) does not automatically hand over goal 1 (uniqueness) for free; that half needs its own split, is the harder half, and should be expected, on current information, to land as limited rather than accepted_within_scope in one loop.

### 2.2 Lieb–Robinson dynamics comparison (roadmap goal 2)

Reading AQ1 §3 (the Nachtergaele–Sims constants `F(r)=(1+r)^-4`, `C<=224`, `||Phi||_F<=2268|tau|`) against AY2's own itemized boundary count (28N(5N+1) extra faces at graph-distance N-1), this direction is, if anything, more immediately tractable than the state comparison, and for a specific quantitative reason worth naming: **the coupling is so small (`|tau|<=1e-8`) that the exponential prefactor in a standard NS-type commutator bound is essentially inert.** At T=1, `2*C*||Phi||_F*T <= 2*224*2268e-8 ≈ 0.01016`, so `e^{2C||Phi||_F T}-1 ≈ 0.0102` — the entire time-growth factor contributes barely more than one percent, not an exponential blow-up. The decay with N then comes almost entirely from `F(r)=(1+r)^-4` (a **polynomial**, not exponential, kernel — a known feature of Nachtergaele–Sims-type bounds for *unbounded* on-site terms, which the historical lens's own reading this loop confirms is still the state of the art for gauge-theory-shaped unbounded on-site operators; see Section 5, H33-3) applied to `O(N^2)` boundary faces (`28N(5N+1)`) each at distance `N-1`: `O(N^2) * O(N^-4) = O(N^-2)`, exactly the shape the roadmap itself already anticipated. **The per-step/per-shell factor is therefore F(distance), the chain count is the O(N^2) face count, and the sum over N of an O(N^-2) tail converges** (`sum N^-2` is a textbook convergent series) — comfortably, with no fine-tuning needed given how small `||Phi||_F` already is at the cap.

What could make it fail is narrower and more concrete than for the state comparison: (i) Nachtergaele–Sims's Theorem 4.1, as AQ1 currently cites it, supplies only the *qualitative* existence of the thermodynamic-limit dynamics; the *quantitative* finite-Lambda-to-finite-Lambda' commutator estimate this loop needs is an intermediate step in that paper's own proof, not yet imported into this project, and re-deriving it could surface a worse power of N or a log N factor once its constants are actually pinned rather than cited by conclusion; (ii) the boundary shell contributes not only extra interaction terms `Phi(X)` but extra on-site terms `h_b` as well, and cancelling their effect on a Duhamel comparison cleanly needs the same "relative-unitary" bookkeeping AT4/AW1 already required for a different comparison — if that bookkeeping does not close for this specific comparison, a non-vanishing zeroth-order piece could survive and the whole O(N^-2) shape would need revision. Neither risk is a reason to expect outright failure; both are named, tractable technical steps, not missing premises the way the Dobrushin bridge (Section 2.1) or the uniform-in-a estimate (roadmap goal 4) currently are.

## 4. Applications stage (BD1/BD2)

The related problems this lens can name, each with the equation that would transfer, what must be re-derived, and the expected obstruction, are tabulated in full in `recommendation.json`'s `applications` list; the headline items:

- **Gauge-group transfer of the AW1 parity theorem and link-flip lemma.** Z2 and U(1) both possess the order-2 central element the flip construction needs and should transfer in structure, with every representation-theoretic *number* (the coefficient 1/144, the energy 3) needing full re-derivation via each group's own character theory. SU(N) with N even likewise has the needed order-2 central element; **SU(3), and SU(N) with N odd generally, do not** — `Z(SU(3))=Z_3={1,omega,omega^2}` has no element of order 2, so the exact `-1`-central flip construction the brief itself flags does not exist there, and the natural substitute (a charge-conjugation antisymmetry, since the fundamental representation `3` is not self-conjugate) is an unproven candidate route, not a fallback that should be assumed to work by analogy.
- **Two spatial dimensions (2+1D).** The whole AM2/AV1 method transfers as a technique; no single numeric constant does, since the coarse-factor geometry, the star-graph degree and every combinatorial constant in `L_k^num`, `J`, `G(R)` are specific to the Z^3/4-site-star geometry. Fewer neighbours in 2D plausibly gives smaller constants (a genuinely promising, not merely formal, direction), but this is a conjecture pending actual re-derivation.
- **Larger Wilson loops and electric-flux/Casimir observables.** AV1's split and AY1's itemized-incidence method transfer as techniques; larger loops give a genuinely weaker (larger) state bound with an honest finite reach obstruction expected once the loop is large enough. Electric-flux/Casimir observables are a qualitatively different case, not merely a harder one: they lack the `j=1/2`-per-link structure AW1's vanishing argument used, so their first-order term is plausibly *nonzero* already, meaning AW1's re-targeting argument (from correlation shift to Wilson mean) does not apply as-is and the first-order analysis would need to be redone from the AM2 fixed point directly.
- **The Round11 two-plaquette graph and earlier finite-model rounds.** AZ2's whole method (trace-monomial basis, five-part residual ledger, exact Rayleigh–Schrödinger coefficients) transfers to any small finite graph of this shape, as this lens's own Round32 memo already proposed (a single cube as the next fixture); the only obstruction is computational (basis growth), not mathematical.
- **The site's physics calculators.** `dist/calculators.js` currently has no lattice-gauge-theory domain at all (only mechanics/em/nuclear/quantum/relativity/thermal/waves, all general-physics OpenStax formulas — confirmed by direct inspection this loop). The admitted `D(tau)`, `K_2^+ tau^2` band and window-radius formulas are already closed-form rational functions of `tau`, so a calculator is a presentation task, not new mathematics — with one obstruction to respect explicitly: per CLAUDE.md, it must hard-clip evaluation to the admitted `|tau|<=1e-8` domain and cite each source gate, never present itself as computing a new admission.

**Proposed split.** *BD1* (paired): the gauge-group transfer (Z2, U(1), SU(N) even, and the SU(3)/odd-N obstruction recorded explicitly with its unproven candidate route) — new representation-theoretic derivation for a new model family, matching how AX1's route-B transfer was itself paired. *BD2* (statement+skeptic): a transfer ledger for the remaining candidates this round's capacity cannot all carry to full production (2+1D, larger loops/electric flux, Round11-style extensions, the site calculator), in the style of AY2 and AZ1 — recording what transfers, what does not, and why, updated with whatever BD1 and the BA/BB/BC sub-rounds have by then actually established, honoring the user's own "if any" framing rather than manufacturing production this round cannot complete.
