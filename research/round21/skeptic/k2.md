## Determinant identity and the two independent copies

The coefficient matrix follows directly from the two Dirichlet rates in
linear parameters `(c,c*zeta)`. Symmetrizing `a1*b2-a2*b1` gives

`D=(1/2) E[G(X)G(X')(X-X')(h_a(X)-h_a(X'))]`.

The difference of the squared derivative weights is
`4a(X-X')[1+a(X+X')]`, yielding the stated factor `2a` in the double integral.
This sign and factor were independently reconstructed before reading the
producers. X and X' are independent copies of the **complete interacting x
marginal**; the identity makes no independence claim about the physical U,V,W
links. Both reports preserve that necessary distinction.

For `0<a<1/2`, the bracket is uniformly positive. Positive marginal density on
the interior, positive G there, and positive measure away from the diagonal
give strict determinant positivity for every finite known kappa. The numerical
bound below has the narrower declared `|kappa|<=1/8` range.

## Uniform density transfer and exact constants

Action oscillation twelve bounds the normalized joint density from below by
`exp(-12|kappa|)` relative to Haar. Conditional integration preserves that
lower bound for the x marginal. Applying it to **both** copies costs
`exp(-24|kappa|)`. The transfer is legitimate because the lower-bounded
double-integral integrand is nonnegative.

An independent Catalan-moment calculation gives
`E_0[G G'(X-X')²]=2*(3/16)*(1/32)=3/256`. Therefore

`D >= 3a(1-2a) exp(-24|kappa|)/128`.

At `a=1/4`, the direct exponential enclosure in the forward checker and the
squared `exp(3/2)` enclosure in reverse both rigorously imply `exp(3)<21`.
The consequent `D>=1/7168` and exact Haar `D(0)=3/512` are correct.
The exact Haar matrix is `[[3/16,0],[25/128,1/32]]`. Choosing one quarter
maximizes this elementary sufficient factor; it does not optimize all possible
statistical designs or observational uncertainties.

At zero a the observables coincide. At a one the nonnegative-integrand argument
fails for some negative X,X', yet the Haar determinant remains positive.
Failure of the sufficient monotonicity proof is correctly kept separate from
universal rank failure.

## Inversion, conditioning and unknown model membership

The inverse formulas are the correct inverse matrix. The original-coordinate
Jacobian is cD rather than D. Output admission requires positive c and strict
`|zeta|<1`; the fixtures reject both scale failures and mobility boundary cases.
The matrix coefficients must correspond to the actual known kappa. The
all-kappa proof does not constitute a numerical implementation of every such
coefficient integral or an error bound for their approximations.

For `a=1/4`, the coefficient upper bounds give row sums of the inverse at most
`(13/16)*7168=5824`. This is an infinity-norm error bound for **linear**
parameters `(c,c*zeta)` with exact matrix coefficients. Recovering zeta requires
division by c. The reverse report's bound with denominator `c-|delta c|` is
valid when that denominator is positive. No uniform nonlinear stability claim
near zero c, uncertain kappa, or arbitrary observation noise is established.

The cubic `p3=x³-3x/8` is odd, so its first rate shift vanishes at Haar. For the
second rate only the `4ax` term survives parity; independent moments give
`E_0[x*p3*G]=3/256-(3/8)*(1/32)=0`. Both rates are therefore unchanged for
every a at kappa zero. This is not claimed at nonzero kappa.

The bound `|p3|<=11/8` is conservative but valid. At zero zeta and
`|xi|<=1/4`, the modified mobility is at least `21/32`; nonzero xi changes
the operator while retaining the static density and two selected slopes.
This supplies a genuine positive out-of-family counterexample. It does not
contradict injectivity of the affine family. In particular, fitting the two
