## The general finite-data argument

At fixed smooth positive density and fixed c, each initial slope is linear
in the mobility. Restricting N such real functionals to polynomials in x of
degree at most N produces an N by N+1 exact matrix. Rank-nullity gives a
nonzero coefficient vector even if some observations are redundant. Its
polynomial is a nonzero function: x has full marginal support on [-1,1],
and a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on an interval. Dividing by the
positive sum of absolute coefficients bounds its supremum by one.

For baseline `m0>=m_star>0`, every
`m_epsilon=m0+epsilon*P`, `|epsilon|<=m_star/2`, remains at least
`m_star/2`. The same smooth elliptic divergence-form construction therefore
applies. All selected slopes and the prescribed stationary density are
unchanged. For nonzero epsilon the second-order coefficient changes on an
open set, so the generator changes. Lower-order drift terms cannot cancel
that principal-symbol difference.

This is an exact mathematical construction. Arbitrary smooth observables
and a known nonzero kappa need not have computable closed-form moments;
the theorem does not supply numerical rank certification for approximate
or noisy matrices. Both reports preserve that distinction. The finite
implementation checks a rational Haar instance and does not purport to
test the general theorem by sampling.

The normalized degree-three example is
`(36-237x-216x²+662x³)/1151`. Its coefficient absolute sum is one, and
independent multiplication by the displayed 3 by 4 matrix gives three
exact zeros. Its existence does not conflict with L1: it uses a different
mobility direction from the specified span of 1, x and p3.

## The fifth polynomial and positive interval

Independent Catalan Haar moments give
`E[x²G]=1/32`, `E[x⁴G]=3/256`, `E[x⁶G]=3/512`,
`E[x⁸G]=7/2048`, with `G=(1-x²)/4`. For
`p5=x⁵-5x³/6+x/8`, the nontrivial odd tests are therefore

`E[x*p5*G]=3/512-(5/6)*(3/256)+(1/8)*(1/32)=0`,
`E[x³*p5*G]=7/2048-(5/6)*(3/512)+(1/8)*(3/256)=0`.

Parity removes all remaining terms in the three L1 derivative squares.
The three initial rates are exactly preserved at Haar. The coefficient
bound `||p5||_infinity<=47/24` gives `m>=49/96` for
`|epsilon|<=1/4`. This bound is conservative; neither report incorrectly
calls it the exact supremum or a necessary positivity boundary.

The finite expansion already independently checked in
[NIST DLMF 18.5.10](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.5#E10)
gives degree-five coefficients 192, -160 and 24 at parameter two.
Thus `p5=C_5^(2)/192` is classical. The polynomial family and the broad
finite-dimensional linear-algebra obstruction are not new discoveries.

## The generator changes, and the complete time curve can detect it

`T=(3x*p5-(1-x²)*p5')/4`
`=2x⁶-5x⁴/2+3x²/4-1/32`.

The sign includes both the Laplacian term and the mobility derivative.
Its value at zero is `-1/32`; the epsilon-quarter fixture is therefore
`-1/128`. This is an actual nonzero differential-operator witness.

Before reading the producers, I independently reconstructed T and used
Catalan moments to compute `E[T²]=1/1024`. T is even, so its cross term
with the odd `A_1 x=3x/4` is zero. Smooth x belongs to the operator
domain; its finite second spectral moment justifies the second right
derivative of the Euclidean correlation. Consequently

`C_x''(0+)=(c²/hbar²)*(9/64+epsilon²/1024)`.

For every nonzero epsilon the full x correlation is distinguishable by
this curvature, even though all three selected initial slopes agree.
The obstruction cannot be broadened to identical full time traces,
identical generators, or indistinguishability by all observations. This
explicit discriminator is stronger than merely stating that limitation.
