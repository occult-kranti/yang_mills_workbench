# Source-specific inference and exact observables

Use these modern lessons when applying analysis/synthesis to regulated inverses or observable transfer. They come from the paired, model-agent-reviewed Yang–Mills workbench Round26 AB–AD (2026-09-22), not Newton's writings, external peer review or an established priority claim.

## Reconstruct the actual residual

If a local candidate satisfies [K,G]=-A+F, take its equal-energy block before declaring the inverse successful: P_E A P_E=P_E F P_E. A small bound on F locates the possible obstruction but does not establish whether the actual block vanishes. An upper-bound floor is not a positive lower bound. Preserve the no-crossing exception and every exterior sector.

For a Gaussian filter, retain [J_s,G]=-A+R_s(A) and its zero-frequency multiplier. Contracting this particular A does not establish contraction on a subsequent residual or a changed diagonal. Require a new source estimate at the next stage. Specify operator norm, graph-domain control, spatial locality and interaction-weight summability as separate conclusions.

## Transfer only the information observed

Two bounded observables with B_1 Omega=B_2 Omega can share a reference-vacuum scalar when the necessary adjoints, propagators and stationary-state replacements are proved. This does not identify their action on other states, their norms or finite-parameter error budgets. Compute an actual multiplication observable's support, mean, variance, norm and a discriminating higher moment before substituting a rank operator. Charge state replacement using the actual norm.

A nonzero-variance observable may have a constant slow endpoint because the averaged interaction annihilates the reached source. Treat that as a null probe for the proposed scale. Choose a new probe by a predeclared support and selection-rule calculation; do not fit a readout to eigenvectors and call it the original observable. A scalar even in the clock variable cannot determine its sign.

## Preserve what the reconstruction proves

Finite moments determine a spectral measure only with a proved finite support or annihilating polynomial and a complete reachable-component argument. Matching several moments alone does not prove closure. For numerical evolution, distinguish an exact formula for a matrix action, an error bound on its exact semigroup, and an executed certified heat vector. Never describe the first two as the third.
