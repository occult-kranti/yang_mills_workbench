The strongest common methodological gains are source-specific inference, full channel loading and norm-specific locality. No equation was inserted into the Hamiltonian to force a desired pattern. No numerical scale or clock was fitted to equate the three models.

## Inferences specifically rejected

- A boundary upper bound does not prove a nonzero resonant lower bound or an exact inverse.
- Contraction on one source does not prove contraction on later sources or changed diagonals.
- A vanishing initial omitted column does not eliminate subsequently loaded channels.
- A positive variance does not make an observable informative about the chosen slow scale.
- Equality of vacuum-created vectors does not identify multiplication operators or their finite-parameter budgets.
- Gaussian spatial norm approximation alone does not imply graph-domain approximation; AE1 reverse separately controls every crossing commutator.
- A spatial upper budget that fails after multiplication by a stronger weight does not establish actual divergence.
- A bound on an exact retained semigroup does not mean that its vector has been numerically evaluated.

## Beyond this workbench

The fixed-spacing finite-graph, initial homogeneous and canonical summable models remain distinct. A physical continuum result still needs uniform volume control, a matched continuum trajectory, a nontrivial reconstructed theory with the required axioms and a positive physical mass gap. Neither the network layout nor a source-dependency edge supplies any of those premises.
