## Domain, operator identity and all orders

Both directions correctly distinguish preservation of the full operator
domain from regularization of arbitrary spectator vectors. The local finite
rank operators have bounded local products with H0_S; their exterior action
commutes with exterior spectral projections. Nonnegativity identifies the
sum domain with the intersection of local and exterior domains. Thus
[H0,S_b]=A_b on D(H0), and finite X is bounded in the graph norm.
Exponentials in that graph norm prove e^(+/-X)D(H0)=D(H0).

Both then integrate the first bounded commutator before expanding bounded
A and Phi. The exact correction is

    R = sum_(n>=1) [ad_X^n(Phi)/n! - ad_X^n(A)/(n+1)!].

The sign and factorial difference are correct. The finite-volume series
converges as a bounded operator for all finite tau; the separate smallness
condition gives uniformity in the prescribed interaction norm. Neither proof
places unbounded H0 in an unjustified operator-norm series.

The forward per-site recurrence is valid. If N_n is the weighted sum over
ordered words, a new intersecting star multiplies each weight/norm bound by
at most 16s. Words whose old support contains the root contribute at most
64s(4+3n)N_n. New stars containing the root contribute at most 256sN_n.
Double counting is harmless in this upper bound. Consequently
N_(n+1)<=64s(8+3n)N_n, starting at N_0<=64b. Summing the rising-factorial
series gives exponent 8/3 and r=192s, as claimed.

The reverse count also validates independently against the frozen preparation.
There are 13 overlapping star displacements. At insertion j there are at
most 13j possible anchors; hence 13^n n! ordered relative words. A fixed
relative word has at most 4+3n translates containing the root. Including
commutator growth and weight gives 16(4+3n)(208s)^n n! b. This includes repeated
anchors, outer generators that miss the seed but meet its generated support,
and all cross-star terms. Restricting retained anchors removes candidate words.

Neither method mistakes the generated supports for four-site stars, substitutes
unordered sets for words, or loses the site-root multiplicity. Their different
constants reflect different valid overcounts.

## Vacuum residual and inference limits

The diagonal coefficient 28|tau| includes four stars per interior site and
follows from Q_S<=sum_(x in S)(I-P_x). Therefore H0+D alone has the
reference ground and gap at least 1-28|tau| when positive. Adding R requires
new information.

Forward's actual-model vacuum calculation is valid. Every omitted elementary
face has two globally free Haar links. Distinct square faces share at most
one link, including faces assigned to different anchors, so integration over
an unmatched free link kills each mixed expectation. Thus for N retained
anchors ||V_1||^2=7N/12. With H0 U_1=V_1, positivity of the inverse on the
excited subspace gives <U_1,H0 U_1>>0 when N>0. The exact analytic expansion
then has vacuum mean -tau^2<U_1,H0 U_1>+O_Lambda(|tau|^3). This establishes
nonannihilation and failure of an uncentered pure relative bound for
sufficiently small nonzero tau in each fixed nonempty volume. It is not a
uniform lower bound or a gap-failure statement.

Reverse's seven-qubit calculation has a different role. Its explicit
second-order excited component and controlled higher-order tail disprove a
general scalar-shift repair of vacuum mixing under the norm/domain hypotheses.
It does not identify a centered off-diagonal matrix element of the actual
SU(2) remainder. Both reports preserve this distinction.

I additionally checked a different one-qubit fixture with
H0=diag(0,1), phi=tau[[0,1],[1,1]]/2 and its rank-two generator. At tau=1/100,
the exact centered off-diagonal term has the strictly negative rational upper
bound -tau^2/4+tau^3/12+tau^4/24. A scalar does not alter that entry; testing
Omega+z e1 yields a linear form term against quadratic reference energy.
This independently rejects the universal centered-pure-relative inference.
It remains a lemma fixture, not a physical SU(2) computation.

Small ||R||_w is not a uniform global operator-norm bound: independent local
scalar terms already have bounded local norm and extensive global norm.
Conversely, an extensive vacuum shift need not damage a gap. The papers
therefore correctly stop short of a numerical stability claim.

## Feedback-selected O2 recommendation

Rank the arbitrary-support closure target first: formulate a homological
inverse and commutator estimate for generated interactions after local vacuum
scalar subtraction, allowing explicit loss between two support weights.
Keep the first-order D and its relative bound in the equations. Determine
whether the resulting numerical recurrence actually controls residual mixing,
relative diagonal growth, inverse gaps and cumulative weight loss through
iteration. The important test is an explicit inequality that closes, or a
counterexample/quantitative obstruction to that stated inequality. Repeating
an unchanged four-star rotation does not test this missing premise.

Importance is high and feasibility is moderate for a rigorous bounded
outcome: O1 now supplies a controlled initial remainder, while its generated
supports identify the exact new class to study. A failure of a fixed-weight
universal commutator estimate must be scoped to that estimate. A failed
iteration certificate does not imply actual gap failure or failure of every
algorithm. Scalar centering alone must not count as removal of vacuum mixing.

Reconstructing all applicable source constants ranks second. It could yield
a useful independent quantitative route, but requires the source's distinct
norm, domain and full induction bookkeeping; extracting one visible constant
or threshold symbol will not suffice. Prefer that route if the explicit
arbitrary-support analysis identifies a concrete source estimate that resolves
its obstruction. This is advice after O1 review, not an executed O2 loop or
a frozen selection. No P, Q or R target is selected here.
