## Independent proof audit

The decisive operator step is sound. A bounded self-adjoint perturbation puts
both Hamiltonians on the same dense domain. Differentiation of the relative
unitary product on that domain contains no observable. The reference orbit is
continuous in the reference graph norm, and boundedness of V makes that graph
norm equivalent to the perturbed graph norm. Integrating this relative-product
derivative and extending by density yields its strong derivative on every
Hilbert vector, with uniform bound `||V||/hbar`.

Only after obtaining this bounded derivative do the reports differentiate a
relative-unitary product with a fixed bounded observable between it and its
adjoint. Thus they never require `B D(H_ref) subset D(H_ref)`. Products of
strongly continuous uniformly bounded factors have strongly continuous vector
orbits. The resulting integral exists vectorwise and defines a bounded
operator; the uniform vector estimate then permits the supremum over unit
vectors. Neither proof assumes an operator-norm Bochner integral or arbitrary
point-norm continuity.

The two displayed integral formulas are identical after the substitution
`s -> t-s`:

`beta_q(t)(B)-beta_0(t)(B)`
`=-(i/hbar) integral_0^t beta_q(t-s)([V_q,beta_0(s)(B)]) ds`.

This equality has the correct sign and oriented endpoints for every real t.
Complete reference-factor splitting preserves the finite factor cover of
`beta_0(s)(B)` for all s. Norm convergence of the Wilson series permits
termwise commutation; only omitted faces meeting every link of that cover
remain. Each face is counted once. Consequently,

`||beta_q(t)(B)-beta_0(t)(B)||`
`<= 2 alpha tau_q |t| ||B|| D_FB(q)/hbar`.

Outer perturbed conjugation preserves the commutator norm. It need not
preserve finite support. Neither report confuses these two roles.

Stationarity then gives the exact negative-time Heisenberg representation of
the contract's shifted single-unitary matrix element. The ground energy
cancels in conjugation because both scalar phases are present. It cannot be
deleted from the original unshifted matrix element. The rank-one state
comparison costs `2d_q ||A||||B||`; the two mean-product changes each cost the
same amount. Complex conjugation is retained in `omega(A*)`. This proves

`|C_q(t)-C_ref(t)|`
`<= ||A||||B|| [6d_q+2alpha tau_q |t| D_FB(q)/hbar]`.

The bound holds in the full fixed representation and hence in the inherited
physical invariant sector. Its application does not transfer a dyadic gap,
change the Hamiltonian or identify a conditional mobility clock.

## Scale and endpoint audit

The exact profile implies
`tau_q/(1-q)^3 -> 8eta/7` and
`sigma_q^2/[alpha^2 eta^2 (1-q)^3] -> 1/5376`.
Since `gbar=alpha(1-eta)/8`, the squared leading state-distance certificate
constant is `eta^2/[84(1-eta)^2]`. For a fixed finite incident-face set of size
m, `D_FB(q)->m/24`. Thus the two terms in the normalized window certificate
have asymptotic coefficients

`6eta/[(1-eta)sqrt(84)]` times `(1-q)^(3/2)`, and
`2eta C m/21` times `(1-q)^(3-gamma)`.

Every term vanishes when `0<=gamma<3`; the sufficient error order is
`O((1-q)^min(3/2,3-gamma))`. The reverse report's global finite-q upper
constants also follow from its displayed positive-polynomial inequalities.
These are asymptotics of upper certificates, not equalities for the actual
projector distance or correlation error.

For `gamma=3` and m>0 the uncapped certificate tends to `2eta C m/21`.
For the origin-xz cover m=28 and `eta=1/2,C=1`, this equals `4/3`.
This is not a lower bound on the error. Constants and empty incident sets
provide exact exceptions; the reverse phase families provide an additional
logical discrimination. No actual endpoint nonconvergence is admitted.
