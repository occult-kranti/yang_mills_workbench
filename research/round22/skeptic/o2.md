## All-step obstruction, numerical tests and conditional gap

The prescribed losses telescope to log(2)/2 and preserve limiting weight
log(2)/2. Let the positive upper budgets be defined by equality in either
submitted recurrence. Their diagonal budget stays at least d0=448|tau|.
While the series condition holds, their residual ratios are bounded below by
12d0/delta_n or 4d0/delta_n. Since delta_n tends to zero, these ratios would
eventually exceed two. A positive residual budget growing geometrically
cannot remain below delta_n/12 or delta_n/4. Hence either scalar recurrence
leaves its admissible region after finitely many steps at nonzero tau.

This proof also covers the same recurrence with any positive summable loss
schedule. It does not infer a lower bound on the actual remainder from an
upper bound. Tau=0 is the exact trivial exception. Reverse's stricter
one-step shrink condition delta>4d+10r also follows by exact rearrangement.

Forward's rational-loss recurrence at tau=2^(-22) has its minimum at step 15
and first violates the series condition at step 42. Its two-sided dyadic
rounding bounds establish both statements, and an independent two-sided
10^(-120) grid calculation reproduces them. At failure its relative
coefficient is still below 1/1000: the series condition fails first.
These indices refer to the stated conservative rational loss, not the
exact-log recurrence or physical residuals.

Reverse correctly verifies four shrinking initial stages at tau=10^(-8),
then uses log(2)<7/10 to show that conditional on reaching stage 197, its
ratio must be at least two thereafter. This is a quantified all-step
obstruction, not an extrapolation from those four stages. Its one-qubit
Taylor control and forward's direct commutator both confirm the genuine
linear term -dr for a bare inverse with retained D; scalar centering does
not remove that term.

The conditional gap arguments are sound: if another valid recurrence gave
summable residuals, admissibility at every stage and kappa_infinity<1, the
finite-volume scalar and diagonal series, unitary products and their
inverses would converge in both Hilbert and graph operator norms. The final
Hamiltonian would have the product ground and gap at least 1-kappa_infinity,
uniformly in volume. The submitted recurrences do not satisfy those premises.
Neither proof constructs an infinite global unitary or representation transfer.
