# Independent challenge and proof: positive correlators and a gap

Status: the statements below are conditional mathematical lemmas, independently derived for this review. They do not establish any missing Yang–Mills premise. No numerical runs were made by this advisor.

## 1. The effective-mass statement is correct with these conditions

Let \(\mu\) be a finite, nonzero, positive Borel measure supported on \([0,\infty)\), let \(E_0=\inf\operatorname{supp}\mu\), and define

\[
C(t)=\int e^{-Et}\,d\mu(E),\quad t\ge0,
\qquad
m_\delta(t)=\frac{\log C(t)-\log C(t+\delta)}{\delta},\quad\delta>0.
\]

Then \(m_\delta(t)\ge E_0\), \(m_\delta\) is nonincreasing for fixed \(\delta\), and \(\lim_{t\to\infty}m_\delta(t)=E_0\).

**Lower bound.** Since \(E\ge E_0\) almost everywhere,
\(C(t+\delta)\le e^{-E_0\delta}C(t)\), which gives the bound after taking logarithms.

**Monotonicity.** Hölder's inequality gives log-convexity of C: for \(0\le\theta\le1\),
\(C(\theta t+(1-\theta)s)\le C(t)^\theta C(s)^{1-\theta}\).
The slopes of fixed-length secants of a convex function are nondecreasing. Thus the negative secant slope \(m_\delta\) is nonincreasing. This argument includes t=0 without assuming finite energy moments. For t>0, differentiation is justified by exponential damping and gives

\[
-\partial_t\log C(t)=\langle E\rangle_t,
\qquad
\partial_t[-\partial_t\log C(t)]=-\operatorname{Var}_t(E)\le0,
\]

where \(d\mu_t=e^{-Et}d\mu/C(t)\). The discrete effective mass is the average of \(\langle E\rangle_s\) over \([t,t+\delta]\).

**Limit.** For every \(\epsilon>0\), the interval \([E_0,E_0+\epsilon]\) has positive measure \(b_\epsilon\). Hence

\[
b_\epsilon e^{-(E_0+\epsilon)t}\le C(t)\le\mu([0,\infty))e^{-E_0t}.
\]

Therefore \(-t^{-1}\log C(t)\to E_0\). Combining this exponential rate with convexity shows every fixed-length negative secant slope approaches the same E0. Equivalently, the monotone \(m_\delta\) has a limit, and its telescoping averages \([\log C(t_0)-\log C(t_0+n\delta)]/(n\delta)\) converge both to that limit and to E0.

**Direction that must not be reversed.** \(m_\delta(t)\ge E_0\) is an upper estimate of the lowest overlapping energy E0, not a certified lower bound on it. Seeing \(m_\delta\approx1\) cannot prove \(E_0\ge0.9\).

## 2. Exact false-plateau example

Take \(\mu=\delta_1+10^{-8}\delta_{0.05}\), so \(C(t)=e^{-t}+10^{-8}e^{-0.05t}\). Its true lowest overlapping energy is 0.05, but a short window near t=0 is governed almost entirely by the mass-1 term. Equal contributions occur at

\[
t_{\rm cross}=\frac{\log(10^8)}{1-0.05}\approx19.3902.
\]

No finite-precision bound on an early window can exclude a sufficiently small positive overlap at lower energy. More seriously, even the exact E0 of one operator channel may exceed the full theory's gap if that operator is orthogonal to a lighter sector.

Finite-temperature/periodic-time data have wraparound terms such as \(e^{-Et}+e^{-E(T-t)}\), and do not directly obey this zero-temperature semigroup formula. Time smearing, operator renormalization, connected subtraction and physical time units must be stated. Unsmeared local fields are distributions; do not assume their coincident-point correlator supplies a finite measure. Use finite-norm states generated by appropriately smeared operators.

## 3. Conditional uniform-bound-to-gap lemma

Assume:

1. A continuum physical Hilbert space \(\mathcal H\), a nonnegative self-adjoint H, and a normalized vacuum \(\Omega\) with \(H\Omega=0\) have already been constructed.
2. States \(\psi_O\in\Omega^\perp\), generated for example by vacuum-subtracted smeared gauge-invariant operators, have linear span dense in \(\Omega^\perp\).
3. Their diagonal Euclidean correlators are the semigroup matrix elements \(C_O(t)=\langle\psi_O,e^{-tH}\psi_O\rangle\). This representation supplies a finite positive spectral measure of total mass \(\|\psi_O\|^2\).
4. There is one common physical m>0 such that, for every O, there are finite constants \(A_O\) and \(t_O\) with \(0\le C_O(t)\le A_Oe^{-mt}\) for every physical \(t\ge t_O\).

Then \(H\) restricted to \(\Omega^\perp\) has spectrum in \([m,\infty)\). In particular there is no additional zero-energy vector in \(\Omega^\perp\).

**Proof.** Let \(P_H\) be the spectral projection measure of H and \(0<\epsilon<m\). Positivity gives

\[
e^{-(m-\epsilon)t}\,\|P_H([0,m-\epsilon])\psi_O\|^2
\le C_O(t)\le A_Oe^{-mt}.
\]

Multiply by \(e^{(m-\epsilon)t}\), then take t to infinity. The squared projection norm is zero. Taking an increasing countable union of these intervals shows \(P_H([0,m))\psi_O=0\) for every O. A bounded projection that vanishes on a set with dense linear span vanishes on the whole vacuum-orthogonal subspace. That establishes the claimed spectral exclusion. This is a standard spectral-theorem argument, reproduced explicitly here.

The lemma needs neither a universal prefactor A for all operators nor an isolated first excited eigenstate. It does need a common strictly positive rate m and control for arbitrarily large times. Decay \(e^{-m_Ot}\) with \(m_O>0\) but \(\inf_Om_O=0\) does not establish a global gap. Nontriviality of the continuum Hilbert space and the full quantum-field axioms remain separate requirements.

## 4. How a regulator bound can reach hypothesis 4

This is one sufficient strategy, not a necessity theorem. Let r label a declared sequence/path of lattice spacings, physical volumes and couplings, with matched renormalized observables \(O_r\). Suppose for every fixed physical t,

\[
C_{O,r}(t)\longrightarrow C_O(t),\qquad
|C_{O,r}(t)|\le A_Oe^{-mt}\quad(t\ge t_O),
\]

where \(A_O,t_O,m\) do not depend on r, and the continuum has already been reconstructed with the positive diagonal representation in hypothesis 3. Passing to the limit at each fixed t preserves the bound. No interchange of t→infinity with r→limit is needed: take the continuum limit first for each t, then apply the proven continuum lemma for arbitrarily large t.

**Missing Yang–Mills obligations:**

- Construct the continuum correlators and their matched renormalized observable algebra.
- Prove the full hypotheses of the applicable Osterwalder–Schrader/Wightman reconstruction, including the required regularity/growth conditions; a finite positive matrix is insufficient.
- Control physical time conversion as lattice spacing changes, remove temporal wraparound, and keep m in fixed physical units.
- Produce a genuinely regulator/volume-independent exponential estimate; fixed-spacing strong-coupling estimates do not automatically follow a weak-coupling continuum trajectory.
- Prove the vacuum-subtracted observable states span densely in the full physical nonvacuum sector, rather than a sampled channel.
- Establish nontriviality and the expected pure nonabelian Yang–Mills short-distance content for each required compact simple group.

**Failure controls:** allow \(A_{O,r}\to\infty\), \(t_{O,r}\to\infty\), or \(m_r\to0\); remove positivity; restrict to a finite time window; or omit a low-energy channel. Each alteration blocks at least one stated inference and must reopen the route.
