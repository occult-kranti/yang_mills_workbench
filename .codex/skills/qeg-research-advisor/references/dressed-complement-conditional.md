# Dressed clusters, full complements and surrounding integration

Use after the six-loop lattice contract when continuing Round18's three goals.

A zero Haar mean in the bare vacuum is not automatically a zero mean in a dressed reference. Prove the relevant free-link factor in the actual reference ground. If that mean vanishes, separate min–max and reference Rayleigh bounds can lose one perturbation norm; without the premise retain the generic two-norm bound and its diagonal two-state falsifier. The physical Hilbert space need not factor even when the full link space does. Explain gauge invariance of the resulting unique ground before restricting a full-space bound.

A full-support summable interaction family is still spatially inhomogeneous. State whether finite boundary rules are restrictions of one infinite coefficient assignment. Boundary reclassification, pointwise eventual stabilization and thermodynamic spectral convergence are different facts. A limit of finite norm budgets is not automatically their uniform upper bound. Conversely, absence of a proof of that upper bound does not establish that an overshoot occurs; require an actual counterexample before reporting one.

For a spectral projector, prove that every physical channel below the claimed complement threshold is included. Count support, vertex intertwiners and all required graph cycles. An added adjoint square does not cover lower six-edge fundamental loops. Use W*W=PV²P−(PVP)² to control the entire complement only after proving P reduces H0 and computing all repeated moments. A codimension-one min–max estimate can use the space orthogonal to a declared trial vector; do not rename a trial-matrix gap as the full lower E1 bound. Continuous coefficient boxes can be proved by analytic worst-constant inequalities; plots then illustrate rather than establish coverage.

Complete Gram data can be sufficient for declared central dot-product observables, including singular data, while remaining insufficient to define a surrounding measure. Validate positivity, rank, vector norms and action-vector constraints without requiring an inverse for a valid singular Gram. An O(4) central-coordinate isometry does not automatically act on the surrounding links. When unfreezing a link, rebuild the union of all affected face weights. Correlated trace coordinates require their induced joint moments, including representation-dimension factors. Keep the observable fixed in omitted-weight comparisons and bound numerator and normalization tails together.

A wrong-model control must disagree at the degree and observable actually tested. On the two-cube graph, independent one-face Haar marginals reproduce the required joint moments through degree four even though full face independence fails at a closed six-face surface. Do not report a higher-order obstruction as a discrepancy in a lower-order exact coincidence. A Gaussian repeated fourth moment and omission of the P subtraction are separate discriminating controls.

Distinguish an incorrect exact identity from a conservative inequality. Omitting the P subtraction gives PV²P, a positive-semidefinite upper bound on W*W; it can be valid for a deliberately weaker norm estimate. Reject it when presented as the exact cross Gram, without asserting that every bound using it is invalid.

A worst-constant two-block comparison may hold only for the nonnegative pair of component norms after an absolute cross-term estimate. Do not replace that argument with an unproved Loewner ordering of the scalar matrices on arbitrary signed vectors. Match the bare-coupling/spacing coefficients before extrapolating a dimensionless box toward a continuum path; a fixed small magnetic/electric ratio can exclude that path.

An outward interval for an analytic lower-bound expression is not a two-sided interval for the unknown physical gap. Label its upper endpoint accordingly. Preserve a coarse enclosure that cannot establish sign or target precision even when a separate analytic inequality proves positivity.

Before demanding an omitted weight or changed word produce a different normalized observable, prove that the selected test is discriminating. A factor may cancel between numerator and partition, and a measure-preserving substitution may preserve a joint law. Retain that exception and choose another observable or a normalization test as needed. It does not justify silently changing the original action. For a nested normalized central average, include its conditional partition in the outer weight.

A graph-incidence premise does not supply equal coupling coefficients. A dependency rule producing b=κ(3e0+V) or exp(2κy) must explicitly consume the common-action premise, even when the current numerical fixture already satisfies it. Withdraw that premise on the actual connection route and require it to block.
