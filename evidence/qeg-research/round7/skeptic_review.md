# Round 7 skeptical review: what a new variable can and cannot repair

Review date: 9 September 2026. Historical round-5 and round-6 derivations were read as mathematical contracts; they were not silently promoted to continuum QED. This review independently derived the identities below and executed `independent_checks.py` without importing production solvers. The current record contains 42 passing gates, including exact algebra, reference-integral checks, and an independent scalar-gravity integration. Ordinary and optimized Python both pass. Symbolic checks are conventional algebraic verification, not proof-assistant certificates; numerical gates are tolerance tests, not interval enclosures.

The constructive result is specific: a dynamical scalar with its own action, force equation and energy can complete the **finite model's new exchange channel**, and a separate classical scalar–Maxwell gravity model can propagate the Einstein constraint. A bounded change of the old matching coefficient cannot repair the unmodified cofinal cutoff obstruction. A cutoff-dependent subtraction can restore a coefficient inequality algebraically, but physical renormalization requires more than that inequality.

## 1. Four meanings of a new variable

| Proposed addition | Meaning | What must be supplied | Current verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A finite constant shift of susceptibility | A changed matching convention or different model parameter | A renormalization condition and consistent current/energy change | Cannot cancel an unbounded cofinal coefficient |
| A cutoff-dependent counterterm | Regulator bookkeeping | Common regulated action, physical matching and finite observables | A coefficient subtraction identity is established; continuum interpretation remains open |
| A renormalization scale μ | Auxiliary scale in a perturbative organization | Beta functions, matching, amplitudes and residual scale-error estimate | Does not itself add a spacetime degree of freedom |
| A physical scalar φ(x) entering f(φ)F² | An added dynamical sector or physical EFT hypothesis | Scalar equation, scalar stress, all exchange terms and admissible data | Two restricted action-based branches are mathematically consistent and testable |

These operations cannot be interchanged by renaming the independent variable. For one active unit-charge Dirac species at one loop, μ de/dμ=e³/(12π²), equivalently μ dα/dμ=2α²/(3π). This is a scale equation in its perturbative domain; it is not a spacetime evolution equation or a nonperturbative UV completion. The source's derivation also distinguishes scale dependence of couplings from that of complete Green functions. [Southampton advanced QFT notes, sections 4.1–4.2](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~doug/qft/aqft4.pdf).

Neither N, the inclusive Landau-level cutoff, nor K, the canonical longitudinal window, has been derived here as a universal physical renormalization scale. The round-6 logarithmic coefficient obstruction is therefore not identified with an observed Landau pole. Choosing e² to decay with the cutoff can change the no-go hypotheses, but must then confront fixed nonzero measured-charge matching; it is not an automatic resolution.

## 2. A strengthened bounded-coupling no-go theorem

Fix b>0 and e²>0. In the continuous longitudinal integral model define

\[
C_{N,K}(a)=\frac b{16\pi^2}\sum_{n=0}^{N}d_n
\int_{-K}^{K}\frac{M_n^2\,dk}{[M_n^2+(k-a)^2]^{5/2}},
\quad M_n^2=1+2bn,\quad d_n=2-\delta_{n0}.
\tag{S1}
\]

Let (N_j,K_j) be cofinal: N_j→∞ and K_j→∞. Suppose |a_j|≤A with a fixed finite A. Let h_j be any proposed replacement for the complete positive bare/matched coefficient, with a uniform finite upper bound h_j≤H. Then

\[
Z_j=h_j-e^2C_{N_j,K_j}(a_j)\longrightarrow-\infty.
\tag{S2}
\]

This statement permits h_j to change continuously in time, depend on the cutoff, or be generated by auxiliary fields, provided the stated upper bound is uniform along the evaluated family. A scalar bounded on a common compact interval, together with continuous f, supplies such a bound on f(φ). Bounded φ alone does not imply bounded f if f has a pole in the allowed interval; such a singular coefficient is outside this argument and creates a new regularity obligation.

**Proof, including the order of limits.** In kinetic momentum p=k−a, the integration interval is [−K−a,K−a]. For K>A and |a|≤A it contains [−(K−A),K−A]. Positivity of every summand yields

\[
C_{N,K}(a)\ge C_{N,K-A}(0).
\tag{S3}
\]

For fixed finite N, direct integration gives

\[
C_{N,\infty}(0)=\frac b{12\pi^2}\left[1+2\sum_{n=1}^N\frac1{1+2bn}\right],
\tag{S4}
\]

which diverges as N increases by comparison with the harmonic series. To obtain a genuinely cofinal result, fix any threshold T. Select finite N₀ for which C_N₀,∞>T. Finite-sum convergence in K supplies finite K₀ for which C_N₀,K₀>T. Monotonicity in both cutoffs implies C_N,K>T whenever N≥N₀ and K≥K₀. Apply this with K_j−A, then use S3 and the bound h_j≤H. This proves S2 without exchanging limits.

**Scope of the no-go.** It concerns positivity of the separated coefficient in the specified closure. It does not prove that the combined renormalized current diverges, that continuum QED does not exist, that all trajectories encounter a denominator zero, or that no new action can change the ultraviolet behavior. If a_j escapes with the cutoff, S3 no longer supplies the conclusion. Moving the physical potential with the regulator also requires a coherent state and gauge prescription; a regulator-dependent escape is not a fixed-physical-problem convergence test.

The finite scalar completion in section 5 preserves the existing fixed-window theorem because f≥1. It does not defeat S2 in a joint limit with bounded scalar and potential data. The two claims have compatible, different domains.

## 3. A legitimate algebraic subtraction, with the missing physical theorem exposed

Fix a finite reference regulator (N★,K★), let C★=C_N★,K★(0), and suppose z★=1+χ★−e²C★>0. Define a new regulator-dependent coefficient

\[
\delta\chi_{N,K}=e^2[C_{N,K}(0)-C_\star],\qquad
Z^{\rm trial}_{N,K}(a)=1+\chi_\star+\delta\chi_{N,K}-e^2C_{N,K}(a).
\tag{S5}
\]

The round-6 continuous-integral maximum at a=0 gives the exact identity and bound

\[
Z^{\rm trial}_{N,K}(a)=z_\star+e^2[C_{N,K}(0)-C_{N,K}(a)]\ge z_\star.
\tag{S6}
\]

S6 is a constructive coefficient lemma. Rejecting its algebra merely because the physical problem remains open would itself be an error. But S6 alone does not establish a covariant renormalization prescription, state/current convergence, directional pressures, finite energy, a continuum action or equivalence to the original matching. A b-dependent finite subtraction at one background must be related to physical renormalization conditions before claiming a universal counterterm. The full state term S and its subtraction must be treated together with C; a separated denominator is not the entire current.

S6 does not hold for an arbitrary fixed quadrature after replacing both continuous C values by discrete sums. A two-node positive Gauss rule on [−20,20] at b=10 has a much larger n=0 contribution when a is aligned with a node than at a=0. Subtraction at the discrete origin therefore need not retain the reference gap. The independent check executes this falsifier. A quadrature-specific supremum bound or certified quadrature error is required.

**Backward obligation:** to call S5 physical renormalization, the target is a regulator-independent renormalized current/stress pair in a specified state. Regressing that goal requires a common regulated generating functional, local allowed counterterms, finite matching, and convergence in a declared topology. The forward coefficient identity meets only the algebraic matching obligation; it does not meet the remaining obligations.

## 4. Time-dependent matching has an unavoidable work term

Retain the finite-model identities U′=xS and C′=−2Dx. Let Z=1+χ(t)−e²C and W_Q=Zx²/2+e²U. Then Z′=χ′+2e²Dx.

If one merely replaces χ by χ(t) in the old divided equation,

\[
Zx'=F-e^2(S+Dx^2),
\]

exact differentiation gives

\[
W_Q'=xF+\frac12\chi' x^2.
\tag{S7}
\]

If instead χ(t) is introduced consistently as an explicitly time-dependent kinetic coefficient in the reduced action, the Maxwell equation acquires −χ′x in its numerator:

\[
Zx'=F-e^2(S+Dx^2)-\chi'x,
\qquad W_Q'=xF-\frac12\chi'x^2.
\tag{S8}
\]

Both formulas are correct for their different equations. Their opposite signs are not a contradiction. They show why a changed coefficient must travel with a changed evolution law and a work ledger. A prescribed coefficient can represent an open driven system; conservation of a larger system requires a specified support sector. Imposing a new energy variable by integrating the deficit is useful bookkeeping, but is not yet a physical stress tensor with directional pressures and its own equation.

The independent script checks S7, S8, and nonzero wrong-model defects. The cancellation cannot be tested by choosing χ′=0 or x=0 everywhere; those degenerate cases erase the distinction.

## 5. Accepted finite variational completion with a physical scalar hypothesis

This branch is a new **finite hybrid model**. It holds b, retained canonical nodes, masses, weights, e² and χ fixed. It is not obtained by assuming a continuum stress formula that has not been computed. Choose

\[
f(\phi)=1+g^2\phi^2,\qquad V(\phi)=\nu^2\phi^2/2,\qquad
Z=f(\phi)+\chi-e^2C(a).
\]

Keep a′=−x and the Bloch equations r_i′=2h_i×r_i; introduce

\[
\phi'=y,\quad y'=-V_\phi+\frac12f_\phi(x^2-b^2),
\quad Zx'=F-e^2(S+Dx^2)-f_\phi yx.
\tag{S9}
\]

The reduced action uses the spinor representation r_i=z_i†σz_i, normalized states z_i†z_i=1:

\[
L=\frac12Z\dot a^2+\frac12\dot\phi^2-V-\frac12fb^2
+e^2\sum_iw_i[i z_i^\dagger\dot z_i-z_i^\dagger(h_i\cdot\sigma)z_i-\omega_i]-aF.
\tag{S10}
\]

The Berry term can be symmetrized to make reality manifest, differing by a total derivative on normalized states. The potential variation uses Z_a=−2e²D and
∂_a[−z†h·σz−ω]=r_z+p/ω; the contact term is retained. These give the Maxwell equation in S9. Scalar variation gives its force equation. Variation of each spinor gives the original two-level Schrödinger evolution and hence the Bloch equation.

Define

\[
W=\frac12Zx^2+e^2U+\frac12y^2+V+\frac12fb^2.
\tag{S11}
\]

The Maxwell exchange contributes −f_φyx²/2 after differentiating its field and modal energy. Scalar plus magnetic energy contributes +f_φyx²/2. Consequently

\[
W'=xF.
\tag{S12}
\]

This cancellation depends on both added terms in S9. Omitting −f_φyx from Maxwell produces the exact defect W′−xF=f_φyx². Omitting the magnetic piece from the scalar force produces f_φyb²/2. Both are checked independently.

Assume the original finite-coefficient gap 1+χ−e²C≥μ>0, physical Bloch norms ≤1, finite positive masses/weights, and continuous external F. Since f≥1 and U≥0, S11 obeys W≥μx²/2+y²/2+ν²φ²/2. Epsilon regularization of √W gives

\[
\sqrt{W(t)}\le\sqrt{W(0)}+\frac1{\sqrt{2\mu}}\int_0^t|F(s)|ds.
\tag{S13}
\]

For ν>0 this bounds x,y,φ on every finite interval; a is bounded there by integrating a′=−x, and all Bloch coordinates remain bounded. The smooth finite vector field with Z≥μ then admits the same ordinary-ODE continuation argument. This is a conventional global-forward theorem for each stated finite model. ν>0 is a sufficient direct scalar bound, not a claimed necessary condition: with ν=0 one may instead integrate bounded y on finite intervals.

The fixed background b is an explicit assumption of this reduced model. This action does not compute stresses associated with the original quantum subtraction under metric variation, and does not justify joining these flat finite modes directly to a time-dependent Bianchi geometry. A conserved scalar energy channel is valuable progress without being a covariant renormalized Dirac stress construction.

## 6. Accepted separate scalar–Maxwell gravity benchmark

Use signature (−,+,+,+), no external current, constant Λ, and

\[
S=\int\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^2}{2}(R-2\Lambda)
-\frac12(\nabla\phi)^2-V(\phi)-\frac14f(\phi)F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\right]d^4x.
\tag{S14}
\]

Such scalar-dependent electromagnetic actions are established model classes, rather than a new invention here. Their existing charged-black-hole solutions use their own symmetry, coupling choices and boundary conditions; those results are not imported as proofs of this project. [Fernandes, sections 1–2, equations 1.1 and 2.2–2.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.01045v3).

For the source-free EM equations ∇_μ(fF^{μν})=0 and a real scalar, the mixed-index identities following from S14 are

\[
\nabla_\mu T_{\rm EM}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}=-\frac14F^2\partial_\nu f,
\qquad
\nabla_\mu T_\phi^{\mu}{}_{\nu}=(\Box\phi-V_\phi)\partial_\nu\phi.
\tag{S15}
\]

The scalar equation is Box φ−V_φ−f_φF²/4=0, so the exchanges cancel. If f is prescribed without the scalar stress/equation, the first term remains. This is the covariant counterpart of the work-ledger problem.

In axial Bianchi I, ds²=−dt²+A²(dx²+dy²)+C²dz², let aligned physical E,B point along z and θ=2H⊥+H∥. Then

\[
\dot E=-(2H_\perp+\dot f/f)E,\quad \dot B=-2H_\perp B,
\quad \ddot\phi=-\theta\dot\phi-V_\phi-\frac12f_\phi(B^2-E^2).
\tag{S16}
\]

The EM stress is ρ_EM=f(E²+B²)/2, p⊥,EM=ρ_EM and p∥,EM=−ρ_EM. Scalar pressure is isotropic: ρ_φ=φ̇²/2+V and p_φ=φ̇²/2−V. Direct differentiation, independent of Einstein equations, gives

\[
Q_{\rm EM}=\frac12\dot f(B^2-E^2),\quad
Q_\phi=-\frac12\dot f(B^2-E^2),
\quad fEA^2=\text{constant},\ BA^2=\text{constant}.
\tag{S17}
\]

Together with the round-5 spatial Einstein evolution, the constraint
C_g=H⊥²+2H⊥H∥−κρ−Λ obeys Ċ_g=−θC_g−κQ. Omitting the scalar electromagnetic force while retaining f-dependent Maxwell evolution leaves Q=φ̇f_φ(B²−E²)/2. One cannot repair this by projecting C_g to zero after each step and then reporting a small constraint residual.

**Executed independent check.** In dimensionless M_Pl=1 units the verifier uses f=exp(0.8φ), V=0.125φ², initial φ=0.2, φ′=0.03, E=0.04, B=0.07, H⊥=0.04, and solves H∥ from the initial constraint. A Radau integration to t=10 on 251 samples obtains max |C_g|=4.943441489491107e−14. The deliberately omitted-force model gives max |C_g|=1.5810495984879252e−4. Its drift follows a separately evolved equation driven by the predicted Q, within the 10⁻⁹ acceptance gate. Electric and magnetic fluxes, output completeness, and tolerance refinement also pass. The result verifies a nondegenerate classical benchmark; it supplies no renormalized QED quantum state or stress.

## 7. Euler–Heisenberg and curvature: retain a controlled benchmark, not another arbitrary filler

A real local Euler–Heisenberg polynomial can provide a separate low-energy nonlinear-electrodynamic benchmark. It must carry its expansion domain, derivative scales and stress obtained from the same action. It is not the nonlocal causal current in a pair-producing state; high-field extrapolation of a low-order polynomial is not a strong-field proof. The original one-loop effective-action framework and generalizations are catalogued in Dunne's review; only its bibliographic scope was checked in this round. [Dunne, Heisenberg–Euler effective Lagrangians](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0406216).

A 2026 preprint treats electric, magnetic and dyonic Einstein–Euler–Heisenberg black holes. Its action-dependent horizon analysis does not by itself validate a polynomial EFT at arbitrarily small radius. This review inspected its introduction and stated scope, not its full numerical implementation; no theorem from it has been imported. [Luo et al., arXiv:2607.21938v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21938v1).

The relevant rigorous EFT lesson is narrower: a well-posed Einstein–Maxwell EFT formulation has been demonstrated in a weak-coupling regime after suitable field redefinitions and gauge choice. Weak coupling compares the higher-order principal-symbol contribution against the leading one; it is not guaranteed along an arbitrary strong-curvature evolution. [Davies and Reall, section 3.6 and strong-hyperbolicity result](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.05603v1).

No NLED production run was required for this cycle. Adding one merely to increase the experiment count would not close the missing charged-quantum stress construction. It remains a distinct controlled branch, rather than fabricated support for the full target.
