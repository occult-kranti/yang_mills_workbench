\subsection{Independent outer cutoffs}\label{r29:an2:r29-an2-independent-outer-cutoffs}}

Fix a nonempty finite complete-factor region F in \([-r,r]^{3}\) and \(n\ge r+2\). Let \(b_n=n(1,1,1)\), and compare the translated orthant cutoff

\(O_{n,L}=[-n,L-n]^3\), for every integer \(L\ge 2n\),

Extend each finite normal ground state by the onsite vacuum product to their common finite union (or a containing rectangle). These are grounds of their respective actual Hamiltonians plus decoupled exterior onsite terms.

Use exactly AN1's canonical reference on \(A_n\), retaining star anchors \([-n+1,n-1]^{3}\), with twice-eroded bulk \(D_n=[-n+2,n-2]^{3}\). Every difference term between either extended Hamiltonian and this canonical Hamiltonian is supported outside D\_n.~For an excluded anchor, some coordinate is \(\le -n\) or \(\ge n\); its star cannot reach D\_n.~Exterior onsite terms are disjoint too. The tensor-domain proof of AN1 therefore gives equality of bounded local commutators and the source ground-in-the-common-bulk condition for \textbf{every} allowed L,M.

\begin{equation}

\label{r29:an2:eq1}

\subsection{Centered marginals are Cauchy, not just a chosen subsequence}\label{r29:an2:r29-an2-centered-marginals-are-cauchy-not-just-a-chosen-subsequence}}

For any \(M,M'\ge n\), repeat the same common-bulk argument directly for \(C_M\) and \(C_{M'}\). It gives

\(\|\rho_{C_M,F}-\rho_{C_{M\prime},F}\|_1\le\varepsilon_n(F)\).

For fixed F, \(\epsilon_n\) tends to zero because \(C_2>0\). This is the Cauchy criterion for \textbf{all sufficiently large pairs}, rather than convergence along a diagonal. Completeness of the trace-class space gives a positive trace-one limit \(\rho_F\). Positivity and trace are preserved by trace-norm convergence.

For F subset F', finite marginals satisfy \(\operatorname{Tr}_{F\prime\setminus F}\rho_{C_M,F\prime}=\rho_{C_M,F}\). Partial trace is trace-norm contractive on trace-class operators, so the limits have the same compatibility. Define \(\omega_{\mathbb Z}(A)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_F A)\) for a bounded observable in F. Compatibility makes this independent of its chosen containing factor. It is positive, normalized and bounded by the operator norm on the local algebra; it uniquely extends to its norm closure. Each local restriction is normal by construction. One marginal alone would not define this state; the compatibility step is essential.
