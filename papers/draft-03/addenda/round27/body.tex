\section{Inherited question and research design}
The unchanged Draft01 compendium records the surviving history through Round26. It distinguishes a finite Gauss-invariant $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ graph, a homogeneous local-stability model and a canonical summable full-link family. Its 293-coordinate heat calculation, complete-support source estimates and Wilson endpoint formulas have different hypotheses. A shared parameter name does not identify their physical clocks, generators, states or domains. Round27 therefore begins with an information question: which independent parameters do the available readouts actually identify?

The panel comprised contemporary model-agent research lenses informed by selected Newton, Tesla, Jung/Pauli, Penrose and Feynman sources. They neither impersonate those people nor claim their endorsement. The advisor chose a contract; separate forward and reverse researchers derived and implemented it without reading the other's current solution before freezing; a separate skeptic tested scope and counterexamples. The mathematical work used the paired directions where useful. The geometry of a double spiral was an organizing analogy, not a proof premise.

The first selection, AI1, analyzes exact readout fibers. Its review motivates AI2's new physical probes. Only after AI2's review is the third contract selected, addressing the homogeneous nonlinear-update obstruction. This sequencing matters: the later question must respond to an observed limitation rather than retroactively redefining the first target. Literature searches, replays, repairs and skill tests do not count as additional physics loops.

\paragraph{Fixed units.} Throughout the canonical identification studies, $\alpha>0$ is an energy, $\hbar>0$ is a fixed action reference, and $E_*>0$ is a separately fixed physical energy scale. The dimensionless parameters satisfy $0<\eta,q<1$. Neither $q$ nor $1-q$ is asserted to be a continuum lattice spacing. The homogeneous study has its own declared dimensionless coupling and support weights. A support weight is proof bookkeeping, not a new force or physical constant.

\subsection{Endpoint coordinates and inherited functions}
Put $\epsilon=1-q$, $\beta=\alpha/\hbar$ and
\begin{equation}
\rho=\eta\epsilon^3,\qquad \lambda=\frac{\beta\rho}{84},\qquad
T_q(z)=\frac{z}{\beta\eta\epsilon^3},\qquad \theta=\frac{z}{84}=\lambda T_q(z).
\label{r27:eq:clock}
\end{equation}
$\beta$ and $\lambda$ have units of inverse time. The inherited endpoint theorem admits $0<z\le10^{-6}$, with $\alpha,\eta$ fixed and $q\uparrow1$. Physical observation time then diverges. The elementary plaquette, a prescribed six-cycle $X$, and a coherent six-cycle pair $S$ have respective endpoint functions
\begin{align}
f_0(\theta)&=1,\nonumber\\
f_X(\theta)&=\frac23+\frac14\cos(2\theta)+\frac1{12}\cos(2\sqrt3\theta),\nonumber\\
f_S(\theta)&=\frac13+\frac12\cos(2\theta)+\frac16\cos(2\sqrt3\theta)
+i\left[\frac14\sin(2\theta)+\frac1{4\sqrt3}\sin(2\sqrt3\theta)\right].
\label{r27:eq:endpoints}
\end{align}
The actual assertion is a demodulated, growing-time limit
$e^{iE_jT_q/\hbar}C_{j,q}(T_q)\to f_j(z/84)$, with
$E_0=3\alpha$ and $E_X=E_S=9\alpha/2$. It is not a derivative theorem at fixed physical time. Analytic extension of the finite functions in \eqref{r27:eq:endpoints} does not enlarge the physical theorem's interval.

At fixed scaled coordinate $z$, the entire limiting readout is independent of $\alpha,\eta,q$. It identifies none of them. Recorded physical timestamps supply the composite rate through the protocol; that information is additional to the endpoint ordinates. At fixed physical $t$, $q\uparrow1$ instead sends $z=\beta\eta(1-q)^3t$ to zero. Confusing these two limits would manufacture an inverse that the data do not provide.

\subsection{Exact fibers and conditional identification}
Define the physical-time \emph{surrogate}, explicitly distinct from an exact finite-$q$ correlation,
\begin{equation}
D_j(t;\alpha,\eta,q)=f_j(\lambda t),\qquad 0<84\lambda t\le10^{-6}.
\label{r27:eq:surrogate}
\end{equation}
Substitution proves that all readouts coincide whenever
\begin{equation}
\alpha\eta(1-q)^3=\text{constant}.\workstar
\label{r27:eq:fiber}
\end{equation}
Conversely, positivity of $\lambda$ and strict increase of the coherent imaginary part identify this one composite rate. Indeed
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{d\theta}\Impart f_S(\theta)
=\tfrac12\cos(2\theta)+\tfrac12\cos(2\sqrt3\theta)
\ge1-4\theta^2>0
\label{r27:eq:monotone}
\end{equation}
on the admitted interval. The inequality uses $\cos x\ge1-x^2/2$. The logarithmic sensitivity row is $(1,1,3)$ in coordinates $(\log\alpha,\log\eta,\log\epsilon)$; its rank is one. More samples of these same complete curves do not remove the two-dimensional nullspace.

For example, in fixed units the exact triples
$(\alpha/E_*,\eta,q)=(1,1/4,1/2)$, $(2,1/8,1/2)$ and $(1,2/27,1/4)$ all have $\alpha\eta(1-q)^3/E_*=1/32$. Compensation also persists arbitrarily near $q=1$. These rational countermodels disprove parameter uniqueness for \eqref{r27:eq:surrogate}, not equality of their actual finite-$q$ Hamiltonians.

Retain a calibrated carrier:
\begin{equation}
U_0(t)=e^{-3i\beta t},\quad
U_X(t)=e^{-(9/2)i\beta t}f_X(\lambda t),\quad
U_S(t)=e^{-(9/2)i\beta t}f_S(\lambda t).
\label{r27:eq:carrier}
\end{equation}
On an open physical-time interval, complete complex surrogate curves identify $\beta$ and $\lambda$. For instance $U_0'(0)=-3i\beta$ and
$U_S'(0)=i(\lambda-9\beta/2)$ as analytic identities of these surrogates. Their sensitivity matrix has rows $(1,0,0)$ and $(1,1,3)$, rank two, leaving the $\eta$--$q$ fiber. Known $q$ then gives
$\eta=84\lambda/[\beta(1-q)^3]$. This conclusion requires calibrated time, action and phase; it is not a measurement already implemented by the endpoint limit.

Sampling also matters. On a uniform grid $t=n\Delta$, the simultaneous change
\begin{equation}
\alpha'=\alpha+\frac{4\pi\hbar m}{3\Delta},\qquad
\eta'=\eta\alpha/\alpha',\qquad q'=q
\label{r27:eq:alias}
\end{equation}
preserves the slow rate and gives integer-$2\pi$ shifts to both carriers, when the transformed parameters remain admissible. A bounded prior frequency range or nonaliased design is needed. Intensity-only data lose the elementary phase entirely. The elementary demodulated probe remains constant despite nonzero variance; $X$, however, is informative through curvature despite its zero linear coefficient.

\subsection{Why the next loop must use actual finite-profile disks}
The actual retained finite-$q$ matrix contains different face-flip weights $q^4$ and $q^5$. With
\begin{equation}
p(q)=2+5q+5q^2+6q^3+3q^4,\qquad
c_S(q)=\frac{q^4(1+q)^2(1+q^2)}{32p(q)},
\label{r27:eq:slope}
