\subsection{Dictionary, coefficient box and label}
\label{sec:ax1-dictionary}
AL1 (Round29) fixes the uniform bulk dictionary in the convention it takes from
Bauer et al.\ (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11829}{arXiv:2307.11829}), with
$W_p=\tfrac12\tr U_p$ and the raw magnetic term $\lambda\sum_p(1-W_p)$
(\repo{research/round29/advisor/al1-gate.json}{AL1 gate}):
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 H_{\rm KS}=\alpha\sum_eC_e+\lambda\sum_p(1-W_p),\qquad
 \alpha=\frac{g^2}{2a},\quad \lambda=\frac2{g^2a},\quad
 \frac\lambda\alpha=\frac4{g^4},\\
 \nu=\lambda=\frac{\alpha\tau}{24}\ \Longrightarrow\ \tau=\frac{24\lambda}\alpha=\frac{96}{g^4}.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:ax1-dictionary}
\end{equation}
Dividing by $\delta=\alpha/8$ and dropping the scalar $\lambda N_p$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{H_{\rm KS}-\lambda N_p}{\delta}=8\sum_eC_e-\frac\tau3\sum_fW_f ,
 \label{eq:ax1-normalized}
\end{equation}
so every face, selected or omitted, enters with the coefficient $-\tau/3$ of
the I1.5 convention. The selected triple is therefore
$(\lambda_L,\mu_M,\lambda_R)/\alpha=(\tau/24,\tau/24,\tau/24)$. The admitted
box $|\lambda_L|,|\lambda_R|\le\alpha/2$, $|\mu_M|\le\alpha/8$ requires
$|\tau|/24\le1/8$, that is $|\tau|\le3$. This is AL1's bridge condition
$g^4\ge32$. At the cap,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{|\tau|}{24}=\frac1{2400000000},\qquad
 g^4=\frac{96}{10^{-8}}=9.6\times10^9\quad(g^2\approx9.80\times10^4),\qquad
 \frac{g^4}{32}=3\times10^8 .
 \label{eq:ax1-box}
\end{equation}
The model is labelled \emph{uniform Kogut--Susskind SU(2) at fixed spacing,
strong bare coupling}. It is never described as weak coupling or as a
continuum approach, and both checkers reject a label that uses either
phrase. No path with $g\to0$ stays inside the box, since the box
needs $g^4\ge32$; the cap sits at the opposite, strong-coupling end. The
physical gap $\alpha/16$ proved below equals $g^2/(32a)$.

For real $g$, $\tau=96/g^4>0$, so $\tau=-10^{-8}$ has no real-$g$ preimage. It
is the $U_E$ image of $+|\tau|$ (Section~\ref{sec:ax1-transfer}), and its
evaluation replays the same $|\tau|$ formula at the mirrored coupling. It is
neither a second Kogut--Susskind coupling nor a second confirmation. The
checkers verify $\tau g^4=96$, $\alpha\tau/24=\lambda$ and
$(\alpha/8)(\tau/3)=\lambda$ on three exact $(g^2,a)$ fixtures. Between them
they reject a non-uniform or ends-only triple, the zero triple, $\tau=4$
(bridge value $1/6>1/8$) and the normalized $\tau/3$ read in units of $\alpha$.

\subsection{Route B: Haar reference and single-factor groups}
\label{sec:ax1-routeb}
\paragraph{Why route B.}
The admitted chain needs a Haar reference in three places: the AV1 state
lemma (face vectors $W_f\Omega_0$ at energy 24 and the Haar moments of $P_R$),
the reset with on-site gap six, and the AW1 grading. Route A keeps the
selected faces on site,
$h_b^{\rm A}=8\sum_eC_e-(\tau/3)\sum_{g\in\mathrm{Sel}_b}W_g$. Its vacuum is
not Haar. For one selected face with $k=\tau/3$, the trial vector
$\Omega+(k/24)W_g\Omega$ has energy numerator $6t^2-kt/2$ at $t=k/24$, that is
$-k^2/96=-\tau^2/864<0$, below the Haar value $0$. Route A keeps the triple in
the box and $J=28|\tau|$, so the AM2 gate already covers the gap of this very
operator. That coverage is an inherited cross-check only: route A does not
supply the Haar vector $\psi=e^{-C}\Omega_0$, and claiming the Haar reference
for it is the rejected control \recid{selected_reference_not_haar}. Route B
keeps the $\tau$-independent Casimir sum on site and moves the selected faces
into the interaction:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat H'_N=\sum_{b\in\Lambda_N}h_b+\sum_{b+S\subset\Lambda_N}\phi_b
 +\sum_{b\in\Lambda_N}\psi_b,\qquad
 \phi_b=-\frac\tau3\sum_{f\in O_b}W_f,\quad
 \psi_b=-\frac\tau3\sum_{g\in\mathrm{Sel}_b}W_g ,
 \label{eq:ax1-routeb}
\end{equation}
with $h_b=8\sum_{e\text{ owned by }b}C_e$ as in \eqref{eq:dict-star}. All four
links of a selected face are owned by $b$, so $\psi_b$ has support $\{b\}$ and
every single-factor group of the box is retained. Routes A and B split the
same finite box operator up to on-site scalars. The route-B box therefore
keeps AQ1's inventory: whole stars inside $\Lambda_N$ and every selected face
of every factor in $\Lambda_N$.

\paragraph{Norms and the per-site sum.}
Since $\norm{W_f}\le1$,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\phi_b}\le21\cdot\frac{|\tau|}3=7|\tau|,\qquad
 \norm{\psi_b}\le3\cdot\frac{|\tau|}3=|\tau|,\qquad
 J'=\max_u\sum_{X\ni u}\norm{V_X}\le4\cdot7|\tau|+|\tau|=29|\tau| .
 \label{eq:ax1-J}
\end{equation}
Both norm bounds are attained at the identity configuration. A site lies in
exactly four stars, anchored at $u-S$ with the incoming stars included, and in
exactly one single-factor group. The reverse checker recomputes $J'$ from the
explicit group list of $\Lambda_3$: the bulk maximum $29$ is attained at the
origin, and boundary sites are lower. Three other groupings are rejected:
charging the selected faces in both a star and a single group (a duplicate
face, per-site sum $33|\tau|$), merging them into the star ($32|\tau|$) and
dropping them ($28|\tau|$, the zero-selected model). A per-face grouping has
the smaller sum $52|\tau|/3$, but it changes the interaction decomposition and
the boundary prescription; the reverse producer discloses it and does not use

\paragraph{No face is charged twice.}

\paragraph{Support and termination.}
Stars have four sites and single groups one. Two creations meeting the same

\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{ad}_{C_1}\cdots\mathrm{ad}_{C_k}(V_X)=0\quad(k>2|X|),\qquad
 \max_X2|X|=8 .
 \label{eq:ax1-termination}
\end{equation}

$|X|\le p$ only as an upper bound: at most $2^{|X|}$ output sets,
$\sum_{I\cap X\ne\varnothing}\norm{c_I}\le|X|\,\norm c_a$ and
$|I_j|\le|M|+|X|$. With $p=4$, single-site groups satisfy AM2's majorant

\begin{equation}
 \norm{L_k(c_1,\ldots,c_k)}_a\le J'\,16\cdot8^k\Bigl(1+\frac{5k}4\Bigr)

and $G(t)=16e^{8t}(1+10t)$ of \eqref{eq:dict-majorant} is unchanged. This

\label{sec:ax1-contraction}

 \qquad
 2J_0'G'(R_a)<2\cdot\frac{29}{10^8}\cdot352=\frac{319}{1562500}<1 .
 \label{eq:ax1-contraction}
\end{equation}
The Lipschitz constant is below $319/3125000$. The self-map inequality alone

\paragraph{On-site theorem.}
By Peter--Weyl, $C_e$ acts as $j(j+1)$ on the spin-$j$ block, and its kernel
is the constants. So $h_b=8\sum_eC_e\ge0$ has the one-dimensional kernel
spanned by the Haar vector $\Omega_b$, and its next eigenvalue is
$8\cdot\tfrac34=6$:
\begin{equation}
 h_b\ge6\,Q_b\ge Q_b,\qquad P_b=|\Omega_b\rangle\langle\Omega_b|,\qquad
 Q_b=I-P_b .
 \label{eq:ax1-onsite}
\end{equation}
The Casimirs are bi-invariant, $\Omega_b$ is constant and every $W_f$ is gauge
invariant. The forward checker confirms the last point on exact
rational-quaternion holonomies for six faces, among them $W$ and selected
faces.

\begin{proposition}[route B, uniform model]
\label{prop:ax1-gap}
Take any nonempty finite complete-factor volume with route-B retention: whole
stars inside the volume and every single-factor group. For either sign of
$|\tau|\le10^{-8}$ the untruncated Hamiltonian has a unique ground
$\psi=e^{-C}\Omega_0$ with $\norm c_a\le1/64$, and
\[
 E_1-E_0\ge\tfrac12\quad\text{(normalized)},\qquad
 \Delta_{\rm phys}\ge\frac\alpha8\cdot\frac12=\frac\alpha{16}=\frac{g^2}{32a}.
\]
The ground is gauge invariant, and the physical subspace contains a nonzero
excited sector.
\end{proposition}
The proof is AM2 Sections~2--6 with the new constants. It uses $h_x\ge Q_x$
\eqref{eq:ax1-onsite}; supports of at most four sites with termination at
order eight \eqref{eq:ax1-termination}; $J\le J'\le J_0'$ in every finite
volume, because boundary per-site sums never exceed the bulk value (checked
for $N=1,2,3$); the self-map and the Banach contraction of
\eqref{eq:ax1-contraction}; the shifted-resolvent exclusion
$\norm b_a\le2J_0'G'(R_a)\norm b_a<\norm b_a$ for real $|z|<\tfrac12$; and
continuity of the isolated branch along the coupling from $0$. AM2's cutoff
removal applies verbatim, because $h_b$ is a Casimir sum on a compact group
with compact resolvent. The two producers prove the excited sector
differently. The forward producer uses a selected face $g$: $\Omega_0$ and
$2W_g\Omega_0$ are orthonormal gauge-invariant vectors, since
$\mathbb E[W_g]=0$ and $\mathbb E[W_g^2]=\tfrac14$, so the physical subspace
has dimension at least two. The reverse producer uses the strict positivity of
the ground, a Schr\"odinger ground of a Laplacian plus a bounded real
potential on a connected compact manifold, so
$(W_g-\langle W_g\rangle)\Omega_H$ is nonzero, gauge invariant and orthogonal
to the ground. The skeptic accepts both arguments.

\begin{equation}
 \omega_N(h_R)\le2\bigl(7\cdot7|\tau|+2\cdot|\tau|\bigr)=102|\tau| .
 \label{eq:ax1-reset}
\end{equation}
The reverse producer rejects $100|\tau|$ as a miscount. The review notes that

groups lie inside $R$ and have Haar mean $0$. The admitted budget stays

$Q_0\otimes I+I\otimes Q_{e_z}-(I-P_0\otimes P_{e_z})=Q_0\otimes Q_{e_z}\ge0$

\subsection{Face counts: 52 per factor and 88 for the cover}
\label{sec:ax1-counts}
Every retained face $f$, omitted or selected, gives a vector $W_f\Omega_0$
with spin $\tfrac12$ on its four links, normalized $H_0$-energy
$8\cdot4\cdot\tfrac34=24$ and norm $\tfrac12$, in the sector of its owner set
$M_f$. Distinct faces share at most one link, so the vectors are orthogonal;
the forward checker verifies this over all pairs of the 88 faces meeting $R$.
A selected face has $M_g=\{b\}$. The first-order collection
\eqref{eq:av1-first} therefore keeps its form in route B:
\begin{equation}
 c'^{(1)}_M=-\frac\tau{72}\sum_{f:\,M_f=M}W_f\Omega_0,\qquad
 \norm{c'^{(1)}_M}=\frac{|\tau|\sqrt{n_M}}{144},\qquad
 \norm{c'^{(1)}_{\{b\}}}=\frac{\sqrt3\,|\tau|}{144}\ne0 .
 \label{eq:ax1-first}
\end{equation}
The single-site creations are new. In the zero-selected model every
first-order support had at least two sites. In route B the supports $\{0\}$
and $\{e_z\}$ are nonzero at first order, meet $R$ and enter the two-creation
term.

The counts are derived from the 24-class encoding as in \eqref{eq:av1-49}. A
face of class $k$ anchored at $b$ has $u$ in its owner set iff $b=u-d$ with
$d\in K_k$. The offset $0$ lies in all 24 classes, and the selected classes
contain only $0$. Hence
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \#\{f:\ u\in M_f\}=\sum_{d\in S}\#\{k:\ d\in K_k\}=24+4+8+16=52=49+3,\\
 \#\{f:\ M_f\cap R\ne\varnothing\}=52+52-16=88 .
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:ax1-52}
\end{equation}
A site lies in 16 owner sets, with multiplicities
$\{1\times5,2\times3,3\times3,4\times3,10\times2\}$; relative to
Table~\ref{tab:av1-owners} the new set is $\{u\}$ with $n=3$. Sixteen faces
lie inside $R$: the 10 omitted faces with owner set $R$ and the 6 selected
faces. Sixteen faces have owner sets containing both sites, and
$16+16-3=29$ owner sets meet $R$. The omitted-only sub-table reproduces AV1's
counts $(49,15,82,10)$. A brute-force fine-lattice enumeration without the
class table reproduces 52, all 16 multiplicities and 88. Finite-box counts
never exceed the bulk values (checked for $N=1,2,3$).

\subsection{Transfer of the flip identity and of \texorpdfstring{$+\tau/144$}{+tau/144}}
\label{sec:ax1-transfer}
\paragraph{Every coefficient is tied to $\tau$.}
In the uniform model every face term of \eqref{eq:ax1-routeb} is
$-(\tau/3)W_f$, selected faces included, and $h_b$ does not depend on $\tau$.
In AW1's notation the uniform Hamiltonian is $H_N(\tau,\kappa(\tau))$ with
$\kappa(\tau)=(\tau/24)(1,1,1)$ in units of $\alpha$, an odd function of
$\tau$. A mixed convention is the rejected control
\recid{uniform_sign_convention}: selected faces with the opposite sign, with
$\tau/24$ read in $\delta$ units, or with zero.

\paragraph{The flip identity, proved.}
Let $U_E=\prod_{e\in E\cap\Lambda_N}\Gamma_e$ with the flip set $E$ of
\eqref{eq:aw1-flipset}. Each $\Gamma_e$ acts as $(-1)^{2j}$ on the spin-$j$
block and, because $-1$ is central, commutes with left and right
translations. So $U_E$ commutes with every $C_e$, with $h_b$, $P_b$ and every
spectral cutoff $1_{[0,L]}(h_b)$, and with every endpoint gauge action, and it
fixes $\Omega_0$. Every plaquette of $\mathbb Z^3$ meets $E$ in one or three
links (Section~\ref{sec:aw1-flip}), the selected faces included, so
$U_EW_fU_E^*=-W_f$ for every retained face. Hence, in every centered box and
at every cutoff,
\begin{equation}
 U_E\,\widehat H'_N(\tau)\,U_E^*=\widehat H'_N(-\tau),\qquad
 U_E\,Q_L\widehat H'_N(\tau)Q_L\,U_E^*=Q_L\,\widehat H'_N(-\tau)\,Q_L .
 \label{eq:ax1-flip}
\end{equation}
Two differences from \eqref{eq:aw1-flip-identity} follow. The patterned
identity $(\tau,\kappa)\mapsto(-\tau,-\kappa)$ becomes $\tau\mapsto-\tau$,
because $\kappa$ is tied to $\tau$. And neither the inherited strip-operator
form nor a $Q_L\to Q_L'$ step is needed, because the reference and its cutoffs
are Haar and Casimir objects that $U_E$ fixes; AW1's modified flip set $E^*$
is not needed either. The odd intersection is re-enumerated with the selected
classes. The forward checker covers the 24 (orientation, base-parity) classes,
the 24 anchored classes at 27 anchors (648 faces) and 3600 plaquettes. The
reverse checker covers the 24 residue cases, the 6591 plaquettes with base
point in $[-6,6]^3$ (3042 meeting $E$ once, 3549 three times, none evenly)
and all 1719 faces of $\Lambda_2$ (1344 omitted and 375 selected). The Wilson
face meets $E$ in three links, and exact rational-quaternion holonomies show
that the traces flip.

\paragraph{Consequences.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:ax1-gap} gives a unique ground at both signs, so
$U_E\psi_N(\tau)\propto\psi_N(-\tau)$. With $U_E\Omega_0=\Omega_0$ and the
intermediate normalization $\langle\Omega_0,\psi\rangle=1$, the creations obey
$c_I(-\tau)=U_Ec_I(\tau)$. In every box and at every cutoff,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \omega_{N,-\tau}(W)=-\omega_{N,\tau}(W),\qquad
 \omega_{N,-\tau}(W^2)=\omega_{N,\tau}(W^2),\\
 C_{N,-\tau}=C_{N,\tau},\qquad c_{N,-\tau}=c_{N,\tau},\qquad
 \rho_{N,R}(-\tau)=U_E\,\rho_{N,R}(\tau)\,U_E^* .
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:ax1-covariance}
\end{equation}
Since $U_EP_RU_E^*=P_R$, $\norm{\rho_{N,R}-P_R}_1$ is exactly even in $\tau$.
For AQ limits, $\mathcal S(-\tau)=\mathcal S(\tau)\circ\alpha_E$ as whole sets
of subsequential limits, as in \eqref{eq:aw1-aq-sets}; individual states at
$\pm\tau$ are paired only along a common subsequence. Oddness gives no
$O(\tau^3)$ remainder.

verbatim. For every face $f$ meeting $R$, selected or omitted,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E[W^2W_f]=0,\qquad \mathbb E[WW_f]=\tfrac14\,\delta_{f,W},\qquad
 P_{24}\,V'\,W\Omega_0=0 ,
 \label{eq:ax1-parity}
\end{equation}
where $V'$ is the route-B interaction. On $\Lambda_2$ the odd-link sets
$W\triangle f$ have sizes $0$, $6$ and $8$, for $1$, $12$ and $1706$ faces.
The twelve are the ten omitted faces that share a link with $W$ and the two
selected faces above. An energy-24 vector has exactly four odd links, so no
face maps $W\Omega_0$ into the energy-24 multiplet. Also
$\mathbb E[W_gW_fW_h]=0$ for all 117\,480 multisets of three faces meeting
$R$, because each face meets $E$ oddly; so the invariant multiplet has zero
first-order splitting. The state, Duhamel, energy and vector-centring terms of
Section~\ref{sec:aw1-parity} therefore vanish separately, and the finite-box
parity theorem \eqref{eq:aw1-parity-conclusion} transfers with its
qualifications: finite boxes, a $\tau^2$ constant for $C_N$ and $c_N$ that is
explicitly unbounded, and only evenness for AQ limits.

\paragraph{The first-order Wilson mean.}
The first-order vector now sums over all retained faces, omitted and selected.
By Kato analyticity in each box ($V'$ bounded, a simple isolated ground with
gap at least $\tfrac12$),
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \psi^{(1)}=-c'^{(1)}=\frac\tau{72}\sum_fW_f\Omega_0,\\
 \omega_{N,\tau}(W)=2\,\mathrm{Re}\langle W\Omega_0,\psi^{(1)}\rangle+O_N(\tau^2)
 =\frac\tau{36}\sum_f\mathbb E[WW_f]+O_N(\tau^2)
 =+\frac\tau{144}+O_N(\tau^2).
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:ax1-mean}
\end{equation}
Only $f=W$ contributes. This is checked face by face over the 88 faces meeting
$R$, and the two selected faces that share a link with $W$ contribute exactly
zero. In the product-split reading of \eqref{eq:aw1-overlap}, only $c_R$
overlaps $W\Omega_R$. Its first-order part is unchanged, because the ten faces
with owner set $R$ are all omitted. The new single-site creations
$c_{\{0\}}$ and $c_{\{e_z\}}$ are orthogonal to $W\Omega_R$, because $W$
carries one spin-$\tfrac12$ factor on a link of each site. The first-order
values are $\pm1/14400000000$ at $\tau=\pm10^{-8}$, as in
\eqref{eq:aw1-mean}. Charging the selected faces at first order, and a zero
first-order mean in the uniform model, are rejected mutations. The
Rayleigh--Schr\"odinger series of the compression \eqref{eq:ax1-compression}
reproduces $1/144$, with the $W_g$ direction contributing zero.
