\section{Sub-round 5: the continuum trajectory and a finite graph}
\label{sec:continuum}

The fifth Round31 roadmap goal, ``Establish a continuum matching
trajectory'', asks for a matched lattice family connected to a nontrivial
four-dimensional continuum theory with the required axioms and a positive
physical gap. Its recorded missing premise reads ``A regulator/coupling
trajectory, physical scale control, continuum limits and full reconstruction
hypotheses remain unproved''
(\repo{research/round31/advisor/roadmap.json}{Round31 roadmap}). Its proposed
first test is to ``State the regulator and coupling dictionary, then identify
one necessary uniform estimate that the present fixed-lattice family actually
supplies or fails to supply''. Sub-round~5 carries out that first test and
nothing more. AZ1 is a statement loop: it names a trajectory as a hypothesis,
applies the AL1 dictionary along it, cites the one necessary estimate that the
uniform Kogut--Susskind model of Section~\ref{sec:ax1} supplies, and shows
exactly where that estimate stops. AZ2 computes exact first- and
second-order Wilson coefficients on the Round11 two-plaquette graph, a
different finite model, with sign-certified enclosures that transfer nothing
to $\mathbb Z^3$ (Section~\ref{sec:az2}).
Neither investigation constructs a continuum theory, and no number in this
section is a fraction of the continuum problem.

\subsubsection{Trajectory, dictionary and admitted regime}
\label{sec:az1-dictionary}
\paragraph{The named trajectory.}
Contract item~1 asks for a trajectory $(a_n,g_n)$ with $g_n\to0$ as
$a_n\to0$, named as a hypothesis of asymptotic-freedom form and not derived.
The producer states it as
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{H}_{\rm AF}:\quad(a_n,g_n),\ n\ge1,\qquad a_n\to0,\quad g_n\to0,\qquad
 g(a)^2\sim\frac1{2b_0\log(1/(a\Lambda))}\quad(a\to0),
 \label{eq:az1-trajectory}
\end{equation}
for some $b_0>0$ and scale $\Lambda$. No beta function is computed, no
numerical $b_0$ is used or source-audited, and no sequence is claimed to be

\recid{trajectory_named} rejects the trajectory marked derived, an unnamed

\paragraph{The dictionary.}

 \label{eq:az1-dictionary}
\end{equation}

\end{equation}

\subsubsection{The one estimate supplied}
\label{sec:az1-estimate}
\paragraph{The estimate, cited.}

\begin{equation}
 \Delta\ge\frac\alpha{16}=\frac\alpha8\cdot\frac12=\frac{g^2}{32a}
 \qquad\text{for }g^4\ge9.6\times10^9,\ \text{at fixed }a .
 \label{eq:az1-gap}
\end{equation}
This is one lower bound, the same for every finite complete-factor volume, so

\paragraph{The admitted regime cannot host a finite-mass limit.}
The certified gap is more than three thousand times the inverse spacing, so
it is a cutoff-scale quantity. A trajectory with $a_n\to0$ that stayed inside
the admitted regime would have a certified $\Delta_n\ge3061/a_n$, which
diverges in units of the fixed $E_\star$. At $a_n=1/10$, $1/100$, $1/1000$ and
$1/10000$~fm the certified lower ends are about $3.0618621\times10^4$,
$3.0618621\times10^5$, $3.0618621\times10^6$ and
$3.0618621\times10^7$~fm$^{-1}$. A finite physical gap along $a_n\to0$
requires $a_n\Delta_n\to0$, which is impossible inside the admitted regime,
where $a_n\Delta_n>3061$ for every $n$. Equivalently, $(\alpha a)^2=g^4/4\ge
2.4\times10^9$, so the electric scale is at least $20000\sqrt6/a$. This is
the physical-scale face of the same failure point (row~R3 of
Table~\ref{tab:az1-requirements}). The skeptic adds a conditional remark,
which is not a premise and was not required. If $\Delta_n\to m<\infty$ and
$g_n^2/a_n\to\infty$, as on any trajectory of asymptotic-freedom form, then
$\Delta_n\ge g_n^2/(32a_n)$ must fail for all large $n$. The form of the
estimate, and not only its proof, is then incompatible with the hypothesized
limit.

\subsubsection{The failure point}
\label{sec:az1-failure}
\paragraph{Every trajectory with \texorpdfstring{$g_n\to0$}{g\_n -> 0} leaves the regime.}
The failure is elementary. If $g_n\to0$, then for every threshold $T>0$ there
is an index $n_0$ with $g_n^4<T$ for every $n\ge n_0$; that is the definition
of the limit. With $T=9.6\times10^9$, every trajectory with $g_n\to0$ leaves
the admitted regime at a finite index. From that index on
$\tau_n=96/g_n^4>10^{-8}$, the AM2/AX1 contraction is no longer available as
admitted, and the family supplies no gap estimate at the spacing $a_n$ at all.
In particular it supplies no estimate uniform along $a_n\to0$ on that
trajectory. With $T=32$, the AL1 bridge condition $g^4\ge32$, equivalently
$r=4/g^4\le1/8$ or $\tau\le3$, also fails from a finite index on. The I1
omitted-interaction conditions $g^4>672/c_1$ and $g^4>1344c_2$, with the
source constants $c_1(S)$ and $c_2(S)$ unevaluated, likewise fail from a
finite index on for any fixed positive values (HNM-AL1.3).

\paragraph{The scope \texorpdfstring{$g_n\to0$}{g\_n -> 0} is essential.}
At fixed $g$ in the admitted regime the estimate survives any change of
spacing. With $g^2=10^5$ and $a_n=1/n$, the bound $g^2/(32a_n)=3125n$ holds at
every $n$ and increases. That sequence is common rescaling: $a_n\Delta\ge3125$
stays fixed, and in units of $E_\star$ the bound diverges like $1/a_n$, so it
gives no finite-mass limit either. A negated statement about estimates along
$a_n\to0$ is therefore true only on a trajectory with $g_n\to0$. Every such
statement in this subsection is scoped that way, as the gate statement is
(``\texttt{on a trajectory with g\_n->0}''; skeptic reading~9, review
finding~N3).

\paragraph{The AL1 frozen path.}
On AL1's explicit test path $a_n=a_0/n$, $g_n^2=1/n$, the bridge fails at
every $n\ge1$: $g_n^4=1/n^2\le1<32$ and $\tau_n=96n^2$, while
$\alpha_na_0=1/2$ stays fixed. AL1 records it as ``an explicit algebraic test
path, not a claim that this is the physically correct renormalization
trajectory''. The checker re-verifies it for $n=1,\ldots,1000$. The contract's
``the AL1 bridge condition $g^4\ge32$ also fails'' therefore means ``fails
from a finite index on'' on a trajectory with $g_n\to0$, and ``fails at every
$n$'' only on the AL1 path (defect~D5).

\paragraph{The cap is not a sharp threshold.}
$10^{-8}$ is the preregistered cap at which the AM2 and AX1 certificates are
admitted. The AM2 reverse report states that it does not establish a sharp
threshold, and the contract's ``the AM2 contraction needs
$\tau\le10^{-8}$'' is read as ``is admitted only for'' (defect~D3, skeptic
reading~7). The contraction inequalities alone would allow the route radii
$|\tau|\le7/274688$ for the self-map, about 2548.3 times the cap
($g^4\ge26370048/7\approx3.767\times10^6$), and $|\tau|<1/20416$ for the
exclusion ($g^4>1959936$); see Section~\ref{sec:ax1-contraction}. No gate
froze or reviewed them. They are not admitted regimes, because the rest of
the AX1 chain was evaluated only at the cap. Nor do they change the
conclusion. The contraction's smallness parameter is
$J'=29|\tau|=2784/g^4$, so any finite contraction radius bounds $g^4$ from
below. The declared toy trajectory leaves the two route radii at $n^*=8$ and
$n^*=9$ (Table~\ref{tab:az1-extras}).

\paragraph{Failure of a certificate, not of the gap.}
Nothing here says that the gap closes where the certificate stops. The AL1
gate records its obstruction as ``failure of a specified sufficient

\subsubsection{Bridge obstruction, common rescaling and continuum requirements}
\label{sec:az1-requirements}
\paragraph{The AL1 bridge obstruction.}
Contract item~4 asks for it to be retained. In AL1 the selected bridge needs
$r=4/g^4\le1/8$, that is $g^4\ge32$ or $\tau\le3$, and the ends need
$r\le1/2$, that is $g^4\ge8$ (HNM-AL1.3). In the uniform model this is the
condition $|\tau|/24\le1/8$ on the uniform selected triple
(Section~\ref{sec:ax1-dictionary}). The AL1 gate records that its frozen path
violates the bridge for every $n\ge1$, and that every path with $g\to0$
eventually violates it. The R18-B2 bound $g^4\ge32/3$ concerned a different
finite-graph coefficient box. Its substitution is a rejected mutation, and
dropping the bridge is rejected by \recid{bridge_obstruction_retained}.

\paragraph{Common rescaling fails.}
The coupling $\tau=24\lambda/\alpha$ depends on $g$ alone. Exactly, for every
$c>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \tau(ca)=\tau(a),\qquad \alpha(ca)=\frac{\alpha(a)}c,\qquad
 \lambda(ca)=\frac{\lambda(a)}c,\qquad
 \frac{g^2}{32\,ca}=\frac1c\cdot\frac{g^2}{32a},\qquad
 r(c\alpha,c\lambda)=r .
 \label{eq:az1-rescaling}
\end{equation}
In the skeptic's homogeneity degrees in $(g^2,a)$, $\alpha$ has degree
$(1,-1)$, $\lambda$ has $(-1,-1)$, $\tau$ and $r$ have $(-2,0)$, the gap has
$(1,-1)$ and the lattice gap has $(1,0)$. The producer's exponents in $g$ are
twice the degrees in $g^2$. A common rescaling
$(\alpha,\lambda)\to(s\alpha,s\lambda)$ is exactly $a\to a/s$ at fixed $g$.
Changing the spacing at fixed $g$, changing the energy unit or rescaling the
whole Hamiltonian therefore moves the gap $g^2/(32a)$ but never moves $\tau$
or $r$. No admission ratio moves, and only $g$ decides whether a point lies in
the admitted regime or satisfies the bridge, so common rescaling can neither
repair nor break the cap or the bridge. The AL1 gate selected a later loop to
test common scale changes, but that result is not a shared premise of AZ1,
and the invariance is derived here from the AL1 dictionary alone
(defect~D4). An independent magnetic multiplier $\lambda\to s\lambda$ at fixed
$\alpha$ is a change of model. It changes $g^4$ to $g^4/s$, and the bridge
then needs $s\le g^4/32$. On the declared toy $s_{\max}<1$ from $n=132$ on,
about $0.98816$ at $n=132$, and $s_{\max}\to0$ as $g\to0$ (skeptic).

where $K$ is the sum of the seven link Casimirs $j(j+1)$, both faces carry the
same coupling, and $\rho_{11}=1$ is Round11's shared-link weight, written
$\rho_{11}$ here to keep it apart from the residual norm $\rho$. The constant
$-2\tfg$ moves no state. For $\tfg<0$ the Round11 solver's interface, which
requires $\lambda\ge0$, does not apply; every bound below uses only
$\norm{W_1+W_2}\le2$ and needs no sign. The sign follows I1.5: the star
operator is $\phi_b=-(\tau/3)\sum W_f$ in units of $\delta$, that is
$-(\tau/24)W_f$ in units of $\alpha$, so the first-order mean is positive for
positive coupling. A static ground-state mean does not depend on $\alpha$,
and the clock of \eqref{eq:dict-clock} is named only. The normalization
dictionary of the contract is
\begin{equation}
 \tfg=\frac\tau{24},
 \label{eq:az2-dictionary}
\end{equation}
because the $\mathbb Z^3$ face coefficient in units of $\alpha$ is
$\nu/\alpha=\tau/24$ (I1) and the graph's coupling multiplies one face each.

\paragraph{The kinetic operator.}
In quotient coordinates $K=3C_U+3C_V+S$: $C_U$ acts on $(x,z)$, $C_V$ on
$(y,z)$, and the shared-link term $S$, with derivative $L_U-R_V$, on $(x,y)$.
The checker reproduces the Round11 K5 identities exactly:
\begin{equation}
 K1=0,\qquad Kx=3x,\qquad Ky=3y,\qquad Kz=\tfrac92z,\qquad
 K(xy)=\tfrac{13}2xy-\tfrac12z .
 \label{eq:az2-k5}
\end{equation}
The last identity is the shared-link cross term. The span of $x$ and $y$ is
the exact eigenspace of $K$ at its free gap $3$.

\subsubsection{Basis, cutoff and the first omitted shell}
\label{sec:az2-basis}
\paragraph{Why the complete residual is finite.}
$P_D$ is spanned by the monomials $x^ay^bz^c$ with $a+b+c\le D$, in Round11
order, of dimension $\binom{D+3}3$: 84 at $D=6$ and 165 at $D=8$. $K$ is
triangular in degree, with diagonal
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon(a,b,c)=3j(j+1)+3k(k+1)+\ell(\ell+1),\qquad
 j=\frac{a+c}2,\quad k=\frac{b+c}2,\quad \ell=\frac{a+b}2
 \label{eq:az2-eps}
\end{equation}
(Round11 E2). $K$ preserves every $P_d$, and multiplication by $x$ or $y$
raises the degree by at most one. Hence $H_{\rm FG}v\in P_{D+1}$ for every
$v\in P_D$: the complete full-space residual is a finite polynomial, computed
exactly, and nothing lies beyond shell $D+1$. $D$ is the total polynomial
degree. No per-link $j_{\max}$ is well-posed for this basis, as the contract
states.

\paragraph{Exactly orthogonal sectors.}
For a monomial $m_s$ of degree $D+1$, $Q_Dm_s=m_s-P_Dm_s$ is a single
spin-network sector $(j,k,\ell)$ of shell $D+1$ by Peter--Weyl on each link:
$m_s$ has link spins at most $(j,k,\ell)$ coordinatewise, and the monomials of
a down-closed label set span exactly the spin networks with those labels. The
checker verifies orthogonality exactly: the Schur complement
$G_{D+1,D+1}-B^{\mathsf T}G_D^{-1}B$ has zero off-diagonal entries, for 36
sectors and 630 pairs at $D=6$ and 55 sectors and 1485 pairs at $D=8$; the
top retained shells, with 28 and 45 sectors, pass the same test. The Gram
matrix is block diagonal in the four link-flip parity classes (blocks
24/20/20/20 at $D=6$ and 45/40/40/40 at $D=8$), and its exact
$LDL^{\mathsf T}$ has all pivots positive. Every omitted norm is therefore an
exact sum of sector weights.

\paragraph{Per-link spins and the product channel.}
The degree-two vertices force one spin along each three-link path: $j$ on
$h_1$, $v_L$ and $h_3$; $k$ on $h_2$, $v_R$ and $h_4$; and $\ell$ on the
shared link $v_M$. A shell-$(D+1)$ sector exceeds the retained per-link
maximum $D/2$ on the $j$ links if and only if $b=0$, on the $k$ links if and
only if $a=0$, and on $v_M$ if and only if $c=0$. The \emph{product channel}
is the set of sectors with $a,b,c\ge1$: every link spin is within $D/2$, yet
the joint degree is omitted, so these states are not implied by any per-link
item. The exact thresholds are minima of $\varepsilon$ over all omitted
shells. The enumeration stops once the Round11 E3 shell minimum
$m_d=\tfrac58d^2+2d+\tfrac38(d\bmod2)$, which increases with $d$, exceeds
every class minimum (Table~\ref{tab:az2-thresholds}). Both joint thresholds
equal the contract's 45 and 69 and the E3 formula, $m_7=45$ and $m_9=69$. The
lowest omitted state excites the shared link. The skeptic reaches the same
floors independently: $\varepsilon$ increases strictly in each exponent, so
no later shell undercuts shell $D+1$ and $QKQ\ge m_{D+1}Q$ on the whole
omitted space.

\subsubsection{The five-part ledger and the enclosure theorem}
\label{sec:az2-ledger}
\paragraph{The complete residual.}
Take a rational Ritz vector $v\in P_D$, its unit vector $\hat v$, its Rayleigh
quotient $\mu$ and its complete residual $r=(H_{\rm FG}-\mu)\hat v\in P_{D+1}$.
Then
\begin{equation}
 \rho^2=\norm{H_{\rm FG}\hat v}^2-\mu^2=\rho_P^2+\rho_Q^2,\qquad
 \rho_Q^2=\tfg^2\,\norm{Q_D(x+y)\hat v}^2 ,
 \label{eq:az2-residual}
\end{equation}
exactly, where $\rho_P$ is the retained part and $\rho_Q$ the omitted part.
Contract item~1 asks for five separately itemized parts, and a single nonzero
omitted matrix element, it adds, proves leakage but does not bound it. The
producer itemizes all five at each of the 14 points (the free reference and
the twelve grid points, at both cutoffs):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{Per-link representation tail}, one row for each of the seven
 links: the retained maximum spin $D/2$, the exact threshold of the states
 whose spin on that link exceeds $D/2$, the exact weight of $r$ in those
 sectors, the link's boundary layer in $\hat v$ (the shell-$D$ sectors with
 spin exactly $D/2$) and the certified per-link tail of the true ground state
 (item~7 below).
\item \emph{Joint product channel across all seven links}: $\rho_Q^2$ as the
 exact sum over all shell-$(D+1)$ sectors, the product-channel weight and its
 threshold, the corner overlaps between per-link classes, and the $x$- and
 $y$-face channels with their interference $2XY$. That interference is
 positive, so the two face channels are not orthogonal and may not be added
 in quadrature. The checker verifies exactly that the per-link classes plus
 the product channel minus the corners equal $\rho_Q^2$, and it reports the
 certified joint tail $T_{\rm joint}$ of the true ground state.
\item \emph{Gauge-invariant projection}: exactly~0. Every basis element is a
 polynomial in traces of closed holonomies, invariant under all six vertex
 actions, and $H_{\rm FG}$ commutes with the gauge action. An exact
 rational-quaternion fixture checks the invariance: seven rational unit
 quaternions give $(x,y,z)=(13/18,1/6,-4/27)$, unchanged under four sets of
 six vertex transformations, while the open three-link path
 $h_3^{-1}v_L^{-1}h_1$ changes; $z$ does not depend on $v_M$, and the $v_M$
 flip maps $(x,y,z)$ to $(-x,-y,z)$.
\item \emph{Ritz and eigenvector residual}: $\rho_P^2$, which is below
 $5\times10^{-199}$ everywhere because the Ritz proposal is rounded at
 $10^{-100}$; $\rho^2$; the certified gap; and the angle bounds $s_A$ and
 $s_B$.
\item \emph{Arithmetic}: exact \sourcepath{Fraction} arithmetic throughout.
 The only non-rational steps are square roots, rounded upward by integer
 square roots with width at most $10^{-55}$. Exported ends are rounded
 outward to denominators $10^{45}$, which contributes at most about
 $2\times10^{-45}$ of each width, included. The 110-digit decimal inverse
 iteration only proposes $v$; its rounding enters, measured exactly, in
 item~(d).
\end{enumerate}
The preregistered error terms map onto the ledger as \recid{truncation_jmax}
$\to$ (a)+(b)+(c), the $D$-cutoff truncation (producer defect~D1);
\recid{eigenvector_residual} $\to$ (d); and \recid{arithmetic} $\to$ (e). The
skeptic's contract review read the map the same way, with (c) reported as
not applicable with its reason; the producer reports it as exactly~0 with
the same reason.

\paragraph{The enclosure theorem.}
Write $b=3-2|\tfg|$. The certificate chains seven items; Round11 T1--T5 and
E3--E4 are inherited, and Kato, Rayleigh--Ritz, Weyl, Temple, Eckart and
Davis--Kahan are cited \cite{standard}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \emph{Complete residual.} For $v\in P_D$, $r\in P_{D+1}$ and
 \eqref{eq:az2-residual} is exact.
\item \emph{Certified gap (Weyl).}
 $E_1(H_{\rm FG})\ge E_1(K)-\norm{\tfg(W_1+W_2)}\ge3-2|\tfg|=b$, where
 $E_1(K)=3$ comes from the complete free spectrum (Round11 E3, with
 $\mathrm{span}\{x,y\}$ at~3), and $E_0\le\mu$ by Rayleigh--Ritz. At every
 point $\mu<b$, so the ground state is simple and $E_1-E_0\ge b-\mu$, which
 ranges from 2.80 to 3.00.
\item \emph{Davis--Kahan and Temple.} The angle $\theta$ between $\hat v$ and
 the ground state satisfies $\sin\theta\le\rho/(b-\mu)=:s_A$, and
 $E_0\ge\mu-\rho^2/(b-\mu)$.
\item \emph{Round11 tail comparison.} Put $t=t_\ell-2|\tfg|$, so that
 $Q_DH_{\rm FG}Q_D\ge t\,Q_D$ (E4), and take $R=b<t$. Young's inequality gives
 $H_{\rm FG}\ge B\oplus R\,Q_D$ with
 \begin{equation}
  B=A-\frac{CC^*}{t-R},\qquad A=P_DH_{\rm FG}P_D,\qquad CC^*=P_DVQ_DVP_D,
  \label{eq:az2-schur}
 \end{equation}
 so $E_0\ge\lambda_0(B)$ because $\lambda_0(B)\le\mu<R$. With
 $\beta_0=\langle\hat v,B\hat v\rangle=\mu-\rho_Q^2/(t-R)$ and the exact
 $\eta=\norm{(B-\beta_0)\hat v}$, finite Temple gives
 $\lambda_0(B)\ge\beta_0-\eta^2/(b_B-\beta_0)$ with
 $b_B=b-4\tfg^2/(t-R)\le\lambda_1(B)$. This energy bound is 14 to 24 times
 sharper than Temple's, because the omitted leakage is divided by $t-R$,
 about 42 at $D=6$ and 66 at $D=8$, instead of by $b-\mu\approx2.8$.
\item \emph{Eckart.}
 $\sin^2\theta\le(\mu-E_0)/(E_1-E_0)\le(\mu-E_0^{\rm low})/(b-\mu)=:s_B^2$.
 At every grid point $s_B<s_A$, by a factor of 3.7 to 4.9 (the review records
 3.74 to 4.85 exactly). The headline enclosure therefore uses the tail
 comparison together with the certified gap.
\item \emph{Observable.} With $\norm{W_1}\le1$, vector centering and
 $s=\min(s_A,s_B)$,
 \begin{equation}
  \bigl|\langle\hat v,W_1\hat v\rangle-\langle\psi_0,W_1\psi_0\rangle\bigr|
  \le2s\sigma+2s^2\le2s+2s^2,\qquad
  \sigma=\norm{(W_1-\langle W_1\rangle)\psi_0}\le1 .
  \label{eq:az2-observable}
 \end{equation}
\item \emph{Certified tails of the true ground state.} From
 $Q_D(H_{\rm FG}-E_0)\psi_0=0$ and $Q_DH_{\rm FG}Q_D\ge t\,Q_D$,
 $\norm{Q_D\psi_0}\le(\rho_Q+2|\tfg|s)/(t-\mu)=:T_{\rm joint}$. For a link
 class $e$ let $\Pi_e$ project onto spin above $D/2$; it commutes with $K$,
 and its range lies in $Q_D$ by the triangle inequality. With $c_e$ the number
 of faces containing the link, $t_e$ its threshold and $\Lambda_e$ its
 spin-exactly-$D/2$ layer,
 $\norm{\Pi_e\psi_0}\le c_e|\tfg|(\norm{\Lambda_e\hat v}+s)/(t_e-2|\tfg|-\mu)$.
 The smaller of the per-link and joint bounds is reported: for $v_M$ the joint
 bound, for the other six links the per-link bound, smaller by factors of
 about 50 to 140.
\end{enumerate}
At every grid point and cutoff, then, $\langle W_1\rangle$ lies in
$[w_R-(2s+2s^2),\,w_R+(2s+2s^2)]$ rounded outward, where
$w_R=\langle\hat v,W_1\hat v\rangle$; $E_0$ lies between the larger of the
Temple and tail-comparison bounds and $\mu$; and the ground state's omitted
weight is at most $T_{\rm joint}$.

\subsubsection{The derivative at zero and the own free reference}

\paragraph{The exact derivative.}

\begin{equation}
 \psi_1=K^{-1}Q_0(W_1+W_2)\Omega=\frac{W_1+W_2}3
 \label{eq:az2-psi1}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Second- and higher-order coefficients as finite-model values}
\label{sec:az2-coefficients}
\paragraph{The exact series.}
The producer computes the Rayleigh--Schr\"odinger series about the free ground
state exactly, by polynomial back-substitution, since $K$ is triangular in
degree. Every $\psi_n$ lies in $P_n$, and the full-space equation
$K\psi_n=-V\psi_{n-1}+\sum_kE^{(k)}\psi_{n-k}$ holds as a polynomial identity
for $n\le5$. The coefficients are therefore identical at $D=6$ and $D=8$, and
their truncation error is exactly zero, not merely bounded. The first two
vectors are
\begin{equation}
 \psi_1=\frac{x+y}3,\qquad
 \psi_2=\frac{x^2}{24}+\frac{4xy}{39}+\frac{y^2}{24}+\frac{4z}{351}-\frac1{48},
 \label{eq:az2-psi2}
\end{equation}
and Table~\ref{tab:az2-coefficients} lists the coefficients. In closed form,
\begin{equation}
 \langle W_1\rangle=\frac{\tfg}6-\frac{187}{33696}\tfg^3
 +\frac{767713}{2523156480}\tfg^5+\cdots,\qquad
 E_0=-\frac{\tfg^2}6+\frac{187}{67392}\tfg^4+\cdots .
 \label{eq:az2-series}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Checks on the table.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Hellmann--Feynman.} $a_n=-(n+1)e_{n+1}/2$ holds exactly for
 $n\le4$; for instance $a_1=-e_2=1/6$ and $a_3=-2e_4=-187/33696$.
\item \emph{Why the second-order coefficient of $\langle W_1\rangle$
 vanishes.} The shared-link flip $U_E$ with $E=\{v_M\}$ meets both faces once,
 sends both $W_f$ to $-W_f$ and commutes with every Casimir and with the gauge
 action. So $\langle W_1\rangle(-\tfg)=-\langle W_1\rangle(\tfg)$ and every
 even coefficient is zero. This is the finite-graph analogue of the AW1 flip
 lemma (Section~\ref{sec:aw1-flip}), recorded as a consistency observation
 only; it is a finite-graph centre-flip fact and says nothing about $K_2$ or
 the AQ remainder.
\item \emph{A second route for the second-order values.} The even parts of the
 certified $\pm1/1000$ enclosures give $a_2\in[-1.0114\times10^{-4},\,
 1.0114\times10^{-4}]$ and $e_2\in[-0.16681837,\,-0.16651496]$ at both
 cutoffs, which contain $0$ and $-1/6$.
\item \emph{Grid remainder, a labelled comparison.}
 $(\langle W_1\rangle-\tfg/6)/\tfg^3$ is about $-5.54961982868\times10^{-3}$
 at $1/1000$ and $-5.54657925470\times10^{-3}$ at $1/10$, against
 $-187/33696\approx-5.5496201\times10^{-3}$.
\end{itemize}

one enclosure is not the coefficient. These are finite-model values only. No
remainder constant of any other model is bounded or estimated from them.

\subsubsection{The dictionary, the mandatory sentence and the gate fields}
