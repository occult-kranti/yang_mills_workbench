\section{Sub-round 4: local closeness of subsequential states}
\label{sec:state}

The fourth Round31 roadmap goal, ``Identify limiting states and boundary
effects'', asks either for state identification or for a quantitative
boundary comparison between two frozen construction families, with a local
observable class and a common physical clock
(\repo{research/round31/advisor/roadmap.json}{Round31 roadmap}). AQ1 supplies
its states by diagonal extraction along centered whole-star boxes
(Section~\ref{sec:dictionary}) and does not prove uniqueness, whole-sequence
convergence or a rate in $N$. Sub-round~4 does not identify states either.
AY1 compares every subsequential limit of two named finite-volume
constructions on the cover $R$, with explicit constants. AY2 is to state, with
those constants, what the comparison does not establish. This edition records
the AY1 gate; the AY2 subsection is completed when its gate lands. The model
returns to the zero-selected patterned family of
Sections~\ref{sec:av1}--\ref{sec:aw2}. It is not the uniform model of
Section~\ref{sec:ax1}.

\subsubsection{Two named construction families}
\label{sec:ay1-families}
Both families use the centered coarse cubes $\Lambda_N=[-N,N]^3$ with
$N\ge2$:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \text{F1 (AQ1):}\quad&\widehat H^{(1)}_N=\sum_{b\in\Lambda_N}h_b
 +\sum_{b+S\subset\Lambda_N}\phi_b,\\
 \text{F2 (I1 \S6):}\quad&\widehat H^{(2)}_N=\sum_{b\in\Lambda_N}h_b
 +\sum_{b\in\Lambda_N}\phi^{(\Lambda_N)}_b,\qquad
 \phi^{(B)}_b=-\frac\tau3\sum_{f\in O_b,\ M_f\subset B}W_f .
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:ay1-families}
\end{equation}
F1 is the box operator of \eqref{eq:dict-star}, I1's source empty-boundary
rule: an anchor group is retained only when its whole star lies in the box.
F2 retains every omitted face whose actual owner set $M_f$ lies in the box,
grouped at its anchor. The anchor always lies in $M_f$, because the offset $0$
belongs to every class support, so no retained face has an anchor outside the
box. I1 \S6 pads a finite set $B$ to $B_+=\bigcup_{b\in B}(b+S)$ and places
the on-site $h_x$ alone on $B_+\setminus B$. The padded sites carry no
interaction, so the padded ground is $\psi^{(2)}_N\otimes\Omega_{\rm pad}$,
and its density on $R$ equals that of $\widehat H^{(2)}_N$. The contract
leaves the F2 box geometry open. Both producers and the skeptic use the same
centered $\Lambda_N$, and the bounds below hold for every finite coarse region
containing $R$, since face counts only decrease. Coarse translation by
$(N,N,N)$ is the fine translation $(4N,2N,N)$, which preserves residues, face
classes and owner sets. It maps F2 on $\Lambda_N$ to I1's
all-contained-support prescription on an orthant box.

The families genuinely differ at the boundary. In I1's own example the set
$B=\{0,e_z\}$ contains no whole star, so F1 retains no face there, while F2
retains the ten faces with owner set $B$. On the centered cubes F1 retains
$21(2N)^3$ faces and F2 retains $14m(m+1)^2+7m^2(m+1)$ with $m=2N$, so F2
keeps $28N(5N+1)$ boundary faces more than F1
(Table~\ref{tab:ay1-families}). F1 is strictly contained in F2, and on
$\Lambda_2$ the F2 faces are in turn contained among the F1 faces of
$\Lambda_3$. The nearest extra face has an owner set at $\ell^\infty$ distance
$N-1$ from $R$. For every $N\ge2$ both families retain the same 82 faces
meeting $R$, the same ten faces with owner set $R$ and the same seven full
groups meeting $R$, because the owner sets of the faces meeting $R$ lie in
$[-1,1]^2\times[-1,2]\subset\Lambda_2$. At $N=1$ the faces meeting $R$ number
49 in F1 and 60 in F2, which is why both families start at $N=2$.

\paragraph{Two topologies and one clock.}
The contract requires two topologies, named separately.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{States}: the trace norm on $B(\mathcal H_R)$,
 $\lVert \rho-\rho'\rVert_1=\sup\{|\tr((\rho-\rho')A)|:\ A\in B(\mathcal H_R),\
 \lVert A\rVert \le1\}$. Convergence on fixed observables is not enough. The
 densities $(1-D/2)|e_0\rangle\langle e_0|+(D/2)|e_n\rangle\langle e_n|$
 converge weak-* to a limit of mass $1-D/2$ while staying a trace distance
 $D$ apart. The reset energy supplies the tightness that excludes this, as in
 \eqref{eq:av1-passage}.
\item \emph{Dynamics}: the operator norm on each fixed bounded local
 observable, uniformly on compact time windows (Nachtergaele--Sims, as placed
 in AQ1). It is not uniform over the unit ball of a local algebra, and it is
 named only. F1 inherits the star-indexed interaction. For F2 the native
 owner-set-indexed interaction has supports of $\ell^1$-diameter at most two
 and per-site sum at most $49|\tau|/3$, so its weighted norm is at most
 $81\cdot49|\tau|/3=1323|\tau|$ (reverse). No dynamics, stationarity or GNS
 generator is constructed for F2 limits, and neither producer claims that the
 dynamics of the two families coincide.
\item \emph{Clock}: $s=\alpha t_E/\hbar$, $\theta=\alpha t/\hbar$ and
 $G=H/\alpha$, as in \eqref{eq:dict-clock}; the normalized clock $u=s/8$ and
 the exponent 24 are forbidden in packets. The comparison is static. Both
 families are evaluated at the same coupling and in the same unit convention,
 with face coefficient $-\tau/3$ in units of $\delta=\alpha/8$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Two valid padding conventions.}
The producers index the F2 groups differently. The forward producer, like
the skeptic, uses the actual supports $X_b\subset(b+S)\cap\Lambda_N$. With
that convention AM2 needs no padding, and the padded sites only fit I1's
source empty-boundary form. The reverse producer indexes each clipped group
by its declared support $X_b=b+S$ inside the AM2 volume $B_+$. It follows the
AM2 reverse report's scope statement, under which each group has ``its
declared support of size at most four''. With that convention padding is
required, and the reverse checker rejects its removal, because an indexed

collections supported in $B$, so
$\psi^{\rm pad}=\psi^{(2)}_B\otimes\Omega_{B_+\setminus B}$ in either reading.
Both conventions are valid, and the density on $R$ is the same (review
finding~N1).

\paragraph{Reset and compactness.}
Reset the finite ground density on $R$ to $P_R$ and keep its exterior
marginal, as in AQ1. The on-site terms on $R$ drop to zero, groups not
meeting $R$ keep their expectations, and unbounded exterior energies cancel
after bounded spectral truncation. Each group meeting $R$ changes by at most
twice its norm. The groups meeting $R$ are exactly the seven anchors $R-S$,
each of norm at most $7|\tau|$, and on any finite $F$ at most $4|F|$ groups
meet $F$. Hence
\begin{equation}
 \omega^{(2)}_N(h_R)\le2\cdot7\cdot7|\tau|=98|\tau|,\qquad
 \omega^{(2)}_N(h_F)\le2\cdot4|F|\cdot7|\tau|=56|\tau|\,|F|=:C_F .
 \label{eq:ay1-reset}
\end{equation}
Enumeration on $\Lambda_2$ and $\Lambda_3$ gives exactly $98|\tau|$ for both
families. Charging face by face gives the labelled refinement
$2\cdot82|\tau|/3=164|\tau|/3$, which is not used, and the two-anchor orthant
reset $28|\tau|$ is rejected. Since $h_F$ has compact resolvent,
$\tr(\rho_{N,F}(1-Q_{F,L}))\le C_F/L$ for $Q_{F,L}=1_{[0,L]}(h_F)$, and
purification with partial-trace contractivity gives
$\lVert \rho-Q\rho Q\rVert_1\le2\sqrt{C_F/L}$. The F2 densities on every finite
region are therefore trace-norm precompact. Diagonal extraction over the
nested cubes gives subsequences along which every local density of F2
converges in trace norm, and the compatible limits define locally normal
states. F2 needs this extraction of its own, because its limits do not come

\subsubsection{The closeness \texorpdfstring{$2D$}{2D}}
\label{sec:ay1-closeness}
Let $\omega$ and $\omega'$ be subsequential limits at the same $\tau$,
$\omega$ along boxes of family F$i$ and $\omega'$ along boxes of family F$j$,
where $i=j$ is allowed. Here $D$ is the AV1 gate's forward tier-(ii) bound
\eqref{eq:av1-Dii},
$D=585079838465912592144137406066050/42981220507576537932303142777593983768257$,
about $1.3612\times10^{-8}$. Every finite-volume density on $R$ lies in the
closed trace-norm ball of radius $D$ about $P_R$: by AV1 for F1, and by
Section~\ref{sec:ay1-premises} for F2. Trace-norm limits stay in closed balls
\eqref{eq:av1-passage}. Hence
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \norm{\rho_R-\rho'_R}_1&\le\norm{\rho_R-P_R}_1+\norm{P_R-\rho'_R}_1
 \le2D\le\frac1{1250000},\\
 2D&=\frac{1170159676931825184288274812132100}{42981220507576537932303142777593983768257}
 \approx2.72249057405\times10^{-8}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:ay1-2D}
\end{equation}
The target margin is about $29.38$. The target is read from the contract:
the forward checker pins it to $1/1250000$, and the reverse checker ties it to
twice the AV1 target. For effects $0\le A\le1$ the trace-zero difference gives
$|\omega(A)-\omega'(A)|\le D$. Since $D$ increases in $|\tau|$, the cap values
bound every smaller coupling at either sign, and the ratio
$D(\tau)/D(\tau/100)$ is about $100.0098$, so the bound is linear. The $-\tau$
evaluation replays the same $|\tau|$ formula.

\paragraph{Reverse: the vacuum fidelity passed to every limit.}
In every box of either family, at every cutoff $L\ge24$ and for the
untruncated ground, the AV1 split gives exactly
$\tr(\rho_{N,R}P_R)=1/(1+e_N^2)$ with $e_N\le\varepsilon$. Because
$|\tr((\rho-\sigma)P_R)|\le\lVert \rho-\sigma\rVert_1$, the overlap is trace-norm
continuous, so every subsequential limit of F1 or F2 satisfies
\begin{equation}
 F_{\rm vac}:=\tr(\rho_RP_R)\ge\frac1{1+\varepsilon^2}
 =\frac{42981220507576535941210238266176140476416}{42981220507576537932303142777593983768257}.
 \label{eq:ay1-fidelity}
\end{equation}
The mixture inequality used in \eqref{eq:av1-reverse} then gives
$\lVert \rho_R-P_R\rVert_1\le2\varepsilon/\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2}\le D$. So the
reverse inequality also certifies $D$, and the triangle inequality gives $2D$.

\paragraph{Labelled variant: the Bures angle.}
Let $A(\rho,\sigma)=\arccos\sqrt{F_{\rm U}(\rho,\sigma)}$, with $F_{\rm U}$ the
Uhlmann fidelity; it is a metric on states. For the pure $P_R$,
\eqref{eq:ay1-fidelity} gives $A(\rho_R,P_R)\le\arctan\varepsilon$, so
$A(\rho_R,\rho'_R)\le2\arctan\varepsilon\le\pi/2$. The
Fuchs--van de Graaf inequality then gives
$\tfrac12\lVert \rho_R-\rho'_R\rVert_1\le\sin(2\arctan\varepsilon)=2\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon^2)$,
that is,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \norm{\rho_R-\rho'_R}_1\le\frac{4\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon^2}
 &=\frac{1170159668967453566242603438964736}{42981220507576537932303142777593983768257}\\
 &\approx2.72249055552\times10^{-8}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:ay1-bures}
\end{equation}
This bound is genuinely pairwise and slightly below $2D$, and the skeptic
re-derived it. It shows that $2D$ is not an artefact of the triangle through
$P_R$. The contract names $2D$, so the Bures value is a labelled refinement,
not the headline.

\paragraph{Quantifiers.}
The statement covers every pair of subsequential limits of F1 or F2 at the
same $\tau$, from the same family or from different families. It holds for a
chosen subsequential limit of either family, every one separately, and it
does not identify limits with one another. It is uniform local closeness, not
uniqueness, whole-sequence convergence, translation invariance or a rate in
$N$. The proof uses only a bound that holds in every box, so no rate can come
from it. The triangle route is sharp in form. The forward checker takes pure
states $v_\pm=(\cos\phi,\pm\sin\phi)$ with rational
$\cos\phi=(1-m^2)/(1+m^2)$ and $m=1/1000$. Each lies at distance
$D_{\rm ex}=2\sin\phi$ from $P$, yet they are $2D_{\rm ex}\cos\phi$ apart. So
two states that both satisfy the one-state bound may differ by nearly $2D$.

\paragraph{The same coupling is essential.}
$P_R$ does not depend on $\tau$, so $2D$ also bounds two states at different
couplings. The limits at $+\tau$ and $-\tau$ are certifiably different on
$R$, by at least $2\sqrt{10}|\tau|/72-2K_2'\tau^2\approx8.757\times10^{-10}$
(Section~\ref{sec:ay1-k2}), yet both lie within $2D$ of each other. The bound
$2D$ alone is therefore a common enclosing ball, not a boundary comparison.
The comparison content is the matching first-order term of
Section~\ref{sec:ay1-density}, and every statement keeps ``at the same
coupling'' (review finding~N5).

\subsubsection{The common first-order density}
\label{sec:ay1-density}
\paragraph{Forward: the \texorpdfstring{$R$}{R}-marginal of the first-order vector.}
AV1's creation convention is $\psi=e^{-C}\Omega_0$ with
$\langle\Omega_0,\psi\rangle=1$ and $c^{(1)}=L_0=-(\tau/72)\sum_fW_f\Omega_0$
(\eqref{eq:av1-first}, \eqref{eq:aw1-psi1}). Because
$\langle\Omega_0,c_I\rangle=0$,
\[
 |\psi\rangle\langle\psi|=|\Omega_0\rangle\langle\Omega_0|
 -\sum_I\bigl(|\hat c^{(1)}_I\Omega_0\rangle\langle\Omega_0|+\text{h.c.}\bigr)+O(\tau^2),
 \qquad\norm\psi^2=1+O(\tau^2).
\]
Contract item~3 asks for the $R$-marginal of
$-(c^{(1)}\Omega_0^*+\text{h.c.})$, restricted to the creations meeting $R$.
With the AV1 convention that form gives $+\tau/144$ for the Wilson mean,
unlike AW1's display (Section~\ref{sec:aw1-mean}); wording finding~W7
records it as verified. The supports sort into three kinds.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Disjoint from $R$.} The marginal is
 $\langle\Omega_{\rm out},c_I\rangle|\Omega_R\rangle\langle\Omega_R|=0$.
\item \emph{Straddling.} Here
 $\tr_{\rm out}|W_f\Omega_0\rangle\langle\Omega_0|
 =|\langle\Omega_{\rm out}|W_f\Omega_0\rangle\rangle\langle\Omega_R|$. Every
 straddling face has a link owned outside $R$ that occurs once, and its Haar
 integral vanishes; the forward checker verifies this for all 72.
\item \emph{Inside $R$.} The supports are $R$, $\{0\}$ and $\{e_z\}$. No
 omitted face has a one-site owner set, so
 $c^{(1)}_{\{0\}}=c^{(1)}_{\{e_z\}}=0$. The zero-selected family has no
 single-site creations, unlike route~B (Section~\ref{sec:ax1-counts}).
\end{itemize}

\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\;\rho^{(1)}_R=-\bigl(|c^{(1)}_R\rangle\langle\Omega_R|+|\Omega_R\rangle\langle c^{(1)}_R|\bigr)
 =\frac\tau{72}\sum_{f\in F_R}\bigl(|W_f\Omega_R\rangle\langle\Omega_R|
 +|\Omega_R\rangle\langle W_f\Omega_R|\bigr).\;}
 \label{eq:ay1-rho1}
\end{equation}

and the four $yz$ classes with $q=0$ (Section~\ref{sec:av1-counts}). The
Wilson face $W$ is the $xz$ class with $r=0$, $q=0$. The coefficient

single-contact faces per site, and every one of them drops out of the

\paragraph{Reverse: the vector overlap identity.}
Decompose any density on $R$ in blocks relative to $P_R$:
$\rho=F_{\rm vac}P_R+|\rho_{\perp0}\rangle\langle\Omega_R|
+|\Omega_R\rangle\langle\rho_{\perp0}|+\rho_{\perp\perp}$, with
$\rho_{\perp0}=(1-P_R)\rho\,\Omega_R$ and
$\rho_{\perp\perp}=(1-P_R)\rho(1-P_R)\ge0$.
\begin{itemize}
\item The scalar fidelity identity $F_{\rm vac}=1/(1+e^2)$ with
 $e\le\varepsilon$ gives $0\le1-F_{\rm vac}\le\varepsilon^2$. The diagonal
 block has no first-order part, because the $\tau$-derivative of the scalar
 identity vanishes.
\item Positivity gives $\tr\rho_{\perp\perp}=1-F_{\rm vac}\le\varepsilon^2$,
 so the excited block has no first-order part either. Exactly,
 $\lVert (F_{\rm vac}-1)P_R+\rho_{\perp\perp}\rVert_1=2(1-F_{\rm vac})=2e^2/(1+e^2)$.
\end{itemize}
A first-order term is therefore off-diagonal and fixed by the single vector
$\rho_{\perp0}$. The scalar identity cannot supply it; its vector form can.
For every box of either family, every cutoff $L\ge24$ and the untruncated
ground,
\begin{equation}
 \rho_R\,\Omega_R=\frac{(1_R\otimes\langle\varphi_{\rm out}|)\psi}{\norm\psi^2},
 \qquad \varphi_{\rm out}=(\langle\Omega_R|\otimes1)\psi .
 \label{eq:ay1-overlap}
\end{equation}
Indeed
$\langle v,\tr_{\rm out}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)\Omega_R\rangle=\langle v\otimes\chi,\psi\rangle$
with $\chi=(\langle\Omega_R|\otimes1)\psi$, and the AV1 split
$(P_R\otimes1)\psi=\Omega_R\otimes\varphi_{\rm out}$ gives
$\chi=\varphi_{\rm out}$. The $\Omega_R$ component of \eqref{eq:ay1-overlap} is
the scalar fidelity identity. With $n=\lVert \varphi_{\rm out}\rVert$,
$(1_R\otimes\langle\varphi_{\rm out}|)\psi=n^2(\Omega_R+\hat\xi)$ with
$\hat\xi\perp\Omega_R$, and $\rho_{\perp0}=\hat\xi/(1+e^2)$. Sort the supports
of $\delta$ in \eqref{eq:av1-delta}. The creations inside $R$ contribute
$-c_R-c_{\{0\}}\otimes\Omega_{e_z}-\Omega_0\otimes c_{\{e_z\}}$ exactly, with
$n^2$ cancelled. Each straddling creation contributes at most
$T\lVert c_I\rVert$, by the outside-excitation lemma \eqref{eq:aw1-outside}, and
each pair at most $\lVert c_I\rVert \lVert c_J\rVert$. The first-order part of $\hat\xi$ is
therefore $-c^{(1)}_R$, and the reverse route yields the same operator
\eqref{eq:ay1-rho1}. The contract's phrase ``derivative of the
vacuum-overlap/fidelity identity'' thus holds in vector form only; the scalar
derivative vanishes, and the gate records the point as non-blocking.
Derivatives are taken in finite boxes only.

\paragraph{Properties.}
The vectors $W_f\Omega_R$, $f\in F_R$, satisfy
$\langle W_f\Omega_R,W_g\Omega_R\rangle=\tfrac14\delta_{fg}$ and
$\langle\Omega_R,W_f\Omega_R\rangle=0$, because $\mathbb E[W_f]=0$,
$\mathbb E[W_f^2]=\tfrac14$ and distinct faces share at most one link. The
skeptic checks all $82\cdot81$ ordered pairs of faces meeting $R$. With the
orthonormal vectors $e_f=2W_f\Omega_R$,
\begin{equation}
 \rho^{(1)}_R=\frac\tau{144}\sum_{f\in F_R}\bigl(|e_f\rangle\langle\Omega_R|
 +|\Omega_R\rangle\langle e_f|\bigr),
 \qquad(\rho^{(1)}_R)^3=\frac{10\tau^2}{144^2}\,\rho^{(1)}_R,\qquad
 \tr\rho^{(1)}_R=0 .
 \label{eq:ay1-rho1-basis}
\end{equation}
So $\rho^{(1)}_R$ has rank two, eigenvalues $\pm\sqrt{10}|\tau|/144$, a zero
diagonal and a zero excited block, and
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \norm{\rho^{(1)}_R}_1=\frac{\sqrt{10}\,|\tau|}{72}
 \approx4.39205230578\times10^{-10},\\
 \norm{\rho^{(1)}_R}_1^2=\frac1{5184000000000000000},\qquad
 \norm{\rho^{(1)}_R}_1\le\frac{52704627669473}{120000000000000000000000}
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:ay1-rho1-norm}
\end{equation}
at $\tau=\pm10^{-8}$; the last bound is the directed upper bracket. The
labelled triangle bound is $5|\tau|/36$. The operator is self-adjoint, traceless and gauge invariant.
The consistency checks with AW1 and AV1 are exact.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\tr(\rho^{(1)}_RW)=2\cdot\tfrac\tau{144}\langle\Omega_R,We_W\rangle
 =+\tau/144$, the AW1 coefficient \eqref{eq:aw1-mean}, that is
 $\pm1/14400000000$ at $\tau=\pm10^{-8}$. Only $f=W$ contributes.
\item $\tr(\rho^{(1)}_R(W^2-\tfrac14))=0$, by the parity rule
 \eqref{eq:aw1-parity}.
\item $\lVert \rho^{(1)}_R\rVert_1\le D$.
\item Under the AW1 flip every face term changes sign, so $\rho^{(1)}_R$ is
 odd in $\tau$.
\end{itemize}
The checkers reject the literal AM2-sign display, which gives $-\tau/144$; a
dropped adjoint; a scalar or diagonal first-order ansatz; a first-order
excited block; a zero density; and straddling faces treated as operators on
$\mathcal H_R$.

\paragraph{The same operator for every limit.}
For every $N\ge2$ both families retain exactly the same ten faces with owner
set $R$. This is checked face by face for $N=2,3$ and follows in general from
the geometry of Section~\ref{sec:ay1-families}. So $c^{(1)}_R$, and hence
$\rho^{(1)}_R$, is the same operator for every box of both families and every
cutoff $L\ge24$. With the uniform remainder of Section~\ref{sec:ay1-k2},
every subsequential limit $\omega_\tau$ of either family satisfies, for every
$|\tau|\le10^{-8}$,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\rho_R(\omega_\tau)-P_R-\rho^{(1)}_R}_1\le K_2'\tau^2 .
 \label{eq:ay1-expansion}
\end{equation}
This is a statement about a coefficient, not about states. Subsequential
limits are chosen separately at each $\tau$, so a limit has no
$\tau$-derivative. If operators $X$ and $X'$ both satisfy the expansion along
a sequence $\tau_j\to0$, then $\lVert X-X'\rVert_1\le(K+K')|\tau_j|\to0$. The
first-order coefficient therefore does not depend on the family, the
subsequence or the selection $\tau\mapsto\omega_\tau$, even one that switches
families with $\tau$. It does not identify the states: they may still differ
at order $\tau^2$ on $R$, and without bound elsewhere.

\subsubsection{The \texorpdfstring{$R$}{R}-local constant \texorpdfstring{$K_2'$}{K2'}}

\paragraph{Exact decomposition.}
Use the notation of \eqref{eq:av1-density}: $n=\lVert \varphi_{\rm out}\rVert$,
$e=\lVert \delta\rVert /n$, $\xi=(1_R\otimes\langle\varphi_{\rm out}|)\delta$ and
$\sigma=\tr_{\rm out}|\delta\rangle\langle\delta|$. Sorting $\delta$ by support
gives $\xi/n^2=-c^{(1)}_R+\eta$ with
$\eta=\eta_{\rm am2}+\eta_{\rm str}+\eta_{\rm pair}$, where
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \eta_{\rm am2}=-(c-c^{(1)})_R-c_{\{0\}}\otimes\Omega_{e_z}-\Omega_0\otimes c_{\{e_z\}},\\
 \eta_{\rm str}=-\sum_{I\ \rm straddling}

 \eta_{\rm pair}=\sum_{\rm pairs}
 \frac{(1\otimes\langle\varphi_{\rm out}|)\hat c_I\hat c_J\psi_{\rm out}}{n^2}.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:ay1-eta}
\end{equation}
Exact algebra then gives
\begin{equation}
 r_R:=\rho_R-P_R-\rho^{(1)}_R

 \label{eq:ay1-remainder}
\end{equation}
Each piece has an exact trace norm. Since $\eta\perp\Omega_R$, the first
operator has trace norm $2\lVert \eta\rVert$. Since $\sigma\ge0$, $P_R\sigma=0$ and
$\tr\sigma=d^2$, one has $\lVert \sigma/n^2-e^2P_R\rVert_1=2e^2$. The last operator
has trace norm $2\lVert c^{(1)}_R\rVert =2\sqrt{10}\,a$, which is at most $20a$.

\paragraph{Uniformity and scaling.}
The bound holds uniformly in $N$, in the cutoff $L\ge24$ and in both
families. Finite-box face counts never exceed the bulk counts 82, 72 and 33,
and $T$ and $\varrho$ are the same for both families. It passes to the
untruncated ground by AV1's cutoff removal and to every subsequential limit
because the trace-norm ball is closed; that is \eqref{eq:ay1-expansion}. Every
item is tier (ii). The only inputs that are not $R$-local are $T$ and
$\varrho$, which are anchored maxima of the AM2 fixed point and are uniform in
$N$, as in AW1. The forward checker has a second code path that recomputes
each item from the pins and requires equality, which guards against the
silent undercounts of AW1 review finding~N8. The ratio $B(\tau)/B(\tau/100)$
of the total bounds is about $10000.976$, so the bound is quadratic. The
values of $K_2'$ at $\tau$, $\tau/10$, $\tau/100$ and $\tau/1000$ are about
$13417.528$, $13416.337$, $13416.218$ and $13416.206$, so $K_2'$ is
nondecreasing in $|\tau|$ and the cap value bounds every smaller coupling.

$1.0145\times10^4$ below $2D$. It is the ``agree to first order in tau'' of
the template (closeness order 2). It is still not equality, and it says
nothing outside $R$.

\subsubsection{A recorded observation}
\label{sec:ay1-observation}
The reverse triangle inequality applied to \eqref{eq:ay1-expansion} gives, for
every subsequential limit of either family at either sign,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\sqrt{10}\,|\tau|}{72}-K_2'\tau^2\le\norm{\rho_R-P_R}_1
 \le\frac{\sqrt{10}\,|\tau|}{72}+K_2'\tau^2 .
 \label{eq:ay1-observation}
\end{equation}
At the cap the interval lies in $[4.3786\times10^{-10},\,4.4055\times10^{-10}]$.
The forward previews of the two ends are $4.37863477824\times10^{-10}$ and
$4.40546983333\times10^{-10}$. The skeptic verified each end exactly as a
directed bound,
$(\text{lower}+K_2'\tau^2)^2\le10\tau^2/5184\le(\text{upper}-K_2'\tau^2)^2$.
The review asks that the observation be recorded from these exact values,
because the forward checker does not pin its lower end
(Section~\ref{sec:ay1-evidence}). The upper
end is about 30.9 times below $D$. AV1's $\varepsilon$ charges the 72
straddling faces at first order, but they reach the off-diagonal block only
at second order. The lower end would be the first lower bound on
$\lVert \rho_R-P_R\rVert_1$ in Round32; the AV1 gate provides none
(Section~\ref{sec:av1-limits}).

The gate records \eqref{eq:ay1-observation} as a labelled observation only.
It is not a preregistered target or comparator and not an admitted tier, and
it does not replace $D$ anywhere, including in $2D$. It is not a resolved
interaction shift: \recid{resolved_interaction_shift} stays false, and the
static Wilson mean belongs to AW2 (Section~\ref{sec:aw2}). AY2 may certify it
under its own contract.

\paragraph{Quantitative boundary comparison.}
Contract item~6 requires a definition, and both producers give it in
substance. In AY1 a quantitative boundary comparison is the pair of two
statements: (i) a closeness bound, $\lVert \rho_R-\rho'_R\rVert_1\le2D$ for every
pair of subsequential limits of the two named prescriptions at the same
$\tau$; and (ii) a matching first-order term, the same $\rho^{(1)}_R$ for
every such limit, with second-order difference at most $2K_2'\tau^2$. It is
explicitly not a variational statement about which boundary condition the
infinite-volume theory selects. It compares no energies or free energies of
the prescriptions and ranks neither. It does not say that the limits of
either family are canonical, and it says nothing outside $R$ or about the
dynamics. A definition that claims a variational selection, or drops the
first-order term, is rejected by both checkers.

\paragraph{Uniform in \texorpdfstring{$N$}{N} is not uniform in the spacing.}
Every constant here is uniform in the box size $N$, the on-site cutoff, the
family and the subsequence. It holds at fixed lattice spacing, fixed $\alpha$,
$\hbar$ and $E_\star$, and fixed $\tau$. Nothing is uniform in the spacing,
and $N\to\infty$ at fixed spacing is not a continuum limit. The zero-selected
family carries no dictionary between $\tau$ and the spacing
(Section~\ref{sec:dictionary}). As an illustration from a different model,
the uniform dictionary $\tau=96/g^4$ of Section~\ref{sec:ax1-dictionary}
needs $g^4\ge9.6\times10^9$ for the cap and $g^4\ge32$ for the coefficient
box, so every path with vanishing spacing and $g\to0$ leaves both. The cover
$R$ is two coarse factors, a block of $4\times2\times2$ fine lattice spacings:
fixed in lattice units, it shrinks in physical units as the spacing
decreases. Both checkers reject a claim of uniformity in the spacing, and a
reading of uniformity in $N$ as a continuum statement
(\recid{not_uniform_in_a}).

\paragraph{Phrasing rule.}
Both checkers scan the reports and the exported sentences. They reject a
definite-article phrase for a single AQ-type state or for a single
infinite-volume limit, and any sentence that mentions uniqueness without
``not''. They also require the phrase ``a chosen subsequential''. AQ1's chosen
state is a chosen subsequential limit of F1, and every statement here holds
for each such limit separately.
