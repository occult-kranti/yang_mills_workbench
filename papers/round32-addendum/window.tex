\subsection{Spectral set-up}
\label{sec:av2-spectral}
Let $\eta$ be the spectral measure of $G_\tau$ in the vector $\chi_\tau$ of
\eqref{eq:dict-observable}. AQ1 gives $G_\tau\ge0$, so $\eta$ is a positive
measure on $[0,\infty)$ with finite mass
$\eta([0,\infty))=\norm{\chi_\tau}^2=\omega_\tau(W^2)-m_\tau^2\le1$, and
\begin{equation}
 c_\tau(\theta)=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{i\theta x}\,d\eta(x),\qquad
 C_\tau(s)=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-sx}\,d\eta(x).
 \label{eq:av2-spectral}
\end{equation}
Only this nonnegativity enters. The AQ2 gap, which would restrict
$\supp\eta$ to $\{0\}\cup[1/16,\infty)$, is not used. The free reference has
$\eta_0=\tfrac14\delta_3$ and the functions \eqref{eq:dict-reference}.

\subsection{The window and its transform}
\label{sec:av2-window}
With the frozen convention
$\hat g(\theta)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb R}g(x)e^{-i\theta x}\,dx$ and $s>0$,
the window and its transform are
\begin{equation}
 g(x)=\begin{cases}e^{-sx},&x\ge0,\\ e^{sx}\bigl(1-2sx+2s^2x^2\bigr),&x<0,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 \hat g(\theta)=\frac{4s^3}{\pi(s-i\theta)^3(s+i\theta)},
 \qquad
 |\hat g(\theta)|=\frac{4s^3}{\pi}\,(s^2+\theta^2)^{-2}.
 \label{eq:av2-window}
\end{equation}
For $y=-x>0$ the left branch is $e^{-sy}(1+2sy+2s^2y^2)>0$. So $g$ is positive
and continuous, lies in $L^1\cap C_0$ with $\norm{g}_1=8/s$, and is $C^2$ at
the origin: both sides give the value, first and second derivatives
$(1,-s,s^2)$. The third derivative jumps by $-8s^3$. That jump fixes the
$\theta^{-4}$ decay $(2\pi)^{-1}\,8s^3/\theta^4=4s^3/(\pi\theta^4)$. As a
check, $\hat g(0)=4/(\pi s)=\norm{g}_1/(2\pi)$.

\paragraph{Forward route: half-line transforms.}
Put $a=s-i\theta$ and $b=s+i\theta$, so $a+b=2s$ and $ab=s^2+\theta^2$. Laplace
transforms of the two branches give
\begin{equation}
 2\pi\hat g(\theta)=\frac1b+\frac1a+\frac{2s}{a^2}+\frac{4s^2}{a^3}
 =\frac{8s^3}{a^3b},
 \label{eq:av2-halfline}
\end{equation}
because $(2s-a)(a^2+2sa+4s^2)=8s^3-a^3$. For a general left polynomial
$1+c_1y+c_2y^2$ the same calculation gives
\begin{equation}
 2\pi\hat g(\theta)\,a^3b=(2s-c_1)\,a^2+(2sc_1-2c_2)\,a+4sc_2 .
 \label{eq:av2-family}
\end{equation}
$C^1$ matching ($c_1=2s$) removes the $\theta^{-2}$ term, and $C^2$ matching
($c_2=2s^2$) removes the $\theta^{-3}$ term. The Poisson kernel is the kinked
member $c_1=c_2=0$, with $\hat g=s/(\pi(s^2+\theta^2))$. Its $\theta^{-2}$
tail is exactly why its absolute first moment diverges. The $C^1$ window is
$c_1=2s$, $c_2=0$, with $\hat g=2s^2/(\pi a^2b)$.

\paragraph{Reverse route: residues.}
The reverse producer starts from the target. The only dynamical input is a
real-time bound linear in $|\theta|$, so $C(s)$ must be written as
$\int K(\theta)c(\theta)\,d\theta$ with $\int K(\theta)e^{i\theta x}d\theta=e^{-sx}$
on $x\ge0$. Values on $x<0$ are unconstrained. Exactness on $x\ge0$ requires
exactly one simple pole at $\theta=is$ in the upper half-plane. Placing every
other pole at $\theta=-is$ gives the family
\begin{equation}
 K_n(\theta)=\frac{(2s)^n}{2\pi\,(s+i\theta)(s-i\theta)^n},\qquad
 |K_n(\theta)|\sim|\theta|^{-(n+1)},
 \label{eq:av2-residue}
\end{equation}
whose normalization comes from the residue at $is$. Finite $M_0$, $M_1$ and
$M_2$ force $n=3$: $n=1$ is Poisson and $n=2$ the $C^1$ window. The residue at
the triple pole reproduces $e^{sx}(1-2sx+2s^2x^2)$ on $x\le0$, and only then
was the result compared with the frozen window. The skeptic added two further
routes: the annihilator identity
$(\tfrac{d}{dx}+s)(\tfrac{d}{dx}-s)^3g=-8s^3\delta_0$ with Wallis integrals,
and a Leibniz expansion of the lower residues.

\paragraph{Constants.}
All four routes give
\begin{equation}
 M_0=\norm{\hat g}_1=2,\qquad
 M_1=\int_{\mathbb R}|\theta|\,|\hat g(\theta)|\,d\theta=\frac{4s}{\pi},\qquad
 M_2=\int_{\mathbb R}\theta^2|\hat g(\theta)|\,d\theta=2s^2 .
 \label{eq:av2-constants}
\end{equation}
The forward producer reads them from exact antiderivatives, each a rational
function of $\theta$ plus $B\arctan(\theta/s)$, whose rational parts vanish at
$\pm\infty$.
The reverse producer uses the residue at $is$ for $M_0$ and $M_2$. For $M_1$
it uses a keyhole contour,
$\int_0^\infty\theta(s^2+\theta^2)^{-2}d\theta
=-\sum_{\pm is}\mathrm{Res}\bigl[z\log z/(z^2+s^2)^2\bigr]=1/(2s^2)$. Note
that $\int\hat g=g(0)=1$ while $\norm{\hat g}_1=2$. The transform is complex,
not a positive mass-one kernel, and charging the state term with mass one
would under-charge it by half; that mutation is a rejected control.

\subsection{Window lemma}
\label{sec:av2-lemma}
$L^1$ inversion gives $g(x)=\int\hat g(\theta)e^{i\theta x}\,d\theta$ for
almost every $x$. Both sides are continuous, so equality holds at every real
$x$. Since
$\int\!\!\int|\hat g(\theta)|\,d\theta\,d\eta(x)=M_0\,\eta([0,\infty))<\infty$,
Fubini gives
\begin{equation}
 C_\tau(s)=\int_{[0,\infty)}g\,d\eta=\int_{\mathbb R}\hat g(\theta)\,c_\tau(\theta)\,d\theta,
 \qquad
 C_0(s)=\int_{\mathbb R}\hat g(\theta)\,\tfrac14e^{3i\theta}\,d\theta
 =\tfrac14g(3)=\tfrac14e^{-3s}.
 \label{eq:av2-lemma}
\end{equation}
Both hypotheses matter, and each is executed as a damaging control. A spectral
atom at $x=-1$ would be read as $g(-1)=e^{-s}(1+2s+2s^2)$, which is $5/e$ at
$s=1$, instead of the heat value $e$; the window under-reads every negative
atom. The mirrored convention $\theta\to-\theta$ evaluates
$g(-3)/4=25e^{-3}/4$ on the free atom. A window matched to $e^{-sx}$ only above
an assumed gap is rejected, because only $G_\tau\ge0$ is used. Fourier
inversion and Fubini's theorem are cited, not machine-checked; the checkers
verify the transform algebra, the modulus, the antiderivatives and inversion
at $x=0$ exactly.

\subsection{Real-time comparison}
\label{sec:av2-duhamel}
The comparison restates AT4 with its domain argument. In each centered box
$\Lambda_N$, $N\ge2$, split $G_N=A_N+B_N$, where $B_N=\sum_{b\in R-S}V_b$
takes all seven incident stars whole. Then
$\norm{B_N}\le7\cdot7|\tau|/8=49|\tau|/8$, and $A_N$ contains the free
evolution on $R$ and everything disjoint from $R$. On the common domain the
relative unitary $U(\theta)=e^{i\theta G_N}e^{-i\theta A_N}$ has the bounded,
strongly continuous derivative $ie^{i\theta G_N}B_Ne^{-i\theta A_N}$.
Vectorwise integration gives $\norm{U(\theta)-I}\le|\theta|\norm{B_N}$. Only
then is the bounded $W$ inserted:
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\alpha^N_\theta(W)-\alpha^0_\theta(W)}\le2|\theta|\norm{B_N}\le k|\theta|,
 \qquad k=\frac{49|\tau|}{4}.
 \label{eq:av2-slope}
\end{equation}
The factor two is necessary. The exact fixture $U=\mathrm{diag}(u,\bar u)$
with $u=(3+4i)/5$ and $X=\sigma_x$ gives
$\norm{UXU^*-X}^2/\norm{U-I}^2=16/5$. The slope is local: an extensive star
norm would grow like $(2N)^3$ and is rejected. AQ1's local norm convergence of
the dynamics on compact intervals passes \eqref{eq:av2-slope} to the actual AQ
evolution at every real $\theta$. Adding and subtracting the free evolution in
the actual state, and then the local reference state, gives
\begin{equation}
 |c_\tau(\theta)-c_0(\theta)|
 \le k|\theta|+D+m_\tau^2\le k|\theta|+D+D^2\qquad(\theta\in\mathbb R).
 \label{eq:av2-compare}
\end{equation}
The state term uses full trace duality. The operator $W\alpha^0_\theta(W)$
has free expectation $\tfrac14e^{3i\theta}$, which is not real at
$\theta=\pi/6$. It is therefore not an effect, and the $D/2$ refinement of
AT4 is not applied; exact $2\times2$ fixtures show that the refinement fails
for such operators.

\subsection{Retained failures and the crossover}
\label{sec:av2-failures}
Table~\ref{tab:av2-failures} lists the certificates evaluated at unchanged
$\tau=10^{-8}$ and $s=1$ alongside the admitted one. The AT4 Poisson
certificate at $L=10^4$ is reproduced to $10^{-15}$ and remains insufficient.
With $D\to0$ and the tail $s/(\pi L)$, the Poisson dynamics-plus-tail cost
obeys, for every $L>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{ks}{\pi}\log\Bigl(1+\frac{L^2}{s^2}\Bigr)+\frac{s}{\pi L}
 \ge\frac s\pi\Bigl[2k\log\frac Ls+\frac1L\Bigr]
 \ge\frac{2ks}{\pi}\Bigl(1+\log\frac1{2ks}\Bigr)
 \ge1.2650882233\times10^{-6},
 \label{eq:av2-floor}
\end{equation}
with the minimum near $L=1/(2k)\approx4.08\times10^6$. The value at
$L=4081633$ is at most $1.265088224\times10^{-6}$. With the admitted $D$

No choice of $L$ meets $10^{-6}$ at $s=1$. This proves the Round31 planning
observation of Section~\ref{sec:checkpoint} and retains it as an insufficient

The reason is the kernel's first moment:
\begin{equation}
 \int_{-L}^{L}|\theta|\,\frac{s}{\pi(s^2+\theta^2)}\,d\theta
 =\frac s\pi\log\Bigl(1+\frac{L^2}{s^2}\Bigr)\xrightarrow[L\to\infty]{}\infty,

 =\frac{4s}{\pi}\,\frac{L^2}{s^2+L^2}\ \uparrow\ \frac{4s}{\pi}.
 \label{eq:av2-moments}
\end{equation}

$\log65>4$. At $L=10^{32}$ the Poisson dynamics term alone exceeds
$5.7\times10^{-6}$. The window removes both the cutoff and its tail.
