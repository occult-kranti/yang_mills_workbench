\section{Process history of the Round32 cycle}
\label{sec:history}

This section records how the cycle was run, using the repository files named
in each paragraph. It adds no mathematical claim, and none of the planning
numbers below is an admitted value. All times are UTC. The freezing tool
wrote the \sourcepath{frozen_at} stamps and the gate tool wrote the
\sourcepath{completed_at} stamps, both from the machine clock. Like commit
dates, they are self-reported and are not trusted timestamps. The roles are
those of the status section: an advisor who owns contracts, gates and
publication; a skeptic; three methodological lenses; one research assistant
per lens after each sub-round; and forward and reverse producers. All of them
are model agents with correlated ancestry, working under the project's
workflow contract \cite{agents}.

\subsection{Authorization and brief}
\label{sec:history-brief}
A user instruction of 23 September 2026 authorized Round32 as an adaptive
cycle. It named an expert panel of Newton/Tesla, Jung/Pauli and
Penrose/Feynman lenses, with occult and mystical source reading, together
with an advisor, a skeptic and subagents for coding and simulation. The
structure was three panel deliberation loops, then five sub-rounds of two
investigations each. After every sub-round each lens would receive a research
assistant, and the panel could change goals, directions, skills and roadmap.
Publication was by merge to the main branch. The panel was also allowed to
produce statements, derivations and experiment-setup images where it judged
them more useful than a numerical certificate.

The advisor's brief of the same day \cite{r32panel} restated the admitted
Round29 and Round31 results with their quantifiers
\cite{r29am,r29aq,r29al,r31at}. It listed the five ranked Round31 roadmap
goals and five technical observations for the panel to test, none of them
admitted. Before reading any panel memo, the advisor optimized the AT4
Poisson certificate over its cutoff. The result was a dynamics-plus-tail
floor of about $1.27\times10^{-6}$ at $s=1$, whatever the state term. An
Arb/mpmath cross-check tool \cite{flint,mpmath} confirmed that independent
enclosures lie inside the recorded AT5 intervals. Both were planning
observations; AV2 later proved the floor as a retained failure
(Section~\ref{sec:av2-failures}).

\subsection{Three deliberation loops}
\label{sec:history-loops}

\paragraph{Loop 1: triage, memos and plan~v1.}
The skeptic wrote first. Its prospective triage was written before any
Round32 expert memo, contract or producer work existed, and it read nothing
under \sourcepath{experts/} \cite{r32skeptic}. It inventories every admitted
result with its quantifiers, sets a complete error ledger for each roadmap
goal, assesses the brief's five observations, takes a position on direction
and fixes a minimum acceptable deliverable for each sub-round. The companion
catalogue \sourcepath{prospective-controls.json} names 60 control ids in seven
groups: universal~12, cap~15, shift~9, finite graph~5, uniform~9, state~5 and
continuum~5. Each id names a damaging mutation that every producer checker
must reject by explicit exceptions that stay active under \texttt{-O}. The
catalogue's status is
\recid{prospective_skeptic_requirements_not_executed}.

Each lens then wrote a memo, a source ledger and a structured recommendation
\cite{r32lenses}, and the Jung/Pauli lens also wrote the occult reading
ledger \cite{r32occult}. The advisor's synthesis records eight points of
agreement:
\begin{itemize}
\item ordering the creation product removes the square root;
\item the Poisson floor is an artifact of the kernel;
\item the shift of the centered correlation is second order, so the only
first-order Wilson effect is the uncentered mean $\tau/144$ and roadmap
goal~2 was re-targeted to it;
\item route~B for the uniform model;
\item finite-graph certificates are a different model;
\item the direction rules;
\item the Jung pre-registration rules;
\item no occult or historical source became a premise.
\end{itemize}
Three disagreements were left open: a second-order Duhamel comparison against
the window kernel, preview estimates of $K_2$ spanning $1.9\times10^4$ to
$1.6\times10^8$, and the rank of the finite-graph loop. Plan~v1 already had
the five sub-rounds of Table~\ref{tab:plan}. The modern memo arrived after the
synthesis draft and was merged into the same note. It added the Gauvin lead,
the $J_0$ problem for the uniform model and a concrete design on the Round11
two-plaquette graph \cite{r11}.

\paragraph{Loop 2: critique of plan~v1.}
The inputs were the skeptic's loop-2 response and the three lens responses.
Three loop-2 sessions were interrupted by an account spend limit and
relaunched on a smaller model; their written responses are complete. The
skeptic reported two facts that changed numbers in every memo, including its
own. A bulk factor lies in 49 omitted-face owner sets, not the bound 84
\cite{r21i1}. The second-order multiplier of the Wilson mean is 1, not 4. The
response also listed eight vetoes.

The advisor adopted ten corrections:
\begin{itemize}
\item the count of 49 faces per factor;
\item the multiplier of 1, with the resulting range for $K_2$;
\item the AV1 target $4/10^7$, set by AV2 feasibility;
\item reverse premise isolation, enforced by the tools;
\item separate forward and reverse routes;
\item confirmation of the window kernel;
\item the $J_0$ re-freeze for the uniform model;
\item the finite graph after sub-round~2, never as the resolution of goal~2;
\item an interim Gauvin sentence;
\item the Jung pre-registration block with the skeptic's four amendments.
\end{itemize}
Five vetoes were recorded (Section~\ref{sec:plan}). The modern lens confirmed
the window constants with sympy and Arb and withdrew its own taper. It also
proposed the link-flip set $E$, which the advisor checked in all three
orientations on a $7^3$ block, and Eckart's inequality as a candidate route
for the cutoff-vector step. Each lens accepted the errors the skeptic
itemized in its memo, in its loop-2 response or in loop~3.

\paragraph{Loop 3: objection pass and freeze.}
Each lens and the skeptic read the draft freeze note, \sourcepath{plan.json}
and the AV1 and AV2 draft contracts, and wrote a sign-off. Nobody vetoed the
plan. The skeptic signed off the plan and both contract texts, but vetoed
freezing AV1 until its selection note stopped contradicting the contract in
three places: the note gave a $10^{-6}$ target where the contract has
$4/10^7$; it asked producers to find unaided a normalization trap that the
contract spells out; and it said ``independently'' where ``separately'' is
correct. The skeptic's loop-3 note also fixed the conditions under which the
link-flip antisymmetry holds. These are the identity
$H(\tau,\kappa)\mapsto H(-\tau,-\kappa)$, antisymmetry in $\tau$ only at the
zero triple, a whole-set statement for AQ limits, and no $O(\tau^3)$ remainder
from oddness alone, and they entered AW1 as obligations. The advisor applied
every condition, recorded the change from plan~v1 to~v2, adopted the
attribution sentence fixed by the skeptic's Gauvin diff record
(Section~\ref{sec:history-sources}) and froze plan~v2.

Rules added to the method snapshots at this point \cite{r32methods}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Newton:} compute the target's first-order coefficient exactly
before charging a first-order error budget;
\item \emph{Tesla:} name source, load, clock and transfer element with their
exact constants, and use the $\pm\tau$ mirror as a control;
\item \emph{panel guide:} the Jung pre-registration rules, with two
cautionary cases, Jung's 1952 astrology statistics and Besant and
Leadbeater's occult chemistry \cite{jungsync,besant}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Sub-round updates, assistants and lens reviews}
\label{sec:history-updates}
After each sub-round, each lens reviewed the gates and wrote an update, one
assistant per lens ran the tests that lens had requested, and the advisor
wrote a panel update \cite{r32panel,r32lenses}. The assistant packages count
zero research loops, and nothing in them decides an admission. Each update
kept the goals of the remaining sub-rounds.

\paragraph{Panel update~1 (23 September, after AV1 and AV2).}
The Newton/Tesla assistant reproduced the exact Haar moments by two routes,
rebuilt the 49/15/82 face enumeration from the I1 table without the
producers' code, reproduced the forward AV1 constant bit for bit, and
reproduced the window budget of about $1.832\times10^{-7}$. The Jung/Pauli
assistant ran the $\tau=0$ null replay and the $\tau$-scaling exponents,
$1.0000$ for the linear tiers against exactly $0.5$ for the square-root
bound. Its audit of the AV2 pre-registration found that the frozen contract
lists 24 controls while its mirror lists 23. Both producers had implemented
the missing id, so the gate records a non-blocking clerical defect, and the
freezing tool now rejects such a mismatch. The modern assistant built a
python-flint harness and checked the flip parity exhaustively on $6^3$ and
$9^3$ blocks. The AW1 contract gained the free-link cross-check, a full flip
enumeration, pinned $K_2$ terms and a positive demonstration at nonzero
$\kappa$. The AZ2 cutoff was reread as a polynomial degree.

\paragraph{Panel update~2 (24 September, after AW1 and AW2).}
Roadmap goal~2 was recorded as met in its re-targeted, static form. The AX1
and AX2 contracts gained three requirements: a proof that route~B ties every
face coefficient to $\tau$; an itemized incidence table with a
no-double-count control; and binding of the admitted AX1 bound, never the
target. The update also records that the gate-bound $K_2^+$ is the skeptic's
conservative itemization. The scratch-folder rule of
Section~\ref{sec:history-rules} was adopted here.

\paragraph{Panel update~3 (24 September, after AX1 and AX2).}
The historical lens preferred a single producer plus skeptic for AY1, and its
dissent was recorded; AY1 stayed paired because its padding-family
verification, first-order density and R-local $K_2'$ are new derivations. The
update also settled the content of AZ1 and the recipe for AZ2. AX1's frozen
contract carried a mis-rounded prose threshold. Both producers and the
skeptic caught it, and the gate binds exact rationals only.

\paragraph{Panel update~4 (24 September, after AY1 and AY2).}
The Jung assistant's audit recorded nine defects in the sub-round-4 record,
none of which decides admission. They led to the second vocabulary extension and to a
new final-integration audit item on shared field names. The modern lens
withdrew its sub-round-3 expectation that $K_2'$ would be smaller than
$K_2^+$: it is about four times larger, and the panel update records the
expectation as a category error. The historical lens withdrew its AY1 dissent.
The AZ1 and AZ2 contracts gained the requirements that the panel update
lists.

The historical and modern lens updates record dated web searches after every

\subsection{Process rules added during the cycle}
\label{sec:history-rules}
Four rules were added as repairs by record. None of them edits a frozen file.

\paragraph{Scratch disclosure.}
The skeptic's AW1 review, finding N1, found that both AW1 producers had used
the shared scratchpad root between 23:14Z and 23:20Z without disclosing any
reads. The forward producer's \sourcepath{k2.py} had overwritten the skeptic's
own pre-comparison scratch file at the same path. Whether any agent read
another's file cannot be determined from the files. Isolation was therefore
verified for repository inputs only, and scratch-channel independence is
unrefuted but unaudited. From sub-round~3 on, producers keep private scratch
folders and disclose any scratchpad read in their reports. The skeptic keeps
its own work in private folders as well, and checks the disclosed folders by
name and time.

\paragraph{Control mirror.}
After the AV2 clerical defect, the freezing tool refuses any contract whose
pre-registration control list differs from its control list.

\paragraph{Prose never admits.}
A threshold written in prose never enters the admission path. Checkers read
exact targets from the hash-checked contract snapshot.

\paragraph{Vocabulary.}
The vocabulary extensions are recorded once, in the plan, and later contracts
write the extended vocabulary directly.

Two earlier rules held throughout. Same-author passes are never called
independent review, and agreement between producers is described as
``separate'', not ``independent'', when the producers share a premise.

\subsection{Tools}
\label{sec:history-tools}
The shared tools compute no scientific result \cite{r32contracts}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \sourcepath{freeze.py} snapshots the declared premises into a producer's
inputs. It replays the checker under normal and \texttt{-O} Python into fresh
external directories, requires byte-identical outputs, writes and verifies
\sourcepath{freeze.json}, and rejects a reverse inventory that differs from
\texttt{AGENTS.md}, the contract and its shared premises.
\item \sourcepath{freeze_contract.py} validates and stamps a contract.
\item \sourcepath{record_gate.py} writes a gate from the skeptic's reviewed
statement and limitations, with complete evidence bindings, and can never
widen the reviewed scope.
\item \sourcepath{arb_crosscheck.py} compares exported rational enclosures
with python-flint Arb balls and mpmath intervals \cite{flint,mpmath}. It is
never the admission arithmetic.
\end{itemize}
Every producer checker writes a non-empty \sourcepath{checks} list and
Boolean claim flags. All admission arithmetic uses exact
\sourcepath{fractions.Fraction}.

\subsection{Sources, attribution and disclosure}
\label{sec:history-sources}

\paragraph{Gauvin.}
The modern memo identified Gauvin's arXiv:2503.15539 \cite{gauvin} as the
closest external analogue of AM2 and AQ, and asked for a version diff before
any attribution. The Round29 source ledger already recorded the v3 supplement
as the AM template, so AM2 is not claimed to be independent in any case. The
skeptic downloaded v1, v2, v3 and both supplements, recorded their hashes and
diffed them \cite{r32gauvin}. Every item of the fixed-cutoff theorem, its
lemmas and supplement A.6--A.11 first appears in v2, submitted 11~September
2026; none appears in v1. The v3 files are byte-identical to those bound in
Round29. Gauvin credits the creation-operator method to Bravyi, DiVincenzo and
Loss \cite{bdl}. The record proposes this sentence, quoted with its section
signs and dashes typeset:
\begin{quote}\small
``The AM2 creation-expansion gap and the AQ construction follow strategies
that first appear publicly in Gauvin arXiv:2503.15539v2 (2026-09-11;
\S II.C--D and supplement A.6--A.11). The project read them in v3
(2026-09-14) and records them as its template. Gauvin credits the
creation-operator method to Bravyi--DiVincenzo--Loss (CMP 284, 2008,
\S\S2.1--2.4). v1 (2025-03-06) contains none of these items. The SU(2)
selected-reference application and its explicit constants are this project's
record; scientific priority is not claimed.''
\end{quote}
The loop-3 freeze adopted the shorter form printed in the status section,
which also says that no independence is claimed. Under both wordings
\recid{priority_statement_allowed} stays false. No inference about the
direction of influence is drawn.
