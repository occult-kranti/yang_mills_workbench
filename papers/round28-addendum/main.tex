\section{Inherited obligations and adaptive research design}
Round27 established an actual one-step correction for the selected cubic-source sector $G+A_3$, with a narrower-coupling contraction of its selected mixing family and explicit support-weight loss. Its extra original remainder $E$ remained conditional. The first Round28 question is therefore whether that omitted interaction can be completely reconstructed and controlled. A positive bound for an extracted reference operator must remain separate from a gap of the full Hamiltonian with its remaining mixing.

The authorized continuation has five goal pairs with two reviewed investigations per pair. The first three inherited branches concern homogeneous remainder control, cancellation-aware canonical readouts and larger physical graphs. Their six reviews led to the homogeneous representation and Wilson-nonvacuity pair; that review then led to the quantitative observable goal. Every second contract is selected after its first review, and only gated results enter this manuscript. Repairs, literature searches, replays and refinements within a frozen contract are not additional research loops.

The advisor freezes each model, observable, domain, parameter interval and desired conclusion. Separate forward and reverse researchers work before exchanging current results; a separately authored skeptic challenges the proof and exact checks. Shared inherited premises mean these are independent derivations and implementations, not independent physical observations or external peer review. Accepted gates control which chapters enter the compiled manuscript.

Physical units remain explicit. Fixed positive $\alpha$, $\hbar$ and $E_*$ cannot be recalibrated to hide a failed matching estimate. In the homogeneous normalized Hamiltonians, physical energy is obtained using $\delta=\alpha/8$, with original spacing fixed. Support weights and filter durations are proof parameters. Parameters in the canonical summable family are not automatically a regulator trajectory or a homogeneous-model identification.
