\section{Process history of the Round33 cycle}
\label{sec:history}

This section records how the cycle was run, using the repository files named
in each paragraph. It adds no mathematical claim, and none of the planning
numbers below is an admitted value. All times are UTC. The contract freezer
wrote the \sourcepath{frozen_at} stamps and the gate recorder the
\sourcepath{completed_at} stamps, both from the machine clock; like commit
dates, they are self-reported and are not trusted timestamps. The roles are
those of the status section: an advisor who owns contracts, gates and
publication; a skeptic; three methodological lenses; one research assistant
per lens after each sub-round; and forward and reverse producers. All of them
are model agents with correlated ancestry, working under the project's
workflow contract \cite{agents}.

\subsection{Authorization and brief}
\label{sec:history-brief}
After the merged Round32 cycle \cite{r32add}, the user asked on 24 September
2026 to ``continue with the next 3 rounds - and then apply all these
solutions and new equations to solve their related problems - if any''. As in
Round32, a round was read as a sub-round of two investigations with the
advisor, the skeptic and the expert panel, one research assistant per lens
after each sub-round, and goal changes allowed after each. The advisor's
brief \cite{r33panel} therefore fixed a panel deliberation of two loops, three
research sub-rounds (BA, BB, BC), an applications stage (BD) that applies the
admitted equations where they apply and records an explicit obstruction where
they do not, and then integration and release. The brief restated the
admitted Round32 equations and constants, the ranked Round32 roadmap and the
ten closing-panel rules of Round32. The contract freezer, committed with the
Round33 skeleton at 20:54 on 24 September, enforces rules R1--R5 and R8; the
gate recorder enforces R6 and R7. The brief also listed the advisor's
candidate directions, boundary-influence decay, a Lieb--Robinson comparison
of the dynamics, restated certificates and a list of applications, as
triage input, not decisions.

\subsection{Two deliberation loops and plan~v2}
\label{sec:history-loops}

\paragraph{Loop 1: memos, triage and plan~v1.} Each lens wrote a memo, a
source ledger and a structured recommendation \cite{r33lenses}, the Jung lens
also kept the occult and government-record reading ledger \cite{r33occult},
and the skeptic wrote a prospective triage with a control catalogue
\cite{r33skeptic}. The advisor's synthesis records six points of
convergence. The first corrected the brief: the smallness $JG(R)<1/64$ is
absolute, and the per-step rate is the ball ratio $64JG(R)=37888|\tau|$,
about $3.79\times10^{-4}$ at the cap, while the global Lipschitz constant is
never a decay factor. The others were that a weighted norm and complex-coupling
analyticity give the same coefficient decay; that coefficient decay is not
decay of the reduced density, so marginal locality needs a new lemma and is
the round's main risk; that the dynamics comparison is mechanical once the
extra faces and their distances are counted; that consequences restate and
never strengthen; and a first list of applications, with the SU(3)
obstruction and the first-order coefficients per group recorded as previews
for the producers to derive. Four dissents were recorded. The Jung lens
asked for a separate Dobrushin/HTW loop, which the advisor did not schedule.
The historical lens asked to split the dynamics into a derivation and a
certification, adopted in substance. The modern lens reserved the word
uniqueness for its strong form and set the Cauchy rate at $N^{-1}$, both
adopted. The skeptic asked for margins of at least 2, frozen rate--constant
pairs and per-constant scaling brackets, adopted as contract rules. Plan~v1
fixed the four sub-rounds of Table~\ref{tab:plan}.

\paragraph{Loop 2: sign-offs and the vetoed freeze.} All three lenses signed
off plan~v1. The skeptic signed off the architecture with thirteen required
changes, P1--P13, and vetoed the BA1 and BA2 freezes until its blocking edits
were applied; its loop-2 review lists 16 blocking and 9 non-blocking entries
for BA1 and 14 and 6 for BA2 \cite{r33skeptic}. Among the edits: the BA1
floor target moved from $1/16$, whose margin was 1.99924, to $1/12$; the BA2
reverse route was replaced, since a triangle inequality through the two
limits misses the comparison target at every $N$; the within-family Cauchy
targets were quantified over all $M>N$; and the Nachtergaele--Sims theorem was
committed as a source excerpt \cite{ns}. The freezer's placeholder detector
had misread inequality notation as a placeholder span and was repaired
before the freeze (rule R1). The Jung lens kept a narrowed dissent, answered
in the BB selection note. The loop-1 panel previews that justify the frozen
margins are recorded only in the loop-2 synthesis, which is not a producer
premise, so the producers derive their constants blind.

\paragraph{Plan~v2.} The changes P1--P13 were recorded in
\sourcepath{plan.json} and plan~v2 was frozen before BA1 and BA2
(Section~\ref{sec:plan}). They fixed the forbidden phrasings of the round (P1),
one tier and one route per constant (P2), a closed gate-field vocabulary (P3),
the bracket, margin, excerpt and Cauchy rules (P4), the plan hash recorded at
each freeze (P5), the directions of BB2, BC1, BC2, BD1 and BD2 (P6 and
P8--P11), the isolation of the BB1 reverse from the lens memos and the
triage (P7), and the recording of the changes (P12, P13). The plan history
also carries a later correction entry for the BB1 secondary pair, recorded
beside the earlier entry without editing it.

\subsection{Sub-round updates, assistants and panel updates}
\label{sec:history-updates}
After each sub-round each lens reviewed the gates and wrote an update, one
assistant per lens ran the tests that lens had asked for, and the advisor
wrote a panel update \cite{r33panel,r33lenses}. The assistant packages count
zero research loops, and nothing in them decides an admission. Every panel
update kept the goals of the remaining sub-rounds and recorded that no
occult, mystical or historical source changed any premise.

\paragraph{Panel update~1 (24 September, after BA1 and BA2).} The historical
assistant confirmed every count in both gates by an independent enumeration,
among them the $28N(5N+1)$ extra faces, the padding sites and the nested
source counts. The Jung assistant found the phrase scan of the BA gate,
review and report text clean, and listed four bare ``rate'' phrasings in the
frozen BA contracts, which stay unamended. The modern assistant reproduced
every admitted BA1 and BA2 constant inside Arb and mpmath enclosures and
reconciled the $0.68\%$ gap between the two loop-1 dynamics previews as two
valid directed remainder bounds. The update adopted the modern lens's BB
targets, froze BB2 with BB1 on BB1's targets, named the fixed-$N$ change of
boundary prescription as its own BB1 item, and aligned the scanner with the
plan vocabulary.

\paragraph{Panel update~2 (25 September, after BB1 and BB2).} The historical
assistant enumerated the every-site common-core lemma for all five BB1
comparisons at $N=2,3,4$, found exactly 8 coarse translations among 64 fine
ones, and found the first vacuous point of the item-5 bracket at $N=14419$
independently of the skeptic. The Jung assistant found the four BB packets
clean under the scanner and one closed-vocabulary defect, which the BB2 gate
records. The modern assistant reproduced every BB1 constant of both routes
and found the Kotecký--Preiss side conditions satisfied. The update carried
three lessons into the next contracts: every rate claim carries its certified
range of $N$ in the same clause; an external theorem that a route will use is
a declared premise at freeze with its committed excerpt; and direct
comparisons carry the targets, with union-volume and telescoped variants
labelled only. It let BC2 run and adopted the modern lens's BC2 targets.

\paragraph{Panel update~3 (25 September, after BC1 and BC2).} The historical
assistant enumerated the route-B partition and incidence independently,
recomputed $N_{\rm sign}=4$ and found the 21 reviewed obligation rows well
formed. The Jung assistant found BC1 and BC2 clean under the scanner and ran a
vocabulary-gap audit. It found no affirmative unscoped uniqueness phrasing,
but identified a mechanism by which a heading followed by a list severs an
exclusion cue from the listed items, and it recommended scanner additions.
These were recorded for the next round and not applied mid-round, since a
scanner change during the round would change the admission test of text
already recorded. The modern assistant reproduced every BC2 constant exactly
and inside Arb enclosures, and recomputed the AX2 radius exactly. The update
kept the applications stage unchanged.

\paragraph{Closing lens updates and the roadmap.} After the BD gates each
lens wrote a closing update with a ranked proposal of at most five goals
\cite{r33lenses}, and the advisor recorded the findings, the ranked roadmap
and the handoff \cite{r33panel}. None of the three closing updates read a new
source. The applications-stage assistant packages, one per lens, are
recorded under \sourcepath{experts/<lens>/assistant-4/}; like the earlier
ones, they count zero investigations and decide no admission.

\subsection{Process rules and tools}
\label{sec:history-rules}
The shared tools compute no scientific result \cite{r33contracts}. The
contract freezer enforces rules R1--R5 and R8 at freeze time and requires
the pre-registration control list to equal the contract's. The freezing tool
snapshots the declared premises into each producer's inputs, replays the
checker under normal and \texttt{-O} Python into fresh external directories,
requires byte-identical outputs and writes the freeze record; it also
machine-checks the isolation of a reverse producer's inventory. The
negation-aware phrase scanner, added at the start of the round, checks the
round's forbidden phrasings and each contract's own list, with the mandatory
sentence template removed as one literal; the gate recorder applies it and the
template-span check at gate time. All admission arithmetic uses exact
\sourcepath{fractions.Fraction}.

Four lessons of the round are recorded as rules for the next contracts, in
the panel updates and the handoff: every rate claim carries its range of $N$;
external theorems are committed excerpts declared at freeze; an obligation
row that an admitted result settles only in part is split and frozen with its
reviewed name, as BC1's O1a and O1b were; and a single-direction loop names an
adversarial substitution of another model's constants as a required control,
as BC2's 22 route-A substitutions did. The scanner's vocabulary gap found in
BC1 is a methods decision of the roadmap (Section~\ref{sec:limits-roadmap}),
not a mid-round change.

\subsection{Sources, attribution and disclosure}
\label{sec:history-sources}
Two external sources were committed as excerpts with their PDF hashes: the
Nachtergaele--Sims paper, before the BA freeze, for the Lieb--Robinson
inequality and the limit dynamics used by BA2 and BB2 \cite{ns}, and
Ueltschi's paper, after the BB1 freezes, as the repair source above
\cite{ueltschi}. The AQ1 construction and the AM2 creation expansion are
inherited with the Round32 attribution sentence \cite{r32add}, which credits
their strategy to Gauvin \cite{gauvin}. The lens ledgers record the sources
the lenses read in Round33, at depths from search summaries to selected
sections: four records in the historical ledger, two in the Jung ledger and
27 in the modern ledger, with the occult and government-record reading ledger
kept by the Jung lens \cite{r33lenses,r33occult}. They motivated
methodological rules only. \emph{No occult, mystical or historical source
changed any premise}, and no historical figure participated in or endorsed
the work.
