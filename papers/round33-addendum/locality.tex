\section{BB1: locality of the reduced densities}
\label{sec:bb1}

\textbf{Contribution \projecttag{HNM-C-BB1}.} BA1 bounds how far a change of
box moves the AM2 creation coefficients on supports meeting the cover; it says
nothing about the reduced density, which depends on every coefficient through
the normalization and the straddling supports (Section~\ref{sec:ba1-notdensity}).
The BB1 contract, recorded under the title ``Hruday marginal locality: from
coefficient decay to the reduced densities of the named constructions'', was
frozen after the BA1 gate and before production, on 24 September 2026, as
investigation~3 of~8 and the first of sub-round~2
(\repo{research/round33/advisor/selection-bb1.md}{selection note}). It asks
for the step from coefficients to reduced densities: a bound on
$\norm{\rho^{\rm box\,1}_Y-\rho^{\rm box\,2}_Y}_1$ on the cover and on finite
regions $Y$, for every comparison of the two named families, in each on-site
cutoff space and, at fixed $N$, for the untruncated ground vectors. Both
directions are paired. The forward producer writes the reduced density as a
hard-core polymer gas and verifies a Kotecký--Preiss condition (route
\recid{polymer_kp}). The reverse producer, under premise isolation, applies
the AV1 product-ordering split recursively with per-site charging (route
\recid{iterated_split}). Both received \texttt{AGENTS.md}, the contract and 33
shared premises, among them the BA1 and BA2 gates and reviews.

The contract froze the rate $q=1/64$ and two targets in the
\recid{exact_first_order} tier: $C\le1/250000$ in the $R$ form and
$c_{\rm site}\le1/500000$ in the region form
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\rho^{\rm box\,1}_R-\rho^{\rm box\,2}_R}_1\le C\,q^{N-1},\qquad
 \norm{\rho^{\rm box\,1}_Y-\rho^{\rm box\,2}_Y}_1\le c_{\rm site}\,|Y|\,e^{|Y|/10^8}\,q^{d_Y},\quad
 d_Y=N-\max_{y\in Y}\norm{y}_\infty ,

\end{equation}
for every finite complete-factor region $Y\subset\Lambda_N$, where $N$ is the
smaller box size and $d_R=N-1$. A labelled secondary pair at
$q_2=151552|\tau|$ has targets $1/20000$ and $1/40000$; the crude tier is
reported, never targeted; and the per-step ball ratio of BA1 is not a target

\subsection{The decomposition and the every-site input}
\label{sec:bb1-decomposition}
\paragraph{The AV1-type decomposition.} Fix a comparison and a cutoff
$Q_L$, and let $\Lambda$ be the union of the two volumes, on which both
finite-box ground vectors live with the smaller box's collection zero-extended
(the AM2 fixed point of a volume stays supported in it). For a region $Y$ let
$A$ be the supports meeting $Y$, $T_c=\prod_{I\in A}(1-\hat c_I)=1+D_c$, and
$\varphi_{\rm out}=\prod_{I\notin A}(1-\hat c_I)\Omega$. Every creation not in
$A$ acts as the identity on $\mathcal H_Y$, so
$\psi(c)=T_c(\Omega_Y\otimes\varphi_{\rm out})$. Let
$O=\bigcup_{I\in A}(I\setminus Y)$ be the straddle region and $\omega_O$ the
normalized marginal of $\varphi_{\rm out}$ on $O$. Then, exactly,
\begin{equation}
 N_c(X)=\tr_O\bigl[T_c(P_Y\otimes X)T_c^*\bigr],\qquad
 \rho_Y(c)=\frac{N_c(\omega_O)}{\tr N_c(\omega_O)},\qquad
 \tr N_c(\omega)=1+\tr\bigl[D_c(P_Y\otimes\omega)D_c^*\bigr]\ge1 ,
 \label{eq:bb1-decomposition}
\end{equation}
for every state $\omega$ on $\mathcal H_O$, pure or mixed. The last identity is
AV1's orthogonality: every term of $D_c$ contains a creation excited at a site
of $Y$, so $(P_Y\otimes1)D_c=0$ and the cross terms vanish. Two Lipschitz
bounds follow, and both producers name their constants. In the outside state,
$\norm{\rho_c(\omega)-\rho_c(\omega')}_1\le2(2\varepsilon_Y+\varepsilon_Y^2)
\norm{\omega-\omega'}_1$ with
$\varepsilon_Y\le\prod_{y\in Y}(1+\sum_{I\ni y}\norm{c_I})-1$; for $Y=R$ the
constant is about $2.72\times10^{-8}$. It does not depend on $N$ and is never
used as a decay factor. In the coefficients, the forward proves
$\norm{\rho_Y(c)-\rho_Y(c')}_1\le2(1+t)^{|Y|-1}\sum_{I\cap Y\ne\varnothing}
\norm{c_I-c'_I}$ from a derivative along the segment, with
$t=49/14398580736$ the AV1 self-consistent anchored bound. The reverse proves,
for a change at a single support $J$ and \emph{every} region $Y$,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\rho_Y(c)-\rho_Y(c')}_1\le2\norm{c_J-c'_J}\qquad(\eta=0),
 \label{eq:bb1-single}
\end{equation}
by splitting at $J$ itself: $\psi(c)-\psi(c')=-\hat\delta_J\,\psi_{\neg J}$
with $\psi_{\neg J}=(P_J\otimes1)\psi(c)$, and the trace distance of two
normalized rank-one projections is at most twice the distance of the vectors
over the larger norm. Summed over far supports, \eqref{eq:bb1-single} would
grow with the shell, so it is used only for supports meeting $Y$.

Neither Lipschitz bound can simply be iterated. Iterating the outside-state
bound on the straddle region, then on its straddle region, and so on,
multiplies factors of order $|O_k|t_0$ with regions of growing size; with
$|O_k|=(2k+1)^3$ that factor exceeds one at depth 264. Comparing the two
global ground vectors directly fails too: in the global-fidelity fixture the
one-site marginals stay a fixed distance apart while the global overlap
collapses with the volume. Both are rejected controls. Two further fixtures
fix what must be charged. Coefficient decay is not marginal decay (the
fixture \eqref{eq:ba1-fixture}); and on the chain $r$--$o_1$--$o_2$ with
supports $\{r,o_1\}$, $\{o_1,o_2\}$ and $\{o_2\}$ of amplitudes $a$, $b$ and
$c$, the off-diagonal element of the unnormalized $R$-marginal is exactly
$-abc$: a change two supports away moves the marginal at the order $ab$ of
the two straddling amplitudes, with no first-order term.

\paragraph{The every-site input, form (b).} The BA1 gate admits the
coefficient bound for $u\in R$ only. Both routes need it at every site of the
union volume, and both prove it in full as a BB1 lemma (forward Theorem~8.1,
reverse Theorem~B6); the contract forbids citing it as an admitted BA1
statement. For every site $u$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{I\ni u}\norm{c^A_I(\tau)-c^B_I(\tau)}\le K\,q^{(N-\norm{u}_\infty)_+},\qquad
 K=2T(\rho),\quad T(\rho)=\frac{49\rho/144}{1-28\rho\,G'(R_a)},\quad q=\frac{|\tau|}{\rho}.
 \label{eq:bb1-everysite}
\end{equation}
The proof is BA1's analytic-disc argument (Section~\ref{sec:ba1-disc}) with
$R$ replaced by $\{u\}$. The circle bound $2T(\rho)$ is uniform in $u$,
because the anchored norm is a maximum over sites. The vanishing order at $u$
comes from the order-versus-distance lemma and from the \emph{common-core
lemma at $u$}: every omitted face with a site within distance
$N-\norm{u}_\infty-1$ of $u$ lies in a star $b+S\subset\Lambda_N$, and so is
retained by both prescriptions on every volume containing $\Lambda_N$. Such a
face has a site $q'$ with $\norm{q'}_\infty\le N-1$, and its anchor
$b=q'-s$, $s\in S$, has $b_i\in[-N,N-1]$. Every source term of every
comparison therefore lies at distance at least $N-\norm{u}_\infty$ from $u$.
Sites of the union volume outside $\Lambda_N$ take the exponent $0$, from the
per-box circle bound at the real point. The instances are the headline radius
$\rho=64|\tau|$, which gives exactly the BA1 gate's bound value
$K=49/111790368$ at $q=1/64$; the secondary radius $\rho=1/151552=\tau_*/4$,
which gives $K_2=49/10202112$ at $q_2=151552|\tau|$ and is re-instantiated
and labelled, since the BA1 gate binds only $64|\tau|$ and $\tau_*$; and the
crude input $296/390625$. Both producers enumerate the lemma at every site of
$\Lambda_N$, the reverse for $N=2,3,4$ (125, 343 and 729 sites) and the
forward for $N=2,3$, for the named comparisons and for general volumes, with
the distance $N-\norm{u}_\infty$ attained. The historical assistant's
enumeration covers all five comparisons at $N=2,3,4$.

\subsection{Forward route: hard-core polymers and the Kotecký--Preiss condition}
\label{sec:bb1-forward}
\paragraph{Polymers, Y-clusters and vacuum probabilities.} The product
expands as $\psi(c)=\sum_{\mathcal F}(-1)^{|\mathcal F|}c_{\mathcal F}$ over
families $\mathcal F$ of pairwise disjoint supports, with
$\norm{c_{\mathcal F}}=\prod_{I\in\mathcal F}\norm{c_I}$. Because each $c_I$
is excited at every site of $I$, two families pair to zero against
$A\otimes1$, $A\in B(\mathcal H_Y)$, unless their excitation sets agree
outside $Y$. A \emph{polymer} $\gamma=(\mathcal F,\mathcal F')$ is a pair of
nonempty families with the same excitation set whose bipartite overlap graph
is connected; its activity is
$w(\gamma)=(-1)^{|\mathcal F|+|\mathcal F'|}\langle c_{\mathcal F},c_{\mathcal F'}\rangle$,
and polymers are compatible when their supports are disjoint (hard core). A
\emph{Y-cluster} $\eta$ is a $Y$-matched pair all of whose members are
connected to $Y$, and $\rho_\eta\in B(\mathcal H_Y)$ is its signed partial
trace. Every $Y$-matched pair splits uniquely into one $Y$-cluster and
compatible polymers away from it, so, with
$Z(\Lambda')=\sum_\Gamma\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}w(\gamma)$,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \langle\psi,(A\otimes1)\psi\rangle=\sum_\eta\tr(\rho_\eta A)\,
 Z(\Lambda\setminus\supp\eta),\qquad
 \rho_Y=\sum_\eta\rho_\eta\,\pi(\supp\eta),\\
 \pi(S)=\frac{Z(\Lambda\setminus S)}{Z(\Lambda)}
 =\frac{\langle\psi,P_S\psi\rangle}{\langle\psi,\psi\rangle}\in(0,1].
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:bb1-polymers}
\end{equation}
The outside state enters only through the vacuum probabilities of the
$Y$-cluster supports, each at most 1. This replaces the factor $e^{a|\gamma|}$
of a rooted cluster sum and keeps every later bound free of an exponential in
the size of the differentiated support. The activities obey
$|w(\gamma)|\le\prod_{K\in\gamma}\norm{c_K}$ and
$\norm{\rho_\eta}_1\le\prod_{K\in\eta}\norm{c_K}$. An exploration tree that
adds, at each step, the member of the other side through the least unbalanced
site bounds the combinatorics: with per-site sum $\sigma\le1$, the polymers
through a site cost at most $\sigma^2$, and the $Y$-clusters cost at most
$N_Y=(1+t_a)^{2|Y|}$, with a factor $w^{-d}$ for a member at distance $d$ from
$Y$. These counts are brute-forced on a five-site chain (233 polymers, and 275
and 856 $Y$-clusters), and the polymer identity is checked exactly on four
qutrit sites with all 15 supports.

\paragraph{The mixed-weight contraction lemma.} The Kotecký--Preiss
hypothesis needs a cardinality factor, which BA1's diameter weight does not
carry. The forward therefore proves a new lemma in full. With
$W(I)=w^{\operatorname{diam}I}e^{b|I|}$ and
$\norm c_{w,b}=\max_u\sum_{I\ni u}W(I)\norm{c_I}$,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{L_k(c_1,\ldots,c_k)}_{w,b}\le w\,e^{4b}\,J\,L_k^{\rm num}\prod_j\norm{c_j}_{w,b}.
 \label{eq:bb1-mixed}
\end{equation}
The proof is BA1's weighted estimate with one addition. Besides
$\operatorname{diam}M\le\operatorname{diam}X+\sum_j\operatorname{diam}I_j$, the
output also satisfies $|M|\le|X|+\sum_j|I_j|$ with $|X|\le4$, so
$W(M)\le w\,e^{4b}\prod_jW(I_j)$: the loss is charged once per interaction
term. It is attained at order zero, where a four-site piece gives $M=X$. The
checker verifies the weight inequality over 19432 configurations at three
weight pairs and rejects a loss $w\,e^{3b}$, a loss per creation, the
maximum-instead-of-sum shortcut, $e^b=2$ and $w=42/q$. With $\hat w=w\,e^{4b}$
and $\Gamma=J\hat wG'(R_a)$, the self-map and contraction hold when
$\hat w\le w_{\max}=1/(37888|\tau|)$, and AM2's uniqueness identifies the
fixed point. The two tiers are
$\bar\tau=w\,e^{3b}t_1/(1-\Gamma)$ (exact first order) and
$\bar\tau=J\hat wG(R_a)$ (crude).

The proof weights were declared before any constant, by one rule for both
pairs: the creation weight $w=3/q$, the cluster distance weight $v=e^\mu=2/q$,

needs $v/w<1$ ($v/w=2/3$), admissibility needs $\hat w\le w_{\max}$, and the
criterion needs $a\le2b$. At the headline, $w=192$, $v=128$,

 \begin{gathered}

 \bar\tau=\frac{6143393381125}{9196881302842190592}\approx6.67987\times10^{-7},
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:bb1-weights}
\end{equation}
and $a=\bar\tau^2\approx4.46206\times10^{-13}$. At the secondary pair
$w=1171875/592$, $v=390625/296$, $\hat w\approx1987.45$,

\paragraph{The Kotecký--Preiss condition.} With $a(\gamma)=a|\supp\gamma|$ and
the distance weight $d(\gamma)=\mu\sum_{K\in\gamma}\operatorname{diam}K$, the
per-site bound
$\sum_{\gamma\ni x}|w(\gamma)|e^{a(\gamma)+d(\gamma)}\le\bar\tau^2\le a$
follows from the activity bound, $a|\supp\gamma|=(a/2)\sum_{K\in\gamma}|K|$
(each side of a polymer is a disjoint union), the exploration count and the
mixed norm. Summing over the sites of a polymer, or of a test set of sites,
gives the hypothesis
$\sum_{\gamma'\not\sim\gamma}|w(\gamma')|e^{a(\gamma')+d(\gamma')}\le
a(\gamma)$. The criterion then gives, for the Ursell-weighted cluster sums
$\Phi^T$, the site decay
$\sum_{X\not\sim z,\,X\not\sim s}|\Phi^T(X)|\le a\,v^{-d(z,s)}$, and for
$S\supset Y$ and a polymer $\gamma$ away from $S$ the truncated vacuum
probabilities satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \bigl|\pi_{\Lambda\setminus S}(\gamma)-\pi_\Lambda(\gamma)\bigr|
 \le\sum_{z\in\supp\gamma}\sum_{s\in S}a\,v^{-d(z,s)} ,
 \label{eq:bb1-truncated}
\end{equation}
with no factor $e^{a|\gamma|}$, because both probabilities are real, positive
and at most 1. The checker verifies \eqref{eq:bb1-truncated} exactly on a
six-site chain.

$\mathbb A\times\mathbb A$.
\begin{equation*}
 Z=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac1{n!}\int d\mu(A_1)\ldots\int d\mu(A_n)
 \prod_{1\le i<j\le n}\bigl(1+\zeta(A_i,A_j)\bigr);\ \text{the term $n=0$ is 1.}\tag{1}
\end{equation*}
$\mathcal G_n$ is the set of (unoriented) graphs with $n$ vertices and
$\mathcal C_n\subset\mathcal G_n$ the connected ones.
\begin{equation*}
 \varphi(A_1,\ldots,A_n)=1\ \text{if $n=1$, and}\
 \frac1{n!}\sum_{G\in\mathcal C_n}\prod_{(i,j)\in G}\zeta(A_i,A_j)\ \text{if $n\ge2$}\tag{2}
\end{equation*}
(product over the edges of $G$). A sequence $(A_1,\ldots,A_n)$ is a cluster if
the graph with an edge between $i$ and $j$ whenever $\zeta(A_i,A_j)\ne0$ is
connected.

\textbf{Theorem 1 (Cluster expansion).} Assume that $|1+\zeta(A,A')|\le1$ for
all $A,A'\in\mathbb A$, and that there exists a nonnegative function $a$ on
$\mathbb A$ such that for all $A\in\mathbb A$,
\begin{equation*}
 \int d|\mu|(A')\,|\zeta(A,A')|\,e^{a(A')}\le a(A),\tag{3}
\end{equation*}
and $\int d|\mu|(A)\,e^{a(A)}<\infty$. Then
$Z=\exp\{\sum_{n\ge1}\int d\mu(A_1)\ldots\int d\mu(A_n)\,\varphi(A_1,\ldots,A_n)\}$.
Combined sum and integrals converge absolutely. Furthermore, for all
$A_1\in\mathbb A$,
\begin{equation*}
 1+\sum_{n\ge2}n\int d|\mu|(A_2)\ldots\int d|\mu|(A_n)\,
 |\varphi(A_1,\ldots,A_n)|\le e^{a(A_1)}.\tag{4}
\end{equation*}

Weights (Section 3). $b\ge0$ is a function on $\mathbb A$ and $c\ge0$ a
symmetric function on $\mathbb A\times\mathbb A$ (both may vanish
identically). $\mu_b(A)=\mu(A)e^{b(A)}$;
$\zeta_c(A,A')=\zeta(A,A')e^{c(A,A')}$;
$c(A_1,\ldots,A_n)=\min_{G\in\mathcal C_n}\sum_{(i,j)\in G}c(A_i,A_j)$ if
$n\ge3$; $\varphi_c(A_1,\ldots,A_n)=\varphi(A_1,\ldots,A_n)
e^{c(A_1,\ldots,A_n)}$.

\textbf{Theorem 3 (Decay of correlations).} Assume that
$|1+\zeta_c(A,A')|\le1$ for all $A,A'\in\mathbb A$, and that there exists a
nonnegative function $a$ on $\mathbb A$ such that
\begin{equation*}
 \int d|\mu_b|(A')\,|\zeta_c(A,A')|\,e^{a(A')}\le a(A)\quad
 \text{for all }A\in\mathbb A.\tag{16}
\end{equation*}
Then for all $m\ge1$ and all $A_1,\ldots,A_m\in\mathbb A$,
\begin{align*}
 &\sum_{n\ge m}\frac{n!}{(n-m)!}\int d|\mu_b|(A_{m+1})\ldots\int d|\mu_b|(A_n)\,
 |\varphi_c(A_1,\ldots,A_n)|\\
 &\qquad\le\exp\Bigl\{\frac1{m\gamma}\bigl[(1+\gamma)^m-1\bigr]
 \sum_{i=1}^ma(A_i)\Bigr\}\prod_{1\le i<j\le m}\bigl(1+|\zeta_c(A_i,A_j)|\bigr),
\end{align*}
where
\begin{equation*}
 \gamma=\sup_n\sup_{A_0,\ldots,A_n}\Bigl|\prod_{i=1}^n\bigl(1+\zeta(A_0,A_i)\bigr)-1\Bigr|
 \tag{17}
\end{equation*}
(clearly $0\le\gamma\le2$). The case $m=1$ is
\begin{equation*}
 1+\sum_{n\ge2}n\int d|\mu_b|(A_2)\ldots\int d|\mu_b|(A_n)\,
 |\varphi_c(A_1,\ldots,A_n)|\le e^{a(A_1)},\tag{18}
\end{equation*}
and multiplying (18) by $|\zeta_c(A,A_1)|$ and integrating over $A_1$, from
(16),
\begin{equation*}
 \sum_{n\ge1}\int d|\mu_b|(A_1)\ldots\int d|\mu_b|(A_n)\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^n
 |\zeta_c(A,A_i)|\Bigr)|\varphi_c(A_1,\ldots,A_n)|\le a(A)\quad
 \text{for all }A\in\mathbb A.\tag{19}
\end{equation*}
The paper states that (18) and (19) ``can be proved exactly the same way as
(4) and (5)''.

Section 4.2, lattice polymer models: a polymer is a connected subset of
$\mathbb Z^d$; $\mu$ is the counting measure times a weight $w(A)$; polymers

and $0$ otherwise (the hard-core case, where $|1+\zeta|\le1$ holds).
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Marginal locality from a real-parameter derivative.} Put
$c_\lambda=c+\lambda(c'-c)$ with $\lambda\in[0,1]$ \emph{real}. Every
coefficient of $\psi(c_\lambda)$ is a real polynomial in $\lambda$ and
$Z_\lambda(\Lambda)\ge1$, so $\lambda\mapsto\rho_Y(c_\lambda)$ is smooth, and
no complex $\lambda$ is used. A zero-free region is not needed and none is
proved; the two-qubit vector $\Omega-2z|11\rangle$, whose normalization
$1+4z^2$ vanishes at $z=i/2$, shows why analyticity of the reduced density is
never claimed. The derivative splits into the changes on supports meeting
$Y$, charged by the coefficient Lipschitz bound, and the changes on the other
supports. The latter act through the $Y$-cluster derivatives (the straddling
term) and through the derivatives of the vacuum probabilities (the
normalization term), which \eqref{eq:bb1-truncated} controls. The result is
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\rho_Y(c)-\rho_Y(c')}_1\le2(1+t)^{|Y|-1}\!\!\sum_{I\cap Y\ne\varnothing}\!\!\norm{\delta_I}
 +N_Y\kappa_0\sum_{x\notin Y}\Bigl(\sum_{y\in Y}v^{-d(x,y)}\Bigr)\sum_{I\ni x}\norm{\delta_I},
 \label{eq:bb1-forward-lemma}
\end{equation}
with $\kappa_0=4\bar\tau+2A(t+\bar\tau(S_{v/w}-1))$ and
$A=2\bar\tau^2+a(1+2\bar\tau^2S_{v/w})$; at the headline
$\kappa_0\approx2.67195\times10^{-6}$. In the contract's lemma form this is
$\kappa(I)\le\kappa_0(Y)(w')^{-d_\infty(I,Y)}p(|I|)$ with
$\kappa_0(Y)=N_Y|Y|\kappa_0$, $w'=v$ and $p(s)=s$.

\subsection{Reverse route: the iterated split with per-site charging}
\label{sec:bb1-reverse}
\paragraph{Weights.} The reverse uses only a diameter weight: $W=1024$ at the
headline and $W_2=1/(37888|\tau|)$ at the secondary pair, with the site ratio
$\lambda=2/W=1/512$ and lemma weight $w'=W/2=512$. At the cap $W=1024$ gives
the weighted self-map value $2368/390625<1/64$ and the contraction constant
$39424/390625\approx0.1009$. The lattice sums need $\lambda<q$, so $W=64$ is
rejected. For a comparison put $m_I=\max(\norm{c^A_I},\norm{c^B_I})$, with
per-site sums $t_0\le2T(|\tau|)=49/7199290368$ and
$t_W\le2T(W|\tau|)=49/6321618\approx7.75\times10^{-6}$. The reverse also proves
the mixed-weight lemma for completeness, with the loss $w\,e^{4b}$ and a
fixture showing that $w\,e^b$ is insufficient, but does not use it.

\paragraph{Single-support telescoping.} Order the supports of the union volume
and change them one at a time from $c^A$ to $c^B$. Every intermediate
collection lies in the class $\mathcal C(m)$ of collections with
$\norm{c_I}\le m_I$, and
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\rho_Y(c^A)-\rho_Y(c^B)}_1\le\sum_k\kappa(I_k)\norm{c^A_{I_k}-c^B_{I_k}},\qquad
 \kappa(J)=\sup\frac{\norm{\rho_Y(c)-\rho_Y(c')}_1}{\norm{c_J-c'_J}},
 \label{eq:bb1-telescoping}
\end{equation}
the supremum over pairs in $\mathcal C(m)$ differing only on $J$. By
\eqref{eq:bb1-single}, $\kappa(J)\le2$ for every $J$; that bound is used for
supports meeting $Y$, with $\eta=0$.

\paragraph{The split at the changed support.} For $J$ missing $Y$, apply the
AV1 product-ordering split at $J$: $\psi(c)=\psi_J+\chi(c)$ with
$\psi_J=\Omega_J\otimes\varphi$, $\varphi$ common to $c$ and $c'$, and
$(P_J\otimes1)\chi=0$. Because $J$ is traced out and $\chi$ is excited on
$J$, the cross terms vanish exactly. This gives an exact identity for
$\rho_Y(c)-\rho_Y(c')$ with two channels, and
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\rho_Y(c)-\rho_Y(c')}_1\le2m_J\norm\delta\,\norm{\rho_Y(\varphi)-\rho_Y(c')}_1
 +\frac2{n^2}\bigl\|\Gamma(\delta)-\tr\Gamma(\delta)\,\rho_Y(c')\bigr\|_1,
 \label{eq:bb1-split}
\end{equation}
with $n=\norm\varphi$. The first term is the normalization channel, carrying
the weight $\norm{c_J}^2$ of the $J$-excited component; the second is the
straddling channel. No intermediate normalization is ever divided by, since
orthogonality at $J$ gives $\norm{\psi(c)}^2\ge n^2$.

\paragraph{Only coverings survive.} Expand the creations meeting $J$ into
families. Since $\delta=c_J-c'_J$ is excited at every site of $J$, the
contraction with $\delta$ kills every family that does not cover $J$, and a
covering family contributes with the product of its members' amplitudes. The
spectator families cancel exactly, so no factor $\prod_x(1+s_x)$ over a
region ever appears. The same holds at the next level. Contracting the
outside vector against a covering region $S$ keeps only the coverings of $S$
by supports meeting $S$, which gives a recursion on records
$\Gamma[U,S,g]$ with a strictly growing removed set $U$. The recursion
terminates in every finite volume. Its depth is not bounded uniformly, and no
constant depends on it. Both identities are verified exactly on a five-site
fixture with two excited levels per site.

\paragraph{Per-site charging and the closure.} With the site potential
$h(x)=\sum_{y\in Y}\lambda^{d(x,y)}$ and $H(I)=\sum_{x\in I}h(x)$, three
per-site facts carry the estimate:
\begin{itemize}
\item a support through $x$ meeting $Y$ has diameter at least $d(x,Y)$, so
 $\sum_{I\ni x,\,I\cap Y\ne\varnothing}m_I\le t_Wh(x)$;
\item $\sum_{I\ni x}m_IH(I)\le8t_Wh(x)$, from
 $|I|\le(\operatorname{diam}I+1)^3\le8\cdot2^{\operatorname{diam}I}$, so the
 size of a support is charged once, at the site where the chain enters it,
 never as a product of growing region sizes;
\item the coverings of a region $T$ cost at most $t_0$ in total.
\end{itemize}
The per-level factor is therefore $8t_W=196/3160809\approx6.2009\times10^{-5}$
at every depth. Writing $r=\max_{I\cap Y=\varnothing}\kappa(I)/H(I)$, the
chain bound gives $\kappa(J)\le(c_1+c_2r)H(J)$, hence
\begin{equation}
 r\le\beta^*=\frac{c_1}{1-c_2},\qquad
 c_1=4t_0t_W+\frac{4t_W+4t_0t_W/(1-t_0)}{1-8t_W},\qquad
 c_2=16t_0t_W+\frac{16t_0t_W}{(1-t_0)(1-8t_W)},
 \label{eq:bb1-closure}
\end{equation}
with $c_1\approx3.10066\times10^{-5}$ and $c_2\approx1.69\times10^{-12}$ at
the headline, so that
\[
 \beta^*=\frac{8027378053355287230305861131}{258892194754590065811608028259796}
 \approx3.10066\times10^{-5}.
\] In the contract's lemma form,
$\eta=0$, $\kappa(I)\le\beta^*H(I)\le\kappa_0(w')^{-d_\infty(I,Y)}|I|$ with
$\kappa_0=\beta^*|Y|$, $w'=512$ and $p(n)=n$. The lemma is checked end to end
on a seven-site qubit chain with 23 supports, and each of its 18 single far
changes stays within $\beta^*H(J)\norm{\delta_J}$.

\subsection{Readings, defects and review}
\label{sec:bb1-readings}
\paragraph{Contract readings.} The review reconciles the contract's wording
with both packets as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{D1.} The semantics of \recid{normalization_couples_supports} say
 that a support strictly containing $R$ has a nonzero first-order
 $R$-marginal. It does not. At first order only supports contained in $R$
 move $\rho_R$, namely the ten faces with owner set exactly $R$. Every
 straddling support has zero first-order $R$-marginal, including the 6 of
 the 72 straddling faces that strictly contain $R$, and differs from zero
 only at second order. All three packets exhibit the three kinds, and all
 three found the defect.
\item \emph{R10, non-centred F2 volumes.} The AY1 itemization H1--H5 of the
 AM2 premises is gated for F2 on $\Lambda_N$. Both producers apply it to every

 compression), because no item uses the cube shape. The skeptic checked this
 against the AY1 report and accepts it as a local reading. It is not a gated
 statement, and it affects the F2 branch of c5.

 outside $\Lambda_N$ use the exponent $(N-\norm u_\infty)_+=0$. The lemma
 constant $\kappa_0$ carries the factor $|Y|$. The reverse split weight is

\item \emph{D2, the excerpt rule.} Plan rule P4 was not carried into the BB1

\item \emph{D3.} The reverse exports comparison names truncated to 80

\paragraph{Meaning.} BB1 is a static statement about the reduced densities of
the finite-box ground vectors of the named constructions, one comparison at a
time, at a rate in $N$ per coarse $\ell^\infty$ step at fixed spacing. It
forms no limit object. Whole-sequence convergence, a common limit and
translation statements are BB2's subject, and BB2's contract uses BB1's
frozen targets as hypotheses. BB1 asserts no dynamics, no uniqueness of any
ground state, nothing about boundary conditions outside F1 and F2, no
analyticity of the reduced density in the coupling, and no estimate in the
lattice spacing.
