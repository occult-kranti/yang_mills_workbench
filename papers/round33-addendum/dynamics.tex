\section{BA2: boundary comparison of the Heisenberg dynamics on compact windows}
\label{sec:ba2}

\textbf{Contribution \projecttag{HNM-C-BA2}.} The BA2 contract, recorded under
the title ``Hruday boundary comparison of the Heisenberg dynamics of the
named constructions on compact windows'', was frozen with BA1 before
production as investigation~2 of~8. It takes up the AY2 obligations O5 and O6
(Table~\ref{tab:obligations}), whose route Round32 had named but not written:
a Duhamel and Lieb--Robinson comparison over the extra F2 faces with the AQ1
Nachtergaele--Sims constants
(\repo{research/round33/advisor/selection-ba2.md}{selection note}). The model
is that of BA1; the observables are $A\in B(\mathcal H_R)$ on the cover; the
clock is $\theta=\alpha t/\hbar$ with the window $|\theta|\le8$; the metric is
the coarse $\ell^1$ metric of AQ1~\S3; the decay function is the polynomial
$F(r)=(1+r)^{-4}$, tier \recid{polynomial_lieb_robinson}. An exponential
instance was allowed as a labelled extra without a target and was not
executed.

Both directions are paired. The forward producer runs Duhamel's formula over
the extra faces with the F1 evolution inside the integral and the whole-star
constants (route \recid{duhamel_inner_f1}). The reverse producer, under
premise isolation, puts the F2 evolution inside and uses the owner-set
constants (route \recid{duhamel_inner_f2}), and compares each family with its
own Nachtergaele--Sims limit for the Cauchy estimates. Loop~2 of the
deliberation replaced an earlier reverse route: the triangle inequality
through the two limits gives only a bound of order $1/N$ and cannot meet the
comparison target, so it may appear only as a labelled weaker bound. Both
producers received \texttt{AGENTS.md}, the contract and 27 shared premises,
among them the committed Nachtergaele--Sims excerpt, the AQ1 report, gate and
review, the Round29 source dictionary and primary bindings, and the AY1 and
AY2 records.

\subsection{The Lieb--Robinson theorem as the committed excerpt gives it}
\label{sec:ba2-ns}
Plan rule P4 requires an external theorem to be quoted from a committed source
excerpt, not from a report that cites it. The AQ1 report places the model in
the theorem but does not state the inequality. The advisor therefore committed
\repo{research/round33/sources/nachtergaele-sims-1410.8174v1.md}{an excerpt}
of B.~Nachtergaele and R.~Sims, ``On the dynamics of lattice systems with
unbounded on-site terms in the Hamiltonian'',
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.8174v1}{arXiv:1410.8174v1}, Section~3 (pages
7--8) and Section~4 (page~11). The excerpt binds the PDF (203467 bytes,
14 pages) by the sha256
\begin{center}\small
\texttt{501af040512f2bdb62344ecbd093664e79ad1861338eabbce2c9791fca4e2fba},
\end{center}
identical to the Round29 primary binding \recid{ns-2014}. Its Part~A transcribes the displayed statements and was
checked by the advisor against the rendered page images; its Part~B is the
verbatim \texttt{pdftotext} extraction, in which norm bars and superscripts
are lost; where they differ, the rendered page is authoritative. The excerpt
file itself has sha256 \texttt{6a28f4cd\ldots c6921}, pinned in the forward
checker. The theorem is quoted below as Part~A gives it, set in mathematical
notation. In the quotation $\tau^\Lambda_t$ is the paper's symbol for the
dynamics and $t$ its time; neither is the coupling $\tau$ or the physical time
of this edition.

\begin{quote}\small
Setting (Section 3). $\Gamma$ is a countable set with a metric $d$. If
$\Gamma$ is infinite, $F:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ is non-increasing and
\begin{align*}
 &\norm{F}=\sup_{x\in\Gamma}\sum_{y\in\Gamma}F(d(x,y))<\infty
 \quad\text{(uniform integrability);}\tag{40}\\
 &C=\sup_{x,y\in\Gamma}\sum_{z\in\Gamma}\frac{F(d(x,z))F(d(z,y))}{F(d(x,y))}<\infty
 \quad\text{(convolution condition).}\tag{41}
\end{align*}
To each $x\in\Gamma$ a separable complex Hilbert space $\mathcal H_x$ is
associated; for finite $\Lambda\subset\Gamma$,
$\mathcal H_\Lambda=\bigotimes_{x\in\Lambda}\mathcal H_x$ and
$\mathcal A_\Lambda=\mathcal B(\mathcal H_\Lambda)$. For each $x$ there is a
self-adjoint operator $H_x$ with dense domain $D_x\subset\mathcal H_x$; the
bounded interaction $\Phi$ maps finite $X\subset\Gamma$ to
$\Phi(X)^*=\Phi(X)\in\mathcal A_X$, and
\begin{equation*}
 H_\Lambda=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}H_x+\sum_{X\subset\Lambda}\Phi(X),\tag{44}
\end{equation*}
essentially self-adjoint on the dense domain
$D_\Lambda=\operatorname{span}\{\bigotimes_{x\in\Lambda}\psi_x\mid\psi_x\in D_x\}$
(45); $\tau^\Lambda_t(A)=e^{itH_\Lambda}Ae^{-itH_\Lambda}$ for
$A\in\mathcal A_\Lambda$ (46).
\begin{align*}
 &\norm{\Phi}=\sup_{x,y\in\Gamma}\frac{1}{F(d(x,y))}
 \sum_{X\subset\Gamma:\,x,y\in X}\norm{\Phi(X)}<\infty
 \ \text{defines the space }\mathcal B_F(\Gamma).\tag{48}\\
 &S_\Lambda(X)=\{Z\subset\Lambda:Z\cap X\ne\varnothing\text{ and }
 Z\cap(\Lambda\setminus X)\ne\varnothing\},\tag{49}\\
 &\qquad\text{the surface of $X$ in $\Lambda$, and } S(X)=S_\Gamma(X).\\
 &\partial_\Phi X=\{x\in X:\exists Z\in S(X)\text{ with }x\in Z\text{ and }
 \Phi(Z)\ne0\},\ \text{the $\Phi$-boundary of $X$.}\tag{50}
\end{align*}
\textbf{Theorem 3.1.} Let $\Gamma$ and $F$ be as indicated above. Fix a
collection of local Hamiltonians $\{H_x\}_{x\in\Gamma}$ and an interaction
$\Phi\in\mathcal B_F(\Gamma)$. Let $X,Y\subset\Gamma$ be finite disjoint sets.
For any finite $\Lambda\subset\Gamma$ with $X\cup Y\subset\Lambda$ and any
$A\in\mathcal A_X$ and $B\in\mathcal A_Y$, the bound
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{[\tau^\Lambda_t(A),B]}\le\frac{2\norm{A}\norm{B}}{C}
 \bigl(e^{2\norm{\Phi}C|t|}-1\bigr)D(X,Y)\tag{51}
\end{equation*}
holds for all $t\in\mathbb R$, where the quantity $D(X,Y)$ is given by
\begin{equation*}
 D(X,Y)=\min\Bigl\{\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{y\in\partial_\Phi Y}F(d(x,y)),\
 \sum_{x\in\partial_\Phi X}\sum_{y\in Y}F(d(x,y))\Bigr\}.\tag{52}
\end{equation*}
\textbf{Theorem 4.1.} Let $\Gamma$ and $F$ be as described in Section 3. Fix a
collection of on-site Hamiltonians $\{H_x\}_{x\in\Gamma}$ and an interaction
$\Phi\in\mathcal B_F(\Gamma)$. For each $t\in\mathbb R$ and
$A\in\mathcal A_\Gamma^{\rm loc}$, the norm limit
\begin{equation*}
 \tau_t(A)=\lim_{\Lambda\to\Gamma}\tau^\Lambda_t(A)\tag{77}
\end{equation*}
exists and the convergence is uniform for $t$ in compact sets. The limit may
be taken along any increasing sequence of finite sets $\Lambda$ which tend to
$\Gamma$, and the result is independent of the particular sequence. This
limiting dynamics $\tau_t(\cdot)$ can be uniquely extended to a one-parameter
group of ${}^*$-automorphisms on $\mathcal A_\Gamma$.
\end{quote}

\subsection{The boundary source and the padding}
\label{sec:ba2-source}
On the same box the two padded and unpadded generators differ by
\begin{equation}
 \widehat H^{F2,{\rm pad}}_N-\widehat H^{F1}_N\otimes1
 =\sum_{f\in E_N}\Bigl(-\frac\tau3\Bigr)W_f
 +\sum_{x\in B_+\setminus\Lambda_N}h_x,\qquad |E_N|=28N(5N+1),
 \label{eq:ba2-source}
\end{equation}
read on $\mathcal H_{\Lambda_N}\otimes\mathcal H_{\rm pad}$.

\paragraph{The extra faces.} A face $(b,K)$, with $D_K$ the set of
directions in $K$, lies in F2 but not in F1 on $\Lambda_N$ exactly when
$b\in\Lambda_N$, $b_j<N$ for every $j\in D_K$, and $b_i=N$ for some $i\notin
D_K$. Counting by anchor, the $(2N)^2$ anchors on each of the three planes
with exactly one coordinate $N$ carry $17$, $13$ and $5$ faces, the $2N$
anchors on each of the three edges carry $10$, $3$ and $1$, and the corner
carries none: $4N^2(17+13+5)+2N(10+3+1)=140N^2+28N=28N(5N+1)$. Counting by
class instead, each of the 14 one-direction classes contributes $2N(4N+1)$
and each of the 7 two-direction classes $4N^2$, with $28N(11N+2)$ face--owner
incidences. The enumerations give 616, 1344 and 2352 extra faces at
$N=2,3,4$, and the class identities are checked up to $N=199$. An owner
$y=b+k$ of an extra face keeps $y_i=b_i=N$, so every owner lies on the outer
layer, at $\ell^1$ distance at least $N-1$ from $e_z$ and at least $N$ from
$0$; both minima are attained at the owner $(0,0,N)$, and eleven faces reach
distance $N-1$. No extra face meets $R$, so both families keep the same 82
faces meeting $R$. Each face is one pair (anchor, class), charged once with
norm $|\tau|/3$.

\paragraph{The padding factors out.} The padding term acts on its own tensor
factor and commutes with $\widehat H^{F2}_N$, with every face and with
$B(\mathcal H_R)$. The closure of a sum of self-adjoint operators on separate
factors generates the product of their groups, so
\begin{equation}
 T^{F2,{\rm pad},N}_\theta(A\otimes1)=T^{F2,N}_\theta(A)\otimes1
 \qquad(A\in B(\mathcal H_{\Lambda_N})),
 \label{eq:ba2-padding}
\end{equation}
and the Duhamel source on $\mathcal H_{\Lambda_N}$ is the bounded sum over
$E_N$ alone. The padding has $3(2N+1)^2$ sites (75 at $N=2$, 147 at $N=3$).
Charging the padding terms as a source would give a norm at least the cutoff
$L$ while their exact contribution is zero; both checkers reject it, and both
verify \eqref{eq:ba2-padding} on exact Gaussian-rational fixtures.

\paragraph{Fine units.} A coarse step is $(4a,2a,a)$. The link tails of the
extra faces are at least $N-1$ fine steps from those of $R$ along $z$, the
plaquette corners come within $N-2$ fine steps of the 36 endpoints of $R$
(they touch at $N=2$), and no link is shared. Only link sets matter for
commutation, and no constant uses the fine metric.

\subsection{Duhamel's formula with a bounded difference}
\label{sec:ba2-duhamel}
Let $H_1=H_{\rm loc}+V_1$ and $H_2=H_{\rm loc}+V_2$ on one finite box, with
$V_1,V_2$ bounded and self-adjoint, so $D(H_1)=D(H_2)=D(H_{\rm loc})$. Write
$\tau^{(j)}_u(B)=e^{iuH_j}Be^{-iuH_j}$. Then, for bounded $B$ and real $u$,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \tau^{(2)}_u(B)-\tau^{(1)}_u(B)=i\int_0^u\tau^{(2)}_s\bigl([V_2-V_1,\tau^{(1)}_{u-s}(B)]\bigr)\,ds,\\
 \norm{\tau^{(2)}_u(B)-\tau^{(1)}_u(B)}\le\int_0^{|u|}\norm{[V_2-V_1,\tau^{(1)}_r(B)]}\,dr .
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:ba2-duhamel}
\end{equation}
The cocycle $W(s)=e^{isH_2}e^{-isH_1}$ satisfies
$W(s)\psi-\psi=i\int_0^se^{irH_2}(V_2-V_1)e^{-irH_1}\psi\,dr$ on the common
domain and then on every vector, so it is strongly differentiable; the
product rule on vectors gives the identity. Only vector derivatives of bounded
families are used, never a norm derivative in time. The evolution inside the
commutator, $\tau^{(1)}$, is the \emph{inner} family, and its
Nachtergaele--Sims constants are the ones that enter. Wherever on-site terms
of a larger box or the padding appear, they are placed in the inner
Hamiltonian, where they factor out; the unbounded terms never enter a source.
For each face the bound (51), with $X=R$, $Y=M_f$ and the first entry of the
minimum in (52), gives
\begin{equation}
 \norm{[W_f,\tau^{(1)}_r(A)]}\le\frac{2\norm A}{C}\bigl(e^{v|r|}-1\bigr)
 \sum_{x\in R}\sum_{y\in M_f}F(d(x,y)),\qquad v=2\norm{\Phi_{\rm inner}}_FC,
 \label{eq:ba2-perface}
\end{equation}
since $\partial_\Phi M_f\subset M_f$ and $R\cap M_f=\varnothing$.

\paragraph{Why the first order vanishes.} With the on-site evolution alone,
$e^{irH_{\rm loc}}Ae^{-irH_{\rm loc}}$ stays in $B(\mathcal H_R)$, and no
extra face acts on a link owned in $R$, so the first-order Dyson term of the
difference vanishes identically. In the bound one factor $|\tau|$ is the face
coefficient and the other is the Lieb--Robinson growth $e^{vr}-1=O(|\tau|r)$.
Every constant below is therefore quadratic in $\tau$; a linear label, as
from the trivial commutator bound, is a rejected control.

\subsection{The comparison of the two families}
\label{sec:ba2-comparison}
\paragraph{Forward route (inner F1, whole stars).} Apply
\eqref{eq:ba2-duhamel} on $\Lambda_N$ with inner $\widehat H^{F1}_N$ and outer
$\widehat H^{F2}_N$, add \eqref{eq:ba2-perface} linearly over the extra faces
and integrate over $|u|\le U$:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 b(N)=\frac{|\tau|}{3}\,\Lambda(U)\,S_b(N),\qquad

 \label{eq:ba2-forward}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Within-family Cauchy estimates}
\label{sec:ba2-cauchy}
Let $E'_N$ be the set of faces whose anchor star is not inside $\Lambda_N$.
Every point of such a star has $\norm{y}_\infty\ge N$, so every owner of such
a face lies at $\ell^1$ distance at least $N$ from $0$ and at least $N-1$
from $e_z$, and no such face meets $R$. For every $M>N$ the differences
$F1(\Lambda_M)\setminus F1(\Lambda_N)$,
$F2(\Lambda_M)\setminus F1(\Lambda_N)$ and
$F1(\Lambda_M)\setminus F2(\Lambda_N)$ lie in $E'_N$. With at most 49 faces
through a site, $4r^2+2\le4(1+r)^2$ and $\sum_{k>m}k^{-2}\le1/m$, the tail
over all such faces is
\begin{equation}
 S_c(N)=\sum_{f\in E'_N}\sum_{x\in R}\sum_{y\in M_f}F(d(x,y))
 \le49\Bigl(\frac4N+\frac4{N-1}\Bigr)\le\frac{392}{N-1},
 \label{eq:ba2-tail}
\end{equation}
uniformly in $M$. Each Duhamel pair lives on $\Lambda_M$ (or its padding),
with the on-site terms of the larger box in the inner Hamiltonian, where they
factor out.

\paragraph{Forward.} For F1 the inner Hamiltonian is
$\sum_{x\in\Lambda_M}h_x$ plus the F1 interaction of $\Lambda_N$, the native
restriction of the truncated interaction $\Phi_N$, with
$\norm{\Phi_N}_F\le\norm\Phi_F$. The source is the new stars, split into their
faces, each charged once. For F2 the forward producer goes through F1: one
Duhamel pair between F1 on $\Lambda_N$ and padded F2 on $\Lambda_M$, plus the
comparison at $N$, using $\max_{N\ge2}(5N+1)(N-1)N^{-3}=11/8$. This gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \sup_{M>N}\norm{T^{F,M}_\theta(A)-T^{F,N}_\theta(A)}\le\frac{K_F}{N-1}\norm A,\\
 K_{\rm F1}=\frac{592704\,\tau^2}{1-338688|\tau|}=\frac{9261}{155720800000000},\qquad
 K_{\rm F2}=K_{\rm F1}+\tfrac{11}{8}K_{\rm cmp}=\frac{117747}{1245766400000000},
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:ba2-cauchy-fwd}
\end{equation}
that is, $K_{\rm F1}\approx5.94718\times10^{-11}$ (margin $4.204$) and
$K_{\rm F2}=941976\,\tau^2/(1-338688|\tau|)\approx9.45177\times10^{-11}$
(margin $2.645$). Since $F2(\Lambda_N)\subset F1(\Lambda_M)$ for $M>N$, a
direct F1-inner pair also gives
$\norm{T^{F2,N}_\theta(A)-T_\theta(A)}\le K_{\rm F1}/(N-1)\,\norm A$.

\paragraph{Reverse.} Each family is compared with itself, with the larger box
$\Lambda_M$ inside the integral, so each uses its own interaction: $\Phi$ with
whole stars for F1 and $\Phi'$ with faces charged once for F2. Exchanging
sums bounds the source by $J_F(4/N+4/(N-1))\le8J_F/(N-1)$, with
$J_{\rm F1}=28|\tau|$ and $J_{\rm F2}=49|\tau|/3$, uniformly in $M$:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 c_1&=1016064\,\tau^2E_{\rm up}(1016064|\tau|)
 =\frac{12128856933354903}{118967041015625000000000000},\\
 c_2&=345744\,\tau^2E_{\rm up}(592704|\tau|)
 =\frac{1055961408185869563}{30481402343750000000000000000},
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:ba2-cauchy-rev}
\end{equation}
that is, $c_1\approx1.01951\times10^{-10}$ (margin $2.452$) and
$c_2\approx3.46428\times10^{-11}$ (margin $7.217$). Charging whole anchor
groups instead of faces would recharge old faces: from $\Lambda_2$ to
$\Lambda_3$ it would count 616 old faces again, while the owner-set indexing
charges only the 3920 new ones.

\paragraph{The rate is $1/N$, and it is honest.} With the polynomial $F$ the
tail over $\{\norm y_\infty\ge N\}$ is of order $1/N$. Exact lower witnesses
show that no faster rate follows from these constants: the reverse checker
proves $m\,S(m)\ge1/2$ for the half-shell sums $S(m)$, $m=2,\ldots,256$, and
the forward checker a slab lower bound that contradicts any tail $C_0q^N$
with $C_0=1$, $q=1/2$ from $N=9$ on. The comparison decays faster, like
$N^{-2}$, only because its source is one surface layer. A bound for $N$ to
$N+1$ alone is not a Cauchy estimate, since increments of order $1/N$ sum
like the divergent harmonic series; every estimate above holds for all $M>N$
at once. No exponential decay in $N$ is claimed. Every rate is a rate in $N$
at fixed spacing, per coarse step $(4a,2a,a)$, and nothing is uniform in the
lattice spacing.

\subsection{Whole-sequence convergence and the F2 limit dynamics}
\label{sec:ba2-limit}
Because the Cauchy bounds hold for all $M>N$, both whole sequences
$(T^{F1,N}_\theta(A))_N$ and $(T^{F2,N}_\theta(A))_N$ converge in norm,
uniformly for $|\theta|\le8$. For F1 the limit is AQ1's $T_\theta$
(Theorem~4.1 for $\Phi$). For F2, Theorem~4.1 applied to $\Phi'$ gives a limit
dynamics of its own, and the comparison identifies it with $T_\theta$:
$\norm{T^{F2,N}_\theta(A)-T^{F1,N}_\theta(A)}\le b(N)\to0$. Letting
$M\to\infty$ in the Cauchy bounds,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{T_\theta(A)-T^{F1,N}_\theta(A)}\le\frac{K_{\rm F1}}{N-1}\norm A,\qquad
 \norm{T^{F2,N}_\theta(A)-T_\theta(A)}\le\frac{\min(K_{\rm F1},c_2)}{N-1}\norm A,
 \label{eq:ba2-limit}
\end{equation}
where the second minimum is the gate's ``min(K\_F1, second-route K\_F2)''. For
a general local observable $A\in B(\mathcal H_X)$, $X\subset\Lambda_n$, the
same Duhamel sum with $R$ replaced by $X$ and distance $N-n$ gives, for
example on the forward route,
$\norm{T^{F2,N}_u(A)-T_u(A)}\le(|\tau|/3)\Lambda(U)\cdot49|X|\cdot
4/(N-n)\cdot\norm A\to0$ on every compact window $|u|\le U$; this is
qualitative, and the frozen targets concern $|\theta|\le8$ only. The two
automorphism groups therefore agree on the local algebra and, being
isometric, on the quasi-local algebra: the F2 limit dynamics is the AQ1 limit
dynamics. This closes obligation O5 within the stated scope.

\paragraph{AQ1~\S\S4--5 for the F2 limit states (O6).} AY1 admits for F2 its
own local trace-norm compactness and diagonal extraction. Let $\omega^{(2)}$
be a subsequential limit along F2's own subsequence $N_k$, the local
trace-norm limit of the simple F2 box ground states. Both producers rerun
AQ1~\S\S4--5 for every such state, each one separately.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Stationarity.} Each box ground state is invariant under its own
 evolution. For local $A$ and fixed $n$, $T^{F2,n}_u(A)$ is local, so
 local trace-norm convergence passes $k\to\infty$ and the Cauchy bound
 controls the remainder: $|\omega^{(2)}(T_\theta(A))-\omega^{(2)}(A)|\le
 2c_2/(L-1)$ for every $L$ (reverse), or $\le2\varepsilon_n$ with
 $\varepsilon_n=\sup_{|u|\le1}\norm{T_u(A)-T^{F2,n}_u(A)}$ (forward). Hence
 $\omega^{(2)}\circ T_\theta=\omega^{(2)}$, then on the quasi-local algebra
 by density and for every $\theta$ by the group property.
\item \emph{GNS unitaries and strong continuity.} Stationarity makes
 $U_\theta\pi(A)\Omega=\pi(T_\theta(A))\Omega$ a unitary group with
 $U_\theta\Omega=\Omega$. The map $\theta\mapsto\omega^{(2)}(A^*T^{F2,n}_\theta
 (A))$ is continuous through the normal density on $\Lambda_n$, and the
 uniform approximation passes continuity to $\omega^{(2)}(A^*T_\theta(A))$,
 so $\norm{(U_\theta-1)\pi(A)\Omega}^2\to0$. Density of local cyclic vectors
 and Stone's theorem give a self-adjoint generator.
\item \emph{Nonnegative generator.} For local $A$ the finite correlation
 $\omega_N(A^*T^{F2,N}_\theta(A))$ is the Fourier transform of a positive
 measure on $[0,\infty)$, because the F2 box operator minus its ground energy
 is nonnegative (simple ground state and gap at least $1/2$ in normalized
 units, AY1 via AM2). These correlations converge for every $\theta$ and are
 bounded by $\norm A^2$, so nonnegative smooth tests supported in
 $(-\infty,0)$ pass their zero integrals to the limit spectral measure; the
 negative spectral projection annihilates every local cyclic vector and
 vanishes. Stationarity alone would not suffice: for $H=\mathrm{diag}(0,1)$
 the invariant excited vector has a correlation of negative frequency.
\item \emph{Physical sector.} Every retained F2 term is an on-site Casimir or a
 Wilson loop and the box ground states are simple, so $\omega^{(2)}$ is gauge
 invariant, $T_\theta$ preserves the gauge-invariant algebra, and the
 physical cyclic space reduces the unitary group and its generator.
\end{enumerate}
Only strong continuity of the GNS group is claimed; norm continuity in time
on all of $B(\mathcal H)$ fails, as AQ1's shift fixture shows (a difference
of norm 2). This closes obligation O6 within the stated scope. The F2 limit
states remain subsequential, and nothing identifies them with each other or
with F1 limit states.

\subsection{Meaning}
\label{sec:ba2-meaning}
BA2 concerns the algebraic Heisenberg dynamics of the named constructions: the
finite-box evolutions of bounded observables on the cover, in operator norm,
on the compact window $|\theta|\le8$, at a rate in $N$ at fixed spacing, and
their common norm limit. It does not assert that the GNS dynamics of
different states agree, or that correlation functions of different states
agree; that needs a proved common state, which is BB2's subject. Exact
fixtures in both checkers show one automorphism group with two invariant
vector states whose $\sigma_x$ correlations differ, $(3\pm4i)/5$ in the
forward and $\pm i$ in the reverse fixture. The bounds grow like
$U^2/(1-vU/3)$ with the window, exactly like $(2/(Cv))(e^{vU}-1-vU)$, and
nothing is claimed uniformly in time. The rates are $N^{-2}$ for the
comparison and $1/N$ for the Cauchy estimates, with the polynomial $F$; no
exponential decay in $N$ and no estimate in the lattice spacing $a$ is
claimed.
