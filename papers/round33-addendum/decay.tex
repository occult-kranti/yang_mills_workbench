\section{BA1: boundary decay of the AM2 creation coefficients}
\label{sec:ba1}

\textbf{Contribution \projecttag{HNM-C-BA1}.} The BA1 contract, recorded under
the title ``Hruday boundary decay of the AM2 creation coefficients for the
named constructions'', was frozen before production on 24 September 2026 as
investigation~1 of~8. It asks how far a change of the box boundary can move
the AM2 creation coefficients on supports meeting the cover $R$, for the two
named families F1 and F2 of Section~\ref{sec:families}. Two routes were
assigned
(\repo{research/round33/advisor/selection-ba1.md}{selection note}). The
forward producer uses a diameter-weighted anchored norm that pays a fixed loss
once per interaction term, together with a difference weight that grows with
distance from the boundary source (route \recid{weighted_norm}). The reverse
producer, under premise isolation, continues the AM2 fixed point to complex
couplings, counts the order at which a distant term can enter, and applies
Cauchy's estimate in maximum-modulus form (route \recid{analytic_disc}). The
routes share the AM2 majorant and differ in the decay mechanism. Both
producers received the same 27 inputs: \texttt{AGENTS.md}, the contract and
25 shared premises, among them the AM2, AQ1, AV1, AW1, AY1 and AY2 records and
the I1 dictionary.

The contract froze two (rate, constant) pairs. The headline pair is $q=1/64$
with target $K\le1/2000000$ at the \recid{exact_first_order} tier, where
$q=1/64$ is a chosen ratio realized by the weight $w=64$ or the disc radius
$64|\tau|$, not the ball radius $R_a$ used as a per-step ratio. The floor
pair is the per-step ball ratio $q_{\min}=37888|\tau|=148/390625$ at the cap,
with target $K\le1/12$. The \recid{crude_majorant} tier at $q=1/64$ is
reported, not targeted. The scaling brackets are $[95,105]$ for the headline
constant, $[99/100,101/100]$ for the floor constant and exactly 100 for
$q_{\min}$. The itemized error terms are \recid{weighted_contraction_loss},
\recid{boundary_source_terms}, \recid{order_versus_distance_count},
\recid{exact_first_order_remainder}, \recid{cutoff_uniformity} and

\subsection{Metric, weights and the diameter convention}
\label{sec:ba1-metric}
Distances are coarse $\ell^\infty$ distances on factor sites,
$d(p,p')=\max_i|p_i-p'_i|$, and $\operatorname{diam}I$ is the $\ell^\infty$
diameter of a support. Since
$S-S\subset\{0,\pm e_i,\pm(e_i-e_j)\}$, every whole star has $\ell^\infty$
diameter exactly 1 and $\ell^1$ diameter exactly 2, every face and every
clipped F2 group has $\ell^\infty$ diameter at most 1, and each site lies in
at most four interaction terms. Both producers verified these values by
enumeration on $\Lambda_2$, $\Lambda_3$ and $\Lambda_4$ (64, 216 and 512
stars). The forward norms are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \norm{c}_w=\max_u\sum_{I\ni u}w^{\operatorname{diam}I}\norm{c_I},\qquad
 1\le w\le w_{\max}=\frac{390625}{148},\\
 \norm{\delta}_{B,\beta}=\max_u\sum_{I\ni u}e^{\beta\rho_B(I)}\norm{\delta_I},\qquad
 \rho_B(I)=\max_{p\in I}d(p,B),
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:ba1-norms}
\end{equation}
with $1\le e^\beta\le w$. Here $B$ is the source set of a comparison
(Section~\ref{sec:ba1-sources}). The difference weight grows with distance
from $B$, that is towards $R$. The headline uses $w=e^\beta=64$ and the floor
$w=e^\beta=w_{\max}$. The loss $w^{d_X}=w$ is charged once per interaction
term and never per creation. The reverse route replaces the weight by a disc
of complex couplings of radius $\rho$, with $q=|\tau|/\rho$: $\rho=64|\tau|$
for the headline and $\rho=\tau_*=1/37888$ for the floor.

The convention matters. In the $\ell^1$ reading each star costs $w^2$, the
self-map needs $w^2\le390625/148$, that is $w\le51.37\ldots$, and the rate
per $\ell^1$ step is at least about $1.9465\times10^{-2}$. The ratio $q=1/64$
therefore does not close in $\ell^1$; the verified $\ell^\infty$ diameter 1
carries the headline rate. The distances that matter below are the same in
both metrics, so the $\ell^1$ reading would give only the slower rate. A
cardinality weight $e^{\mu|I|}$ would pay $e^{4\mu}$ per term and give a rate
of at least $(148/390625)^{1/4}\approx0.1395$; it is labelled and not used.

\subsection{The order-versus-distance lemma}
\label{sec:ba1-order}
Scale the interaction by a coupling $z$. The fixed point is then analytic in
$z$ near zero (Section~\ref{sec:ba1-disc}), and its Taylor coefficients obey
$c_1=L^{(1)}_0$ and
$c_n=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1{k!}\sum_{n_1+\cdots+n_k=n-1}L^{(1)}_k(c_{n_1},\ldots,c_{n_k})$.
Expanding each interaction slot writes $c_n$ as a sum over families of $n$
interaction pieces $Y_1,\ldots,Y_n$ (faces, stars or clipped groups). Two
facts of the AM2 estimate drive the lemma: a creation disjoint from the piece
in the interaction slot commutes with it and kills the word, and the output
support satisfies $\mathcal N\setminus X\subset M\subset\mathcal N\cup X$,
with $\mathcal N=\bigcup_jI_j$. By induction on $n$, a nonzero contribution
to $c_{n,M}$ comes from a family that is connected in its intersection graph
and whose union contains $M$. The diameters then add through the interaction:
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{diam}M\le d_X+\sum_j\operatorname{diam}I_j .
 \label{eq:ba1-subadd}
\end{equation}
The sum cannot be replaced by a maximum. For $X=\{0,e_x\}$,
$I_1=\{-e_x,0\}$ and $I_2=\{e_x,2e_x\}$ the output
$M=\mathcal N\setminus X=\{-e_x,2e_x\}$ is disconnected, with
$\operatorname{diam}M=3=d_X+1+1$, while $d_X+\max_j\operatorname{diam}I_j=2$.
Both producers use this fixture as a damaging control, and the reverse checker
verifies \eqref{eq:ba1-subadd} exhaustively on 3033 configurations.

\paragraph{Lemma (order versus distance).} Let $M$ meet $R$, and let a
contribution to $c_{n,M}$ involve a piece $Y$ at distance $d\ge1$ from $R$,
every piece having diameter at most $\operatorname{diam}$. Then
\begin{equation}
 n\ \ge\ 1+\Bigl\lceil\frac{d}{\operatorname{diam}}\Bigr\rceil\ \ge\
 \Bigl\lceil\frac{d}{\operatorname{diam}}\Bigr\rceil .
 \label{eq:ba1-order}
\end{equation}
\emph{Proof.} A point of $M\cap R$ lies in some piece $Z_0$. Connectivity
gives a chain $Z_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_m=Y$ of distinct pieces with consecutive
members intersecting, and stepping through the intersections gives
$d\le m\operatorname{diam}$. Since $d\ge1$, $Y\ne Z_0$, so the family has at
least $m+1$ pieces. \qed

Two interactions that differ only in terms at distance at least $d$ from $R$
therefore give coefficients on supports meeting $R$ that agree through order
$\lceil d/\operatorname{diam}\rceil$ in the coupling. The contract states the
weaker form $\lceil d/\operatorname{diam}\rceil$; the sharp form, with the
$+1$ because the terminal piece need not be crossed, is proved by both
producers. Every bound constant uses the contract form, for a reason given in
Section~\ref{sec:ba1-constants}. The proof uses the $\ell^\infty$
convention with diameter 1.

The contract asked for the order-versus-distance tables in both conventions
on $\Lambda_2$, $\Lambda_3$ and $\Lambda_4$ (Tables~\ref{tab:ba1-order-inf}
and~\ref{tab:ba1-order-one}). Each cell gives the number of interaction terms
at distance $d$ from $R$ and the least tree order at which such a term can
enter, found by breadth-first search on the enumerated intersection graph.
Every cell is at least the bound $1+\lceil d/\operatorname{diam}\rceil$. In
$\ell^\infty$ the bound is attained for every $d\le N-1$, along the chain of
stars anchored at $(0,0,k)$; the larger values at $d=N$ come from the box
walls. The seven terms at distance 0 are the seven incident anchors.

The exact distances are
\begin{equation}
 d(e_z,\text{shell})=N,\quad d(0,\text{shell})=N+1,\qquad
 d(e_z,\text{outer layer})=N-1,\quad d(0,\text{outer layer})=N,
 \label{eq:ba1-distances}
\end{equation}
where the outer layer of $\Lambda_N$ is $\{\max_i|p_i|=N\}$. A shell point
has $|p_i|=N+1$ for some $i$; the only way to come within $N$ of $e_z$ is
$p_z=N+1$, attained at $(0,0,N+1)$. The same argument with $N$ in place of
$N+1$ gives the layer distances, attained at $(0,0,N)$. The two producers
read the nested source set differently, and both readings give the same bound.
The forward producer takes the shell, at distance $N$ from $e_z$, and charges
the source loss $w$ once, since every new term meets the shell. The reverse
producer uses the distance $N-1$ of the new terms themselves: the new F1 star
$(0,0,N)+S$ contains $(0,0,N)\in\Lambda_N$. The sites met by the new F1 stars
are the shell together with the whole outer layer of $\Lambda_N$, with 98,
218 and 386 outer-layer sites at $N=2,3,4$. The $28N(5N+1)$ new F1 faces
lying inside $\Lambda_N$ are exactly the extra F2 faces
$F2(\Lambda_N)\setminus F1(\Lambda_N)$. Each extra face keeps the coordinate
that equals $N$ at all its sites, so it lies in the positive outer layer
$\{\max_ip_i=N\}$.

The reverse producer proves the distance bound for every comparison at once.
\emph{Common-core lemma}: for $N\ge2$, every omitted face at $\ell^\infty$
distance at most $N-2$ from $R$ is retained by both families on every finite
complete-factor volume containing $\Lambda_N$. Such a face has a point $q$
within $N-2$ of $0$ or of $e_z$, so $q\in\Lambda_{N-1}$; its anchor $b=q-s$,
$s\in S$, has $b_i\in[-N,N-1]$, so $b+S\subset\Lambda_N$. The enumeration
against all faces of F2 on $\Lambda_5$ agrees with it (82, 1044 and 3910 faces
within distance $N-2$ for $N=2,3,4$). Hence every source term of every
comparison lies at distance at least $N-1$ from $R$, and the three named
comparisons attain $N-1$.

The F2 padding carries no interaction. The padded fixed point is therefore
supported in $\Lambda_N$, and coefficients on supports meeting the $3(2N+1)^2$
padding sites vanish; both producers work on $\Lambda_N$ directly. In each
comparison the larger interaction, split into its old and new terms with
faces charged once, has per-site sum at most $28|\tau|$ (the enumerated
maximum is 84 face units of $|\tau|/3$), so the AM2 constants apply to it.
The fixed point does not depend on the decomposition, because each $L_k$ is
linear in $V$.

\subsection{The weighted-norm route}
\label{sec:ba1-weighted}
\paragraph{Weighted multilinear estimate.} For collections $c_1,\ldots,c_k$ in
one box, with interaction terms of at most $p=4$ sites and diameter at most 1,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{L_k(c_1,\ldots,c_k)}_w\le w\,J\,L_k^{\rm num}\prod_j\norm{c_j}_w,\qquad
 L_k^{\rm num}=16\cdot8^k\bigl(1+\tfrac{5k}{4}\bigr),
 \label{eq:ba1-multilinear}
\end{equation}
and $\sum_kL_k^{\rm num}t^k/k!=G(t)$. The proof follows AM2's itemized steps.
Support localization is unchanged. Diameter subadditivity
\eqref{eq:ba1-subadd} gives
$w^{\operatorname{diam}M}\le w^{d_X}\prod_jw^{\operatorname{diam}I_j}$ for
$w\ge1$. The root in $X$ contributes $16\cdot8^kwJ$, and the root in a
creation $I_l$ contributes $16\cdot8^k(5k/4)wJ$, the denominator $|I_l|$
cancelling against $\sum_{X\cap I_l\ne\varnothing}w^{d_X}\norm{V_X}\le
wJ|I_l|$. The loss $w^{d_X}\le w$ enters once per interaction term,
independent of $k$ and of the number of creations. In the mixed form one slot
holds a difference measured in $\norm{\cdot}_{B,\beta}$, and the one-point
quantity $\rho_B$ satisfies
$\rho_B(M)\le\rho_B(I_{j^*})+d_X+\sum_{j\ne j^*}\operatorname{diam}I_j$; the
loss is then $e^\beta$. For a source term $X$ meeting $B$ the loss is
$e^{\beta\rho_B(X)}\le e^\beta$.

\paragraph{Self-map, contraction and identification.} Summing
\eqref{eq:ba1-multilinear} over $k$ and telescoping the $k$-linear terms give,
for $\norm{c}_w,\norm{c'}_w\le R_a$,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\Phi(c)}_w\le J\,w\,G(\norm{c}_w),\qquad
 \norm{\Phi(c)-\Phi(c')}_w\le\Gamma\norm{c-c'}_w,\quad \Gamma=J\,w\,G'(R_a),
 \label{eq:ba1-contraction}
\end{equation}
re-evaluated as exact rationals at the declared weights. The self-map
$J_0wG(R_a)\le R_a$ holds exactly when $w\le w_{\max}=390625/148$. At $w=64$
the self-map value is $148/390625$ and $\Gamma=2464/390625\approx
6.30784\times10^{-3}$. At $w_{\max}$ the rational self-map bound equals $R_a$
exactly, the true $G(R_a)\le20.9632\ldots$ making it strict, and
$\Gamma=77/296\approx0.260135$. For $w\ge1$ the weighted ball lies inside AM2's
anchored ball, so Banach's theorem and AM2's uniqueness in its ball identify
the weighted fixed point with AM2's: $\norm{c}_w\le R_a$ for every
$w\in[1,w_{\max}]$, both families, every box, every $Q_L$ and both signs.

\paragraph{Tiers.} Every omitted owner set has $\ell^\infty$ diameter exactly
1, so $\norm{c^{(1)}}_w=w\norm{c^{(1)}}_a\le wt_1$. Convexity of $G$ gives
$\norm{c-c^{(1)}}_w\le Jw(G(t_w)-16)\le\Gamma t_w$, hence the
\recid{exact_first_order} tier
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 t_w:=\norm{c}_w\le T_w=\frac{w\,t_1}{1-J\,w\,G'(R_a)},\\
 T_{64}=\frac{49}{223580736}\approx2.19160\times10^{-7},\qquad
 T_{w_{\max}}=\frac{49}{4036608}\approx1.21389\times10^{-5},
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:ba1-Tw}
\end{equation}
the contract's self-consistent inequality re-derived in the weighted norm. The
\recid{crude_majorant} tier is $t_w\le JwG(R_a)$: $148/390625$ at $w=64$ and
$1/64$ at $w_{\max}$. An exact first-order source combined with the crude
$t_w$ would be labelled crude, and the checker rejects every mislabel.

\paragraph{Difference theorem.} Fix a comparison, a cutoff $L$, a sign and
$|\tau|\le10^{-8}$. Let $c$ be the smaller box's fixed point, zero-extended by
$\iota$, $c'$ the larger box's, and $\delta=c'-\iota c$. Then, for every
$u\in R$ and $1\le e^\beta\le w\le w_{\max}$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{I\ni u}\norm{c'_I-c_I}\le e^{-\beta d(u,B)}\,\frac{S_B}{1-\Gamma_\beta},
 \qquad \Gamma_\beta=e^\beta J\,G'(R_a),\qquad
 S_B\le e^\beta t_1+\Gamma_\beta T_w .
 \label{eq:ba1-difference}
\end{equation}
\emph{Proof.} The map is local: $\Phi_{\Lambda'}(\iota c)=\iota\Phi_\Lambda(c)
+\Psi_{\rm new}(\iota c)$, where $\Psi_{\rm new}$ is the AM2 series with $V$
replaced by the new terms, because
$\mathrm{ad}^k_{\iota C}(\iota V_\Lambda)\Omega$ factors as box~1's value
tensored with the outside vacuum. Hence
$\delta=[\Phi_{\Lambda'}(c')-\Phi_{\Lambda'}(\iota c)]+\Psi_{\rm new}(\iota c)$.
The mixed estimate contracts the bracket by $\Gamma_\beta$. The source's
first-order part is exactly $-(\tau/72)\sum_{\text{new }f}W_f\Omega_0$, with at
most 49 new faces per site and $\rho_B(M_f)\le1$, so it costs at most
$e^\beta t_1$; the remainder costs at most $\Gamma_\beta T_w$ by the source
form. The volume is finite, so the difference is absorbed, and
$\rho_B(I)\ge d(u,B)$ for $I\ni u$ extracts the decay. \qed

The decay comes only from the weight $e^{-\beta d(u,B)}$; the Lipschitz factor
enters only as $1/(1-\Gamma)$. At $e^\beta=w$ the source bound is
$S_B\le T_w$, so the constant multiplying $q^{d(e_z,B)}$, $q=1/w$, is
\begin{equation}

\subsection{The analytic-disc route}
\label{sec:ba1-disc}
\paragraph{Complexified map.} Write the interaction at unit coupling as
$V_1=\sum_XV_{X,1}$, $\norm{V_{X,1}}\le7$, and set $V(z)=zV_1$. Then
\begin{equation}
 F_z(c)=\sum_{k=0}^{8}\frac{L^{(z)}_k(c,\ldots,c)}{k!},\qquad
 L^{(z)}_k=z\,L^{(1)}_k,\qquad
 \norm{L^{(z)}_k(c_1,\ldots,c_k)}_a\le28|z|\,16\cdot8^k\bigl(1+\tfrac{5k}{4}\bigr)\prod_j\norm{c_j}_a .
 \label{eq:ba1-complex}
\end{equation}
The reverse producer re-derives AM2's estimate step by step and records where
$z$ enters: only through $\norm{V_X(z)}=|z|\norm{V_{X,1}}$. No step uses
self-adjointness of $V$ or reality of the coupling, and the coefficient
equation does not involve the ground energy, so nothing else is complexified.
For $|z|\le\rho$ this gives the self-map bound $28\rho G(R_a)$ and the
Lipschitz constant $\kappa(\rho)=28\rho G'(R_a)$. The self-map holds up to
$\tau_*=R_a/(28\cdot148/7)=1/37888$. At the headline radius
$\rho=64|\tau|=1/1562500$ the values are below $148/390625$ and
$2464/390625$; at $\rho=\tau_*$ they are at most $1/64$ and $77/296$. A
radius $2\tau_*$, and the radius $4\tau_*$ that an outgoing-only count $7|z|$
would allow, are rejected controls.

\paragraph{Analyticity.} For $\rho\le\tau_*$, every finite complete-factor
volume of F1 or F2 and every $L$, $F_z$ has exactly one fixed point $c(z)$ in
the ball. The map $z\mapsto c(z)$ is continuous on $|z|\le\rho$ and
holomorphic on $|z|<\rho$, and at real $z=\tau$ it is the AM2 fixed point.
The iterates $c^{(m+1)}=F_z(c^{(m)})$, $c^{(0)}=0$, are polynomials in $z$
that converge uniformly on the closed disc at rate $\kappa^mR_a$; Weierstrass's
theorem gives holomorphy, contraction gives uniqueness in the ball, and AM2's
uniqueness identifies the real value. On the circle, with
$t(w)=\norm{c(w)}_a$ and $t_1(\rho)=49\rho/144$,
\begin{equation}
 t(w)\le T(\rho)=\frac{t_1(\rho)}{1-28\rho\,G'(R_a)}\ \
 \text{(exact first order)},\qquad
 t(w)\le28\rho\,G(R_a)\ \ \text{(crude majorant)},
 \label{eq:ba1-circle}
\end{equation}
and with $J=28|\tau|$ and $w=\rho/|\tau|$, $T(\rho)$ is exactly the weighted
$T_w$ of \eqref{eq:ba1-Tw}.

\paragraph{Cauchy's estimate.} Let $\Lambda^A,\Lambda^B$ be finite
complete-factor volumes containing $\Lambda_N$, with prescriptions in
$\{F1,F2\}$, and $0<|\tau|<\rho\le\tau_*$. Then, for $u\in R$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{I\ni u}\norm{c^A_I(\tau)-c^B_I(\tau)}\le2\,t(\rho)
 \Bigl(\frac{|\tau|}{\rho}\Bigr)^{n_0}\le2\,t(\rho)
 \Bigl(\frac{|\tau|}{\rho}\Bigr)^{N-1},\qquad n_0\ge N .
 \label{eq:ba1-schwarz}
\end{equation}
\emph{Proof.} Embed both volumes in their union with unperturbed on-site
factors on the complement; the fixed points keep their supports and values.
The difference $g(z)$ is holomorphic on the open disc and continuous on the
closed one. Terms of the Taylor recursion whose faces are all common to both
volumes coincide, and every other term contains a source face at distance at
least $N-1$ (common-core lemma), so by \eqref{eq:ba1-order} $g_n=0$ for
$n<n_0$ with $n_0\ge N$. The maximum principle applied to $g(z)/z^{n_0}$
through norming functionals, with $r\uparrow\rho$, gives the factor
$(|\tau|/\rho)^{n_0}$, and the circle bound is $t^A+t^B\le2t(\rho)$. \qed

No Lipschitz constant enters as a decay factor, and no source norm is charged:
the source enters only through its distance, as the vanishing order. The
constants are $K=2T(\rho)$ at the exact tier and $K=2\cdot28\rho G(R_a)$ at the
crude tier. The general comparison is made directly through the union.
Telescoping at exponent $N-1$ would give $889/1006113312\approx8.836\times
10^{-7}$ and a two-step union $49/55895184\approx8.766\times10^{-7}$, both
above the headline target. The headline ratio $q=1/64$ equals $R_a$
numerically only because the frozen disc radius is $64|\tau|$.

\paragraph{What is not complexified.} Only the creation coefficients are
continued in $z$. The reduced density needs the normalization, the continued
$\norm{\psi}^2$, and that can vanish inside the disc: on two qubits
$\psi(z)=\Omega-2z|11\rangle$ has the entire coefficient $2z$, while its
normalization $1+4z^2$ vanishes at $z=i/2$. No zero-free region is proved, and
extending analyticity to the reduced density is a rejected control.

\subsection{Coefficient decay is not decay of the reduced density}
\label{sec:ba1-notdensity}
The ground vector $\psi=e^{-C}\Omega_0$ depends on every coefficient, and its
$R$-marginal couples all supports through the normalization and through the
straddling contractions of AV1. A bound on coefficients meeting $R$ does not
by itself control $\rho_{N,R}$; that step is BB1. Both producers exhibit the
exact fixture (AV1 type, a finite model): qubit sites $r,o,o'$ with $R=\{r\}$
and creations $c_{\{r,o\}}=1/3$ (straddling, the only coefficient meeting $R$),
$c_{\{o\}}=b$ and a decoupled $c_{\{o'\}}$. Changing $b$ from $1/2$ to $0$ leaves every
coefficient meeting $R$ unchanged, yet
\begin{equation}
 \rho_r=\frac1{49}\begin{pmatrix}45&6\\6&4\end{pmatrix}
 \ \longrightarrow\
 \begin{pmatrix}9/10&0\\0&1/10\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \norm{\Delta\rho_r}_1^2=\frac{3681}{60025}.
 \label{eq:ba1-fixture}
\end{equation}
The coefficient Cauchy property is a whole-sequence statement about
coefficients in each cutoff space, obtained from the bound itself and not
from compactness. The gate fields keep \recid{whole_sequence_claimed},
\recid{common_limit_claimed} and \recid{state_decay_claimed} false, because
they concern states; \recid{rate_in_N_claimed} and
\recid{coefficient_cauchy_claimed} are true, the latter with the scope ``AM2
creation coefficients of F1 and F2 on supports meeting R, in each on-site
cutoff space''.

\subsection{Uniformity in the cutoff, traps and error ledger}
\label{sec:ba1-ledger}
Every estimate is dimension-free and holds in each $Q_L$ with constants
independent of $L$. The compressed interactions keep their supports and do
not increase $J$. The compression $Q_Lc^{(1)}$ keeps or annihilates each face
vector: $W_f\Omega_0$ lies in a product of on-site eigenspaces with site
energies at most 18, so every face vector is kept for $L\ge24$, and for
smaller $L$ the first-order counts only decrease. No cutoff removal is applied
to coefficients.

The contract required exact fixtures for three traps; each is a damaging
control in both checkers, and all are finite models that transfer to nothing.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{A weight growing from $R$ fails.} On a chain with a local
 averaging map and a unit source at the far end, the weight growing towards
 $R$ certifies decay (for example $|\Delta_0|\le1/6$ against the exact
 $1/195$ in the reverse fixture), while the reversed weight gives $128/3$, above
 the unweighted bound 2; a non-submultiplicative weight $2^{r^2}$ and a
 min-distance weight are also rejected.
\item \emph{A global Lipschitz bound does not decay.} The nonlocal map
 $(A\delta)_i=\tfrac12\delta_n$ has Lipschitz constant $1/2$ and fixed-point
 difference $\delta_0=1$ for every $n$; the mean-field map on six sites keeps
 $c_0=1/6$ for every position of the source.
\item \emph{A per-creation loss charge fails.} A first-order coefficient on a
 two-site support has diameter $1=d_X$ with no creation, so a loss charged
 per creation misses it. In the forward qubit fixture ($V=\sigma^x\otimes
 \sigma^x$ on $X=R$, $L_0=\tfrac12|11\rangle$) the weighted norm 32 at $w=64$
 exceeds the per-creation bound 16, while the per-interaction bound is 1024.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Controls, replays and review}
\label{sec:ba1-evidence}
Each producer implements all 37 contract controls as damaging mutations that
must raise explicit exceptions. The forward checker runs 51 exact checks with
96 rejected mutations and the reverse checker 61 exact checks with 132. Both
checkers verify the contract hash before any evaluation, read their targets
and brackets from the contract, pin every admitted gate they parse and record
their own hash before the full evaluation. All four producer replays, forward
and reverse under normal and optimized Python, reproduce the frozen outputs
byte for byte, and each closure (31 files) verifies file by file. Both input
inventories equal \texttt{AGENTS.md}, the contract and the 25 shared premises;
the reverse inventory holds no Round33 skeptic, lens, forward, deliberation,
plan or brief file.

The skeptic's pre-comparison package, 101 exact checks, was committed before
either producer's commit. Its values equal the reverse headline, floor and
crude constants exactly. The forward values differ from its values only
because the forward evaluates $G'$ at $R_a$ rather than at $T_w$ and
telescopes the general comparison (factor $64/63$); both are valid upper
bounds, and the forward F1-versus-F2 value equals an exact variant the skeptic
had listed. The post-comparison review adds 20 exact checks, 81 source-edit
runs on temporary copies (two unmutated baselines, 74 validator weakenings,
one per control per producer, and five input edits, every one aborting as
expected) and 10 damaged packets rejected by the review's own value
validator. The producers wrote their final files after the skeptic's commit,
so isolation rests on disclosure and content, not on timing. Both producers
disclose protocol and code-style reads outside their inputs; none carries
premise weight. The review found no blocking issue; its non-blocking findings
are the thin margin, the two readings of the nested source set, the reverse
report's ``positive outer layer'' where the literal set is the full outer
layer, the forward union wording, and the Lipschitz wording of the contract.
