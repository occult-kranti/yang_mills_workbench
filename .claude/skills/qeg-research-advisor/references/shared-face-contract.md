# Shared-face extensions and physical scaling

For a graph extension, derive the incidence matrix before importing a surface formula. Two adjacent cubes have an eleven-face full action and a ten-face outer-boundary action. The latter is a sphere; the former has four links with incidence3. Keep the shared face exactly once and name the action change when its coefficient is set to zero.

The special three-disk reduction gives two disk dimension factors and a three-character invariant multiplicity. Derive these factors from the actual graph. General networks can need higher intertwiner multiplicities and signs; a nonnegative formula on this complex does not prove positivity of general dual weights.

Use center parity to eliminate forbidden supports, then a separate Haar calculation for amplitudes. Even parity is not an amplitude formula. A repeated shared-face insertion is a useful new contraction test. When a comparison action omits a coupling, retain the same observable; its expectation need not vanish under the changed measure.

Character conventions must be explicit. For ordinary SU(2) characters and normalized Haar, integral chi_n*chi_m=delta_nm. Test f=chi_1 before importing any coefficient dimension factor from a source. Derive observable insertions before identifying couplings; a total-degree bookkeeping parameter must not accidentally multiply an insertion by an extra parameter.

For H_N=alpha_N K_N, physical gaps scale by alpha_N. A common alpha_min>0 together with a common dimensionless gap lower bound implies a common physical lower bound. The alpha_min condition concerns that inference, not every possible family: a growing dimensionless gap could compensate a shrinking scale. The free cubic-box counterexample has fixed dimensionless gap3 and thus cannot compensate. Keep Euclidean action coefficients, Hamiltonian energy ratios and algorithmic degrees distinct.

Known finite identities can remove a computational dependency while the original local volume-uniform theorem remains open. Preserve that target and name its missing local constants. Enlarging a ground-state trial space still needs a full-operator excited-state lower bound before claiming a gap.
